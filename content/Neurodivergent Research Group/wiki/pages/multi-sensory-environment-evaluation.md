# Multi-Sensory Environment Evaluation

## Summary

Multi-sensory environment evaluation is the practice of assessing a place through multiple sensory channels (vision, sound, smell, touch/temperature) rather than a single modality. Research has predominantly focused on individual senses — especially sight — but the human experience of any environment is inherently multi-sensory, and holistic assessment better captures how a place affects behaviour, well-being, and comfort.

## Key claims

- Sensory characteristics of a place affect how people perceive and value indoor spaces, how they choose and experience products, and can reduce stress, enhance well-being, and increase work productivity [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Retail environments perceived as more pleasant increase both time and money spent in the store (Donovan et al., 1994, cited in [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]])
- Multi-sensory stimuli should be considered an essential aspect of the design and planning framework of public spaces (Clements-Croome et al., 2019; Henshaw, 2014, cited in [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]])
- Most research has studied one sense at a time, with sight being dominant; other senses have been largely ignored in academic place research (Degen & Rose, 2012; Henshaw et al., 2015; Low, 2015, cited in [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]])
- Qualitative methods (recordings, interviews, focus groups) have dominated sensory place research; quantitative multi-sensory methods are a minority, limiting cross-site comparison [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- **Soundscape** methods (soundwalks, ISO 12913-1) assess the acoustic environment as perceived by people in a place. Soundwalks have been used to profile cities' sonic character [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- **Smellscape** methods (smellwalks) involve naive participants in situ to measure and describe environmental odours. They have been used in rural planning, built environment design, therapy, and cultural heritage assessment [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Visual elements (lighting, colour, layout, greenery, connection to nature) encourage longer stays and influence product expectations — e.g., reddish/lighter coffee shops were associated with expectations of sweeter coffee (Motoki et al., 2021, cited in [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]])
- Somatosensory cues (softness, smoothness, temperature) affect product evaluation; haptic-kinesthetic sensations of flooring affect retail judgements (Imschloss & Kuehnl, 2017, cited in [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]])
- Research would benefit from easy and standardised tools to measure and map the human multi-sensory experience of places [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
