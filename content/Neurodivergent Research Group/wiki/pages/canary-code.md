# Canary Code

## Summary

The Canary Code is a model developed by Dr. Ludmila Praslova for neurodiversity and intersectional inclusion in workplaces and research teams. It focuses on systemically removing barriers faced by the most marginalized workers through six structural practices, explicitly de-centering "professional appearance" and presenteeism in favour of participation, outcomes, and transparency.

## Key claims

- Developed by [[ludmila-praslova]] to focus on systemically removing barriers faced by marginalized workers; the name references canaries in coal mines — workers who register environmental harm first and most acutely [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
- Six practices for including the most marginalized workers: (a) **participation** — including individuals in the work-design process; (b) **outcomes** — evaluating outcomes rather than "professional appearance," presenteeism, or work style; (c) **flexibility** — removing arbitrary barriers related to time, place, and work style; (d) **organizational justice** — fairness of outcomes, procedures, interpersonal treatment, and information provision; (e) **transparency and clear communication** — reducing ambiguity for those who may not register implicit cues; (f) **valid tools for decision making** — directly assessing skills essential to the role rather than proxies [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
- Applied to academic research collaborations, the six practices translate to: collectively determining a detailed project completion plan; focusing on task completion rather than execution style; allowing flexibility in time, place, and work style; discussing task assignments, methods, and authorship decisions in advance; ensuring all team members receive notes and information; making compensation decisions (grant funding, authorship order) transparently based on performance outcomes rather than implicit interpersonal politics [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
- The Canary Code practices should be paired with [[reflexivity]] — neurotypical team members must critically examine how their standpoints influence both the research and the collaboration, not just adopt structural accommodations [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
- The model is framed as intersectional, applying across all forms of marginalization, not exclusively neurodivergence [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
