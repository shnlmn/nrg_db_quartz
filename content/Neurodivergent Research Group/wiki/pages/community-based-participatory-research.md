# Community Based Participatory Research

## Summary

Community-based participatory research (CBPR) is a collaborative research approach that equitably involves community members, researchers, and other stakeholders in the research process, recognizing the unique strengths that each bring. Its aim is to combine knowledge and action to create positive and lasting social change, particularly to address [[health-disparities]]. CBPR has roots in both Lewin's [[action-research]] (northern tradition) and Freire/Fals Borda's liberation pedagogy (southern tradition).

## Key claims

- CBPR equitably involves all partners in all phases of the research process, from study design to dissemination, distinguishing it from approaches that involve communities only in selected stages [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- Key sources defining CBPR include Israel, Eng, Schulz, & Parker (2013); Wallerstein, Duran, Oetzel, & Minkler (2018); and Wallerstein & Duran (2006) [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- CBPR is listed alongside community-based participatory action research and participatory health research as related variations [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- CBPR is distinct from but related to community-engaged research (CEnR), which represents a broader array of academic-community partnerships not necessarily involving equitable partnership at all phases [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- [[community-advisory-board]]s share similar benefits and limitations with CBPR but typically operate at the "involve" level of the [[participation-continuum]], whereas CBPR aspires to "collaborate" or "empower" levels [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- CBPR comprised only 0.1% of publications in peer-reviewed psychology journals despite compatibility with psychology's ethical principles [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Seven defining principles: (1) community as key unit of identity; (2) addressing race, ethnicity, sexism, and social class with [[cultural-humility]]; (3) ecological, multideterminant perspective; (4) equitable research partnerships; (5) acknowledging community strengths; (6) cyclical and iterative process; (7) creating relevant, sustainable, positive change [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Implementation steps: practicing [[reflexivity]], building community relationships, engaging communities via [[community-advisory-board]]s, cocreating research questions, mutually deciding division of labor, and disseminating together [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR does not compromise internal validity; a review of 60 community health studies confirmed this and found CBPR may improve validity by enhancing recruitment and retention (Viswanathan et al. 2004) [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR closes the research-practice gap: biomedical interventions average 17 years from research to practice, whereas CBPR-generated interventions are created with, for, and in the community [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- [[capacity-building]] is foundational: developing community skills, resources, and competencies ensures sustainability beyond individual research projects [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Trust-building requires "showing up" consistently for community activities beyond formal meetings — authentic, consistent support on the community's terms [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Ethical challenges include codefining ethical frameworks with communities, managing multiple role relationships, protecting privacy/confidentiality with community involvement in dissemination, and resolving institutional–community conflicts of interest [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
