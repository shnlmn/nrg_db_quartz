# Universal Design for Learning

## Summary

Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is a three-part instructional framework recommending that educators vary how they engage and motivate learners, present information, and allow learners to demonstrate knowledge. Derived from [[universal-design-public-space|universal design]] principles and applied originally to school/university settings, it can also guide libraries and museums in how they disseminate information and culture.

## Key claims

- UDL recommends varying three dimensions: (1) engagement and motivation, (2) presentation of information, (3) modes of demonstrating learning (Saunders & Wong 2020). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Addressing potential barriers should combine universal design and UDL to "help librarians create more inclusive learning environments that accommodate student learning differences" (Shea & Derry 2019). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Applying UDL in ALM contexts can reduce stressors caused by anxiety and social interaction differences for neurodivergent users. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]

## Application to ALM institutions

- Multiple modes of presenting information (text, visual, audio, tactile) reduces cognitive and sensory load
- Flexible formats for engagement reduce social navigation demands (e.g., chat reference, pre-visit videos, sensory maps)
- Choice in pacing, method, and communication channel supports diverse processing styles

## Relationship to Universal Design

UDL is the learning-specific application of [[universal-design-public-space|universal design]] principles. Where universal design addresses the physical environment, UDL addresses instructional and informational content delivery. In practice the two are complementary: a well-designed physical space (universal design) paired with flexibly delivered content (UDL) creates a more fully accessible institution.
