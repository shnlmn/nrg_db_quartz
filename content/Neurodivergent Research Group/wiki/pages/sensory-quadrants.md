# Sensory Quadrants

## Summary

The Sensory Profile 2 framework organizes sensory processing patterns into four quadrants defined by two axes: **neurological threshold** (how easily the nervous system notices sensory input) and **behavioral response** (how the person responds behaviorally). This 2×2 structure allows practitioners to move beyond binary "sensory dysfunction/typical" labels and understand the specific mechanism and pattern driving a person's sensory behavior.

## The Four Quadrants

| | **High Neurological Threshold** (input less noticed) | **Low Neurological Threshold** (input easily noticed) |
|---|---|---|
| **Active Behavioral Response** | **Seeking** — notices less, so actively seeks intense sensory input to reach threshold | **Sensitivity** — notices input easily, so may respond with distress or strong reactions to ordinary input |
| **Passive Behavioral Response** | **Registration** — notices less, appears inattentive, misses environmental cues (often confused with ADHD) | **Avoiding** — notices input easily, so avoids or withdraws from sensory experiences (often mistaken for oppositional behavior) |

## Clinical Significance

- **Quadrant-level interpretation is superior to composite scores.** Research in [[sensory-processing-autism|ASD populations]] identified two distinct sensory subtypes (Uniformly Elevated vs. Raised Avoiding and Sensitivity), each with different intervention response patterns. Relying on total scores would obscure these clinically meaningful differences.

- **Differential diagnosis support:** The **Registration** quadrant in [[sensory-processing-adhd|ADHD evaluations]] can distinguish between sensory under-responsivity (which warrants sensory-based OT) and primary attentional deficits (which warrant behavioral or cognitive interventions).

- **Environmental adaptation:** Understanding a child's quadrant pattern guides different accommodations:
  - **Seeking:** provide appropriate intensive sensory input; structured sensory breaks
  - **Sensitivity:** minimize unpredictable or intense sensory input; provide predictability and control
  - **Registration:** increase salience of important cues; use multisensory strategies to capture attention
  - **Avoiding:** respect need for control over sensory experiences; gradually expose to avoided inputs
