# Autism Embodiment

**Summary** — Autism embodiment (De Jaegher, 2013) is the concept that autism, including autistic ways of engaging with the world, cannot be separated from bodily and sensory experience. It recognizes autism as fundamentally an embodied, inseparable part of how autistic people perceive, process, interact, and participate in their environments.

## Key claims

- Autism is an embodied, inseparable dimension of autistic people's lived experience, not a separate cognitive condition layered onto typical embodiment [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Autistic ways of engaging with the world are shaped by internal experiences of sensory, motor, and cognitive processing differences [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Autistic participation in research is shaped by bodily experiences—sensory sensitivities, motor planning, emotional regulation—not merely cognitive factors [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Research design must recognize and respond to the role of sensory, emotional, and cognitive embodiment in shaping autistic participation experiences [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
