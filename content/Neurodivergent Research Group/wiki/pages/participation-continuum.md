# Participation Continuum

## Summary

The participation continuum describes a spectrum of stakeholder engagement in research, ranging from minimal involvement (being informed) to full decision-making power (being empowered). Drawn from the IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation and adapted for research contexts by Vaughn & Jacquez (2020), it provides a framework for understanding and choosing appropriate levels of community participation at each stage of the research process.

## Key claims

- Stakeholder engagement is not a dichotomous distinction but a continuum ranging from academic-driven research to equitable shared decision-making between academic and community partners [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- The IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation defines five levels: *inform* (providing information), *consult* (gathering feedback), *involve* (working with stakeholders throughout), *collaborate* (partnering in decision-making), and *empower* (placing final decision-making in community hands) [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- The NIH describes community-engaged health research as a continuum with increasing involvement, impact, trust, and communication flow, from *outreach* to *shared leadership* where final decision-making is at the community level (CTSA, 2011) [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- Key & colleagues (2019) describe research engagement as ranging from *community informed* to *community driven* [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- The choice of participation level is closely tied to the impact research will have in real-world settings; potential for immediate and sustainable impact rises with increasing participation [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- The same research method can operate at different levels on the continuum depending on implementation: traditional focus groups typically function at "consult," while co-facilitated and collaboratively analyzed focus groups reach "collaborate" [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- [[community-advisory-board]]s tend to operate at the "involve" level; projects with trained community co-researchers exemplify "collaborate"; community-led decision-making throughout represents "empower" [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- In an umbrella review of 21 digital health co-design reviews, only 2 qualified the degree of participation using a framework: Cole et al. used an inform–consult–involve–collaborate–empower scale finding most studies involved only the first three levels; Orlowski et al. found 70% of projects reported predominantly consultative consumer involvement [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- A rapid overview of 23 health co-design reviews found engagement intensity ranged from seeking occasional low-intensity end-user feedback to daily, intensely collaborative power-sharing in end-user-led research; [[patient-and-public-involvement|PPI]] distinguishes consultation, collaboration, and publicly-led levels, while consumer involvement research uses eight categories based on four levels of researcher/end-user control [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
