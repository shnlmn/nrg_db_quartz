# Jessica Dark

**Summary** — Jessica Dark is a neurodivergent scholar and doctoral researcher at Birkbeck, University of London, working at the intersection of neurodiversity, inclusive research methodology, and organizational psychology. Her work centers lived experience of neurodivergent people and develops practical frameworks for inclusive research design and workplace inclusion.

## Key accomplishments and contributions

- Developed the [[neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-model]] (NCTIM), a structured framework for inclusive research design with neurodivergent participant groups [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Conducted doctoral research exploring autistic women's experiences of diagnosis disclosure in the workplace, using NCTIM principles to ensure inclusive design within organizational psychology constraints [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Authored prior work on neuro-cognitive trait interaction (Dark, 2023) and inclusive research methods (Dark, 2024) [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Developed the [[eight-principles-of-neuro-inclusion]], emphasizing autonomy, trust, and epistemic respect in inclusive research (Dark, 2024) [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Builds research on foundations of [[neurodiversity-paradigm]], [[epistemic-justice]], [[epistemic-enablement]], [[relational-inclusion]], and [[autism-embodiment]] [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]

## Academic affiliation

- Birkbeck, University of London (Doctoral student and Graduate Teaching Assistant, School of Business, Economics, and Informatics, 2020–2023)
- Supervised by Professor Almuth McDowall (Birkbeck, University of London) and Professor Harriet Tenenbaum (University of Surrey)

## Key insights and perspectives

- As a self-identified neurodivergent researcher, Dark brings lived experience to her methodological development, ensuring frameworks are grounded in authentic understanding of neurodivergent experiences [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Emphasizes that meaningful inclusion requires moving beyond tokenistic, procedural approaches to relational, embodied, ethically grounded practices [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Advocates for positioning neurodivergent people as authoritative knowers of their own experiences and for sustained commitment to valuing and respecting knowledge within neurodivergent communities [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Recognizes that genuine inclusive design requires aligning theory, method, and community knowledge rather than applying frameworks from outside communities [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
