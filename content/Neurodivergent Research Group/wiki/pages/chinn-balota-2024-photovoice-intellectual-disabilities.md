# Chinn & Balota 2024 — Photovoice Systematic Review with Intellectual Disabilities

## Summary

Systematic review of 31 photovoice studies (2003–2022) with people with [[intellectual-disability|intellectual disabilities]], examining adaptations to the method, collaboration and power dynamics, and impact and outcomes. Followed PRISMA guidelines and used thematic analysis. Published in the *Journal of Applied Research in Intellectual Disabilities*.

## Key claims

- Photovoice research with people with intellectual disabilities has grown rapidly: 5 articles published 2003–2012 versus 14 published 2020–2021 [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Most studies had 10 or fewer participants with intellectual disabilities (mean = 15, median = 9); ages ranged from 13 to 98 [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Three themes identified: adaptations to the photovoice method; collaboration, participation and power; and impact and outcomes [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Common adaptations include: individual interviews replacing or supplementing group discussions; supported photo-taking by carers; digital cameras with colored sticker guides; mini printers for tangible copies; walking interviews with researcher accompaniment [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- With few exceptions, participants were not involved in analytic processes of coding and generating themes — this remains seen as a "step too far" in inclusive research (Nind 2011) [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Participants' photos were reproduced in findings of only a third of published articles; in most studies photos served only as discussion stimuli, not as data requiring analysis [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- The "action" element of PAR was largely absent: most studies produced knowledge for academic audiences without clear trajectory to policy makers or tangible change [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Assertions of participant "empowerment" require interrogation: Wang & Burris (1994) define empowerment as requiring access to knowledge, decisions, networks, and resources — not merely giving participants cameras [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Researcher [[reflexivity]] received little consideration in most reviewed studies despite being a key aspect of qualitative research [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- The role of carers/supporters in photo-taking was often under-theorized; photo-taking is frequently a process of co-creation between people with intellectual disabilities and their support networks [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Benefits for participants included pride, achievement, self-knowledge, enjoyment, increased confidence, enhanced advocacy motivation, and skill development [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Ethical issues beyond informed consent (photo ownership, release permissions, privacy, defamation, emotional aftercare) received limited consideration [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Only Watchman et al. (2020) demonstrated tangible downstream impact: photovoice findings were used to co-design dementia training delivered by participants to peers [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Under-represented groups in reviewed studies: children and young people under 18, older adults, racially minoritized groups, and those with additional physical, mental health, or sensory impairments [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
