# Communication Boards

## Summary

Communication boards are a rapid-access, paper-based form of augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) that use symbols to help autistic and other students communicate without needing electricity or batteries. Symbols (objects, photographs, or line drawings) are selected per student and combine core vocabulary (commonly used words) with fringe vocabulary (context-specific words). Boards can be deployed as a single whole-class board, multiple boards placed around a room, or individualized per student, and are meant to be used alongside other AAC types rather than as a student's sole communication option. They also serve as a low-demand communication channel during moments of dysregulation or crisis, when vocal communication may not be accessible.

## Key claims

- Communication boards are rapid-access/paper-based AAC that need no electricity or batteries and can be used quickly across settings. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]
- Symbols should represent both core vocabulary (commonly used words) and fringe vocabulary (context-specific words), selected based on individual needs and preferences. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]
- Boards can be used as a single larger board for a whole class, as multiple boards placed around a classroom, or as individualized boards for specific students. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]
- Most AAC users need multiple ways to communicate throughout the day, so communication boards should be used in combination with other AAC types, not alone. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]
- Giving all students multiple ways to communicate builds acceptance and participation for the whole class, not just AAC users. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]
- A communication board lets a student express themselves without the demand of vocal communication, which can be harder to access during dysregulation or crisis. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]

## How to use

- **Ensure access** — place boards where all students can reach them throughout the day; start with familiar vocabulary.
- **Model and encourage use** — point to words/phrases on the board while speaking; keep modeling even without immediate response; create repeated practice opportunities across settings and people.
- **Respond and reinforce** — acknowledge board use immediately with meaningful feedback (e.g., saying "It is loud — let's turn it down" while pointing to the matching symbol) so students learn their communication is effective.

## Settings and board variants

The source documents both cross-curricular usage guidance (art, music, English language arts, play/leisure) and several ready-made board topics: School, Home, Playground, Community, and Emotions communication boards, each with vocabulary tailored to that setting. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]

## Assembly and access

- Print multiple copies; laminate or otherwise protect for durability.
- Place boards in multiple locations (communication binder, classroom centers, related-arts rooms, nurse's station).
- The TRIAD Communication Board Library (vkc.vumc.org/vkc/triad/communication-resources) hosts downloadable boards for use with students. [[raw/Communication_Boards.extracted.md]]
