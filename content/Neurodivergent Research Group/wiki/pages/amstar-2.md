# AMSTAR-2

## Summary

A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews, version 2 (AMSTAR-2) is a critical appraisal instrument for evaluating the methodological quality of systematic reviews, including both randomized and non-randomized studies. Developed by Shea et al. (2017), it provides 16 items covering protocol registration, search strategy, study selection, data extraction, risk of bias assessment, and reporting of results.

## Key claims

- AMSTAR-2 was used by Kilfoy et al. to assess 21 reviews of digital health co-design; all were classified as critically low quality, with particularly low scores on description of included study designs [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Reviews scored especially poorly on items related to protocol registration (item 2), justification of excluding studies (item 7), assessment of publication bias (item 15), and reporting of funding sources of included studies (item 10) [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- The universally critically low quality rating across all included reviews highlights systemic methodological weaknesses in the digital health co-design literature [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
