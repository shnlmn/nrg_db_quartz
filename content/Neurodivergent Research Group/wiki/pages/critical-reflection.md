# Critical Reflection

## Summary

In participatory action research, critical reflection is the process by which participants examine social reality to take action for change. Rooted in Freire's concept of praxis, it holds that action and reflection are indissolubly united—reflection without action is "sheer verbalism" and action without reflection is "pure activism."

## Key claims

- [[paulo-freire|Freire's]] epistemology rejects both the view that consciousness copies external reality and the solipsist argument that the world is a creation of consciousness; human consciousness brings a reflection on material reality, whereby critical reflection is already action [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Action and reflection must go together even temporally so that praxis cannot be divided into a prior stage of reflection and a subsequent stage of action; when they coincide they become creative and mutually illuminating [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Through praxis, critical consciousness develops, leading to further action through which people cease to see their situation as inevitable and instead as "an historical reality susceptible of transformation" [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Critical theorists argue that interpretivists' authentic accounts of [[lived-experience]] are not enough; these accounts carry voices of inherited tradition and prevailing culture that must be critically examined [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- The critical edge—radically calling into question the cultures being studied—is central to [[participatory-action-research|PAR]] [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- In PAR's iterative reflective cycle, participants collect and analyse data, determine action, then further research the resultant action in a perpetuating corkscrew of data collection, reflection, and action [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
