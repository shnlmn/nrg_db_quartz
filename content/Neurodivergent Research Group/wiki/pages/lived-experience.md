# Lived Experience

## Summary

The firsthand, subjective knowledge that comes from directly experiencing a phenomenon, as distinct from abstract or scientific knowledge derived from measurement and quantification. In PAR and participatory research, lived experience is treated as a legitimate form of knowledge that can inform practice and challenge dominant epistemologies.

## Key claims

- Experiential learning can lead to a legitimate form of knowledge that influences practice; adult educators in low-income countries drew on this to develop participatory research sympathetic to adult learning [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Husserl argued that the scientific world is an abstraction from the lived world—systematic and well-organised, unlike the uncertain, ambiguous, idiosyncratic world known at first hand [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Phenomenologists argue that humans cannot describe an object in isolation from the conscious being experiencing that object, and experiences cannot be described in isolation from their objects; experiences enable humans to engage with their world and unite subject and object [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- [[participatory-action-research|PAR]] stands in contrast with the mathematisation of the scientific world by Galileo, for whom real properties were only those that could be measured, counted, and quantified [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Critical theorists go beyond interpretivists' confidence in authentic accounts, seeing in these accounts voices of inherited tradition and prevailing culture that must be critically examined [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Feminist epistemology refers to "women's ways of knowing or women's experience" as a use of lived experience in research [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Local knowledge — knowledge rooted in experience in a particular social context — is often devalued by social science perspectives that make claims to generalizability or universality; PAR prioritizes this form of knowledge [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- [[photovoice]] enables participants to document and narrate their lived experience through photography, giving voice to those excluded from traditional research methods [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- For people with [[intellectual-disability|intellectual disabilities]], photovoice allows them to concretize lived experience and translate concepts, feelings, and issues into tangible visual representations, making fewer demands on memory than verbal-only methods; photographs preserve perspectives for reflection and discussion [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Neurodivergent people are experts of their own identities; lived experience leads to novel and creative ideas, provides context for study outcomes (e.g., environmental sensory influences) that outsiders may not consider, and confers knowledge of the most respectful terminology for their communities [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
- Neurodivergent people also produce informal knowledge (blogs, online forums, consulting, manuscript reviewing) that neurotypical researchers often do not access; this expertise often goes unrecognized, constituting an uncompensated form of epistemic labor [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
- However, photographs should not be treated as unmediated representations of lived reality; a critically informed approach recognises the inherently indeterminate nature of visual images and the influence of social norms and artistic conventions on photography practices [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
