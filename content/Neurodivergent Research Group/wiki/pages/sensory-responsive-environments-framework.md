# Sensory Responsive Environments Framework

## Summary

The Sensory Responsive Environments Framework (SREF™) is a theoretical and design framework developed by Finnigan (2024) to support the inclusion of neurodivergent individuals with sensory sensitivities in designed outdoor built environments. SREF provides a non-prescriptive, context-sensitive design approach that centers atypical sensory needs through four key principles: [[design-from-sensorial-margins]], [[sensory-zoning]], nature-based approaches, and [[co-design-in-research|co-design]].

## Key Principles

SREF is structured around four integrated principles:

1. **Design from the Sensorial Margins (DFSM)** — Centering neurodivergent user groups experiencing sensory extremes (both [[hypersensitivity|hypersensitive]] and [[hyposensitivity|hyposensitive]]) to create inclusive spaces that benefit the entire sensory spectrum
2. **Sensory Zoning** — Creating diversified landscape areas that offer varied [[sensory-affordances|sensory affordances]] and implicit adaptability, providing users with multiple sensory experiences and options
3. **Nature-Based Approaches** — Integrating [[biophilic-design|biophilic design]], natural materials, water elements, and living elements that provide sensory support, informed by Attention Restoration Theory and Wilson's Biophilia Hypothesis
4. **Co-Design** — Including neurodiverse community members, occupational therapists, and accessibility experts in site planning and design processes

## Design Outcomes

The framework aims to:
- Identify multi-sensorial barriers in designed outdoor environments
- Support atypical sensory requirements for access and participation
- Accommodate both heightened and diminished sensory sensitivities
- Remain adaptable to evolving research, language, and design trends
- Foster a sense of belonging and recognition for neurodivergent users
