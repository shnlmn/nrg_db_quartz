# Subjective Sensory Sensitivities

## Summary

Subjective sensory sensitivities refer to individual experiences of heightened discomfort, aversion, or distraction in response to sensory stimuli such as loud noises, bright lights, strong smells, or tactile sensations. Unlike [[sensory-processing-dysfunction]] (which focuses on nervous system atypicality), subjective sensitivities emphasize the lived experience and personal meaning of these sensations. They significantly impact daily functioning, relationships, and emotional wellbeing across neurodivergent and neurotypical populations.

## Key Claims

- **Sensory sensitivity is trans-diagnostic and pervasive.** A diagnosis-independent study of 713 individuals found that sensory sensitivity themes were not primarily driven by autistic participants (max 30% contribution to any theme); 42% of participants reported no diagnosis or neurodivergence yet described comparable challenges. This converges with quantitative findings that the nature of sensory triggers is largely comparable across diagnostic groups. [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]

- **Sensory sensitivities limit functional and social participation.** Participants reported constraints on sleep, driving, shopping, public transport use, and other essential activities directly due to sensory challenges or indirectly through physical and emotional exhaustion. Sensitivities are effortful and exhausting, requiring substantial energy to manage. [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]

- **Sensory sensitivities create relational strain.** Individuals reported difficulty managing reactions in social contexts—including emotional volatility, avoidance of public spaces, and challenges in communicating needs to others—leading to miscommunication or perceived abnormality in personal relationships. [[social-impact-sensory-sensitivity]]

- **Exacerbating factors are multidimensional.** Sensory sensitivities are amplified by situational factors (unpredictability, focused tasks, crowds) and internal factors (tiredness, stress, symptom change in anxiety/depression/OCD/migraine). Several factors show bidirectional causal relationships — a "sensory avalanche" cycle. [[sensory-sensitivity-exacerbating-factors]]

- **Coping strategies are diverse and context-dependent.** People employ multiple, often simultaneous strategies: limiting sensory input (avoidance, earplugs), compensatory sensory stimulation (fidget toys, stimming, music), self-regulation (mindfulness, breathing, acceptance), social support, and control/prediction through routine. Negative health behaviors (alcohol, skin picking) were also reported. [[sensory-sensitivity-coping-strategies]]

- **Social context shapes expression and management of sensitivities.** Many individuals suppress their reactions in front of others due to concerns about how they appear or impact others, suggesting that [[double-empathy-problem|bidirectional understanding]] and environmental acceptance affect coping success. [[social-impact-sensory-sensitivity]]

- **Specific locations are spontaneously identified as problematic.** Supermarkets/retail, education/work settings, and events/concerts are the most frequently cited challenging locations. Environmental accommodations (adjustable lighting, sound reduction, recovery spaces, prior information) are recommended, alongside individual [[co-design-in-research|co-design]]. [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
