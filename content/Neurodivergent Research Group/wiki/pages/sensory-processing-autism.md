# Sensory Processing in Autism Spectrum Disorder

## Summary

Sensory processing differences are nearly universal in autism spectrum disorder: **95% of children with ASD demonstrate some degree of sensory processing dysfunction** on standardized assessment, with greatest differences in Underresponsive/Seeks Sensation, Auditory Filtering, and Tactile Sensitivity domains. Sensory reactivity is formally included in DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ASD (Criterion B4), making [[sensory-profile-2|standardized sensory assessment]] directly relevant to diagnostic evaluation and supporting the medical necessity for sensory-based occupational therapy.

## Key Claims

### Research Evidence: High Prevalence and Specificity

- **Landmark study (Tomchek & Dunn, 2007):** Analysis of 281 children with ASD vs. age-matched typically developing peers using the Short Sensory Profile established the prevalence and distinctive pattern of sensory processing differences in autism at scale. This evidence directly influenced diagnostic nosology and is cited in DSM-5 development.

- **Sensory subtypes in ASD:** Research using SSP-2 identified two distinct ASD sensory subtypes:
  - **Uniformly Elevated (67% of ASD children):** show high [[sensory-quadrants|sensory scores across all quadrants]]
  - **Raised Avoiding and Sensitivity (33%):** show elevated scores specifically in avoiding and sensitivity quadrants
  - These subtypes show **different intervention response patterns**, underscoring the clinical value of quadrant-level interpretation rather than relying on composite scores alone.

### DSM-5 Diagnostic Criterion Connection

The DSM-5 (APA, 2013) formalized sensory evidence by including sensory reactivity explicitly in **Criterion B for ASD diagnosis**: "hyper- or hypo-reactivity to sensory input or unusual interest in sensory aspects of the environment" now falls under Criterion B4 (restricted and repetitive behaviors domain). DSM-5-TR (2022) maintained this criterion unchanged.

**Clinical implications:**
1. An [[sensory-profile-2|SP-2 evaluation]] documenting Definite Dysfunction in sensory domains is directly relevant to ASD diagnostic evaluations — not as a diagnostic instrument, but as behavioral documentation of the Criterion B4 phenotype.
2. Sensory-based OT interventions for ASD patients are documentarily supported by the diagnostic criterion itself, establishing medical necessity for payers.
