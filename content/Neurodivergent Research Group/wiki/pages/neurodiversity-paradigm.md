# Neurodiversity Paradigm

## Summary

The neurodiversity paradigm is a political and philosophical position holding that neurological and cognitive diversity—particularly among neurodivergent people, those whose minds differ from societal norms—is something to be accepted and even celebrated. It presents an alternative to both the [[medical-model-of-disability]] and the [[social-model-of-disability]], retaining and rejecting aspects of each.

## Key claims

- Differing neurology is valuable for our species [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- There are no "correct" brains; individuals with different neurotypes should be accepted [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- Differences in neurobiology are subject to social dynamics similarly to race or gender [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- Although often framed around autism, neurodiversity is by no means exclusive to autism; it encompasses ADHD, dyslexia, intellectual disability, stuttering, and other forms of neurodivergence [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- Most proponents oppose "cures" for conditions like autism and ADHD, viewing them as different neurotypes rather than disorders, while remaining open to treating conditions like epilepsy and depression [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- The perspective applies regardless of cognitive ability or support needs, though representation of nonspeaking individuals in the movement can be improved [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- Endorsement of the neurodiversity movement is associated with less autism stigma (Kim & Gillespie-Lynch, 2023) [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- The term "neurodivergent" is preferred over "neuroatypical" by some scholars on accessibility grounds: the two words differ by only one letter, creating a confusion risk for readers with dyslexia [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
- Neurodivergence is defined by Walker (2021) as "having a mind that functions in ways which diverge significantly from the dominant societal standards of 'normal'"; the neurodiversity movement is inclusive of all forms of neurodivergence, not only autism, ADHD, and dyslexia [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al. 2023]]
