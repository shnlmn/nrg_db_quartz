# The Sensorial Prism™

## Summary

The Sensorial Prism™ is a visualization framework developed by Finnigan (2024) that conceptualizes the spectrum of hypo- and hyper-sensitivities across all eight sensory systems in a non-dichotomous manner. Rather than representing sensory experience as a linear spectrum, the prism illustrates that neurodivergent individuals may experience varying degrees of sensitivity across different sensory modalities simultaneously, with both extremes represented as natural human variation.

## Eight Senses in the Prism

The framework incorporates:

1. **Visual** — sight and light perception
2. **Auditory** — sound and hearing
3. **Olfactory** — smell
4. **Gustatory** — taste
5. **Tactile** — touch and texture
6. **Proprioceptive** — body position awareness
7. **Vestibular** — movement and balance
8. **Interoceptive** — internal bodily sensations

## Visual Representation

The Sensorial Prism uses dashed center lines to represent the "average" neurotype sensory baseline, while expanding outward shows the spectrum of hypo- and hyper-sensitivities. This contrasts with previous frameworks treating the "average neurotype" as a fixed reference point, instead acknowledging that the concept of average sensory experience is simplistic.

## Design Application

The Sensorial Prism informs [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework|SREF]] by helping designers visualize:

- The range of sensory experiences neurodivergent users may have
- That [[sensory-stacking]] (multiple simultaneous sensory variations) is typical
- That neither hyper- nor hypo-sensitivity is a pathology
- That environmental design should accommodate the full spectrum
