# Intersectionality

## Summary

Intersectionality describes how multiple social structures (gender, race, class, age, sexual orientation, etc.) intersect and interact to produce differentiated, compounded experiences of privilege and oppression. In spatial analysis, it reveals how the same place is experienced differently by people positioned differently across these axes simultaneously.

## Key claims

- Social structures condition everyday experience of urban space; people differently positioned encounter different limitations and freedoms in their use and access to city spaces [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Studying uneven and unequal city experiences identifies both vulnerable groups and groups that are privileged; reveals shelter and safe spaces for different people [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Intersectional analysis avoids essentialising social categories — power structures and identity categories are conceived as non-rigid and non-stable [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Places focus on material repercussions of inequality rather than fixed identity labels [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Situating analysis in specific places (place-centred approach) avoids universalising experience across contexts [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
