# Collins Et Al 2018 Cbpr Psychology

## Summary

Collins, S.E., Clifasefi, S.L., Stanton, J., Straits, K.J.E., Rodriguez Espinosa, P., Andrasik, M.P., Miller, K.A., Orfaly, V.E., The LEAP Advisory Board, Gil-Kashiwabara, E., Nicasio, A.V., Hawes, S.M., Nelson, L.A., Duran, B.M., & Wallerstein, N. (2018). "Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR): Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research." *American Psychologist*, 73(7), 884–898. This article introduces CBPR to a general psychology audience, defining its principles, differentiating it from traditional researcher-centered practice, retracing its historical roots in Lewin's [[action-research]] and Freire's liberation pedagogy, providing concrete implementation steps, and illustrating CBPR through a Housing First case study with the LEAP Advisory Board.

## Key claims

- CBPR comprised only 0.1% of publications in peer-reviewed psychology journals at time of writing, despite its compatibility with psychology's ethical principles [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR's historical roots span a "northern tradition" (Kurt Lewin's [[action-research]], 1940s) and a "southern tradition" (Orlando Fals Borda's participatory research in South America, Africa, and Asia; [[paulo-freire]]'s liberation pedagogy) [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Seven defining principles of CBPR: (1) community as key unit of identity; (2) addressing race, ethnicity, sexism, and social class with [[cultural-humility]]; (3) ecological, multideterminant perspective; (4) equitable research partnerships; (5) acknowledging and promoting community strengths; (6) cyclical and iterative process; (7) creating relevant, sustainable, and positive change [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Implementation steps include: practicing [[reflexivity]], building and maintaining community relationships, engaging communities via [[community-advisory-board]]s, cocreating research questions, mutually deciding division of labor, and disseminating research together [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR does not compromise internal validity; a review of 60 community health studies confirmed this, and CBPR may improve validity by enhancing recruitment and retention in marginalized populations (Viswanathan et al. 2004) [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR is well-positioned to close the research-practice gap: biomedical interventions take an average of 17 years to move from research to practice, whereas CBPR-generated interventions are created with, for, and in the community they serve [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- [[capacity-building]] is foundational to CBPR: assessment of community strengths and needs followed by developing skills, resources, and competencies ensures sustainability beyond individual research projects [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Ethical challenges specific to CBPR in psychology include: codefining ethical frameworks with communities, managing multiple role relationships, protecting privacy and confidentiality with community involvement in dissemination, and resolving conflicts of interest between institutional and community timelines [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- The LEAP Advisory Board case study demonstrated CBPR with chronically homeless individuals with severe alcohol use disorders in a Housing First program; residents cocreated programming (administrative leadership, meaningful activities, pathways to recovery) and were co-owners of research products [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Trust-building required "showing up" consistently for community activities beyond formal meetings; the key was "authentic and consistent support for communities on their terms" [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
