# Formatting Building Experience Questions

> [!info] Promoted from a query. Verify against source pages before citing.

## Summary

A set of principles for formatting research questions about a user's experience in a building, designed to be accessible to neurodivergent participants. These guidelines synthesize insights from [[inclusive-research-methods]], [[double-empathy-problem]], [[recall-bias]], [[alexithymia]], [[participatory-research]], and newer work on [[environmental-experience]], [[cognitive-load]], and [[neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-model]].

## Key claims

- Use precise, bounded questions rather than vague prompts — e.g., "Was the lighting in the lobby too bright, too dim, or comfortable?" instead of "Tell me about your experience in this building." The [[double-empathy-problem]] means questions that seem clear to neurotypical researchers may be ambiguous for neurodivergent participants [[raw/3 tips for designing user research for neurodivergent testers|AbilityNet]]
- Minimise reliance on recall: [[recall-bias]] is heightened for people with ADHD and other conditions affecting working memory. Capture feedback in real time (e.g., via a messaging channel or in-situ diary while participants are in the space) rather than asking them to remember past visits [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Do not assume participants can label emotions verbally: [[alexithymia]] makes "How did the space make you feel?" unreliable. Offer alternative response formats — descriptions of physical sensations ("Did your shoulders feel tense?"), visual or art-based prompts, or descriptions of mental images [[raw/3 tips for designing user research for neurodivergent testers|AbilityNet]]
- Label Likert-type scales fully with a clear descriptor at every point, not just endpoints, and provide an open-text box for elaboration [[raw/3 tips for designing user research for neurodivergent testers|AbilityNet]]
- Offer flexible response formats: let participants respond with screenshots, annotated photos of the space, bullet points, or long-form text rather than forcing a single format [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Ask about [[communication-preferences]] first — start every research session with "How do you prefer to be communicated with?" Traditional research assumptions (spontaneity, eye contact, emotional reciprocity) create barriers; responsive design should offer written/verbal options, processing time, and acceptance of non-linear storytelling [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]] [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Include neurodivergent researchers in question design: following [[participatory-research]] principles ("Nothing about us, without us"), involve neurodivergent people in drafting and testing the questions to surface ambiguity and assumptions that neurotypical researchers may miss [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- The [[sensory-walk-questionnaire]] offers a structured, quantitative approach to evaluating buildings across four sensory modalities (sound, smell, vision, touch/temperature), using a [[check-all-that-apply|CATA]] lexicon that reduces the cognitive load of free-text generation while still capturing multi-dimensional experience [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Mapping sensory characteristics of a place can serve as a first step to localise environmental problems and identify features to improve, which directly supports post-occupancy evaluation of buildings [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]

## Structured response formats

- [[check-all-that-apply]] (CATA) provides a standalone method with its own open question about optimal list length for neurodivergent respondents; a direct complement to the open-ended formats recommended above [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- [[sensory-quadrants]] (Seeking / Sensitivity / Registration / Avoiding) provides a theoretical basis for why different response formats matter: a participant in the Avoiding quadrant will engage with building-experience questions differently than one in the Seeking quadrant. Questions could be stratified or interpreted through this lens [[raw/land-13-00636-v2.extracted.md|Finnigan 2024]]

## Cognitive and attentional considerations

- [[cognitive-load]] — Reducing cognitive overwhelm can be achieved through video walkthroughs of documents, bullet-pointed sections, and flexible formats, all directly applicable to how building-experience questionnaires are presented [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- [[executive-functioning]] — Questions about building experience impose executive demands (task initiation, organizing responses, time management). Embedding structures like clear timelines, pre-written materials, and structured prompts reduces these barriers [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- [[monotropic-attention]] — Sudden topic shifts within a questionnaire may cause disorientation for monotropic participants. This argues for grouping questions by sensory modality or spatial zone rather than jumping between topics [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]

## Theoretical framing of building experience

- [[environmental-experience]] provides the overarching conceptual framework: building experience operates through sensorimotor, emotion-valuation, and knowledge-meaning systems, and can only be accessed through first-person reports. This is the theoretical foundation for why these questions matter [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md|de la Fuente Suárez 2024]]
- [[sensory-experience-in-architecture]] distinguishes between passive stimulus presence and active felt experience. This distinction is critical for question design: asking "Was the lighting bright?" (stimulus) vs. "Did the lighting feel uncomfortable?" (experience) yield fundamentally different data [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md|de la Fuente Suárez 2024]]
- [[emotional-experience-in-architecture]] — The PAD model (pleasure, arousal, dominance) offers a structured alternative to open "how did you feel?" questions, which are problematic for participants with [[alexithymia]]. The Affect Grid or Davitz emotional checklist could serve as adapted response tools [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md|de la Fuente Suárez 2024]]
- [[place-preference]] provides empirical grounding for what building-experience questions should probe: preference is an integrative outcome of sensory quality, emotional response, and perceived affordances [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md|de la Fuente Suárez 2024]]

## Methodological tools for eliciting building experience

- [[rades]] uses 120 visual images to elicit verbal descriptions of experience, bypassing the need for participants to generate language from scratch. This directly addresses the [[alexithymia]] and [[cognitive-load]] barriers identified above. Its open question about neurodivergent populations makes it a strong candidate for adaptation [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md|de la Fuente Suárez 2024]]
- [[envides]] uses sentence-stem completion ("This place…") to elicit environmental descriptions. The structured but open-ended format may reduce cognitive load while still capturing rich data. Its open question about accessibility for different language-processing profiles is directly relevant [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md|de la Fuente Suárez 2024]]
- [[sensory-survey-design]] provides a decision tree for planning sensory data collection (point vs. transect, single-sense vs. multisensory, temporal variation). Building-experience questions should be informed by when and where in the building participants answer them [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-sensory-methodology|Parker et al. 2024]]

## Inclusive research design framework

- [[neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-model]] — NCTIM's three-stage process (map study demands → identify trait interactions → embed responsive features) provides a systematic method for designing building-experience questionnaires. Rather than ad-hoc accessibility tips, NCTIM offers a framework for applying all recommendations on this page [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- [[psychological-safety-in-research]] — Answering questions about building experience can feel risky if participants worry their sensory responses are "abnormal." Anonymity, informal communication channels, and relationship-building are relevant to how building-experience sessions are facilitated [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- [[subjective-sensory-sensitivities]] — Sensory sensitivities are amplified by situational factors (unpredictability, cumulative load, emotional state). A participant's responses may vary dramatically depending on when in their day or visit they are asked [[raw/land-13-00636-v2.extracted.md|Finnigan 2024]]
- [[sensory-processing-dysfunction]] provides the clinical basis for why standard building-experience instruments may not capture neurodivergent experiences. Multisensory integration difficulties mean questions about individual senses may miss the real experience [[raw/land-13-00636-v2.extracted.md|Finnigan 2024]]
- [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework]] — SREF's principle of [[design-from-sensorial-margins]] (centering users with sensory extremes) could be applied analogously to question design: format questions for participants at the sensory margins, and they will work for everyone [[raw/land-13-00636-v2.extracted.md|Finnigan 2024]]
