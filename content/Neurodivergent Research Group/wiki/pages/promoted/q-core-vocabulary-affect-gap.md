# Core Vocabulary Affect Gap

> [!info] Promoted from a query. Verify against source pages before citing.

## Summary

[[core-vocabulary]]'s Descriptive/Quantity category carries only **good** and **bad** as
affect terms. Every specific affective state — calm, happy, excited, safe, relaxed, annoyed, anxious — collapses into one of those two when a participant is limited to core words. This is the *affect gap*: a core-only [[communication-boards|communication board]] can record valence but not arousal, which makes it unable to distinguish outcomes that sensory-environment research is specifically trying to measure.

## The construction

Sensory-state reports can be built from canonical core words using the causal frame `[stimulus] + make + me + feel + [good|bad]`, because **make**, **me**, and **feel** are all canonical [[core-vocabulary]] entries (make/feel = Core Verbs; me = Pronouns; good/bad = Descriptive/Quantity).

Worked example for *"The color light makes me feel calm"*:

> **"Color light makes me feel good."**

Extensions that push the reading toward *calm* rather than generic positive:

| Utterance | Mechanism | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| "Color light makes me feel good. **I want more.**" | "more" signals wanting continuation, which reads as settled rather than merely not-bad | Preferred — all core, low ambiguity |
| "Color light makes me feel good. **I feel slow.**" | "slow" is the only core word touching low arousal | Risky — reads as sluggish or unwell about as easily as calm; do not use without support |

## Word-list check

Applied per item 1 of [[promoted/q-aac-backup-board-audit-checklist]]:

- **Canonical core:** make/makes, me, feel, good, I, want, more, like, slow
- **Fringe — must be tagged:** color, light
- **No core or fringe equivalent:** calm

Note that *color* and *light* stack into a compound. On a board that is either two symbols the participant must combine, or one dedicated fringe entry. For sensory work a single *colored light* symbol is the lower-demand option.

## Why calm is the costly loss

The gap is symmetric — it was first observed on the negative side (*annoyed* → "bad") and confirmed here on the positive side (*calm* → "good") — but the two losses are not equally expensive.

In sensory-environment work, calm is not a mood report. It is a regulation state, and it is the outcome most of these interventions are built to produce (see [[calming-and-alerting-strategies]], [[sensory-refuge]]). A board that cannot distinguish *this space calmed me* from *this space excited me* cannot measure whether a [[sensory-refuge]] worked — both participants tick "good."

## Recommended remedy

[[zones-of-regulation]] supplies exactly the missing vocabulary as tagged fringe:

- **Green** — happy, calm, focused, ready
- **Yellow** — anxious, excited, frustrated, worried

It is already colour-coded, which suits board use, and it is already in use as an emotion-identification support for participants with [[alexithymia]]. Adding the Zones terms as fringe entries alongside the core board satisfies item 2 (core + fringe combo) of [[promoted/q-aac-backup-board-audit-checklist]] for affect specifically.

## Instrument-wording flag

"The color light" is ambiguous before it reaches a participant:

- **colored light** — a hue wash (e.g. a blue or amber wall wash)
- **the color of the light** — correlated colour temperature (warm/cool white)

These are different design variables and produce different findings. Pick one and state which in the instrument. Compare the lighting items in
[[promoted/q-built-environment-questions-autistic-adults]] (B1), which measure brightness only and do not currently address hue or colour temperature.
