# Sensory Room Architectural Installations

> [!info] Promoted from a query. Verify against source pages before citing.

## Summary

Architectural installation interventions for a sensory room address spatial structure, material surfaces, lighting, and modality-specific sensory affordances. The design goal is a field of small precise spatial punctures — each targeting one sensory modality — rather than a single undifferentiated room.

## Spatial Structure

### Escape Space (ASPECTSS criterion 3)

Small partitioned alcove, crawl space, or ceiling-mounted canopy providing neutral-sensory respite, customisable by the user. [[aspectss-design-index|ASPECTSS]] empirical evidence (Mostafa 2008) supports positive effects in learning environments. Venice Biennale 2023 pilot data showed both autistic and non-autistic visitors reported calm "at almost the same levels" in the EscapeScapes installation — supporting [[design-for-autism-design-for-all]].

### Sensory Zoning within One Room

Divide floor area into at least three zones by sensory demand:

| Zone | Function | Sensory profile |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| High-stimulation corner | Vestibular and proprioceptive kit | Hyposensitive / seeking |
| Transitional area | Moderate seating, moderate affordances | Mid-range threshold |
| Low-stimulation retreat | [[sensory-refuge\|Sensory refuge]], minimal input | Hypersensitive / avoiding |

Use furniture, flooring material, or level change as dividers rather than walls, preserving visual continuity and wayfinding. See [[sensory-zoning]].

### Transition Threshold (ASPECTSS criterion 5)

Mark entry into each zone with a material or lighting shift. This signals sensory recalibration and allows users to anticipate and prepare for a different sensory register. Even a change of flooring texture suffices as a threshold cue.

## Material and Surface Interventions

**Acoustic treatment as texture**
Sound-absorbing panels that simultaneously offer tactile affordance — varied surface hardness, fabric-wrapped baffles, cork, felt, uneven relief. Addresses acoustic and tactile modalities in one installation.

**Natural materials**
Wood grain, stone, bark panels provide textural and thermal variety without artificial input. Contrast with synthetic surfaces to mark zone boundaries. See [[biophilic-design]].

**Flooring as sensory sequence**
Carpet → rubber → wood → smooth tile signals zone transitions through proprioceptive underfoot feedback. Supports [[sensory-zoning]] without architectural partition.

## Light

**Controllable spectrum**
Dimmable warm-white over the refuge zone; brighter, cooler over the active zone. Avoid fluorescent lighting entirely — [[biophilic-design]] literature consistently identifies fluorescent flicker as a leading participant complaint.

**Level change and soffit lighting**
A lowered ceiling or fabric canopy over the refuge zone creates enclosure and reduces visual field. Activates prospect-refuge theory: the enclosed zone reads instinctively as protected. Integrates with [[biophilic-design#Theoretical Foundations|Prospect-Refuge Theory]].

## Sensory Affordances by Modality

Drawn from [[sensory-affordances]]:

| Modality | Installation |
|----------|-------------|
| Vestibular | Hammock chair, platform swing, or rocking element in the seeking zone |
| Proprioceptive | Climbing wall, resistance tunnel, or weighted blanket storage |
| Auditory masking | Water feature or white-noise column in the transition zone (masks leakthrough from active zone; provides soft fascination) |
| Tactile | Varied surface panels at hand height across all zones |
| Olfactory | Low-intensity diffuser in the refuge zone with a scent distinct from the active zone — cues zone identity through smell, supporting zone recognition for users with limited visual processing |

## Conceptual Frame

The [[sensory-decolonisation]] framing from the Venice Biennale 2023 ASPECTSS installation — "senso-spatial acupuncture" — is generative for a sensory room: a field of small precise spatial punctures, each targeting one sensory modality, rather than one room trying to do everything. This frames the design brief as an additive, modular system rather than a fixed program.
