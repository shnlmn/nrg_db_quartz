# Hyposensitivity

## Summary

Hyposensitivity (also termed under-responsiveness) is reduced or diminished responsiveness to sensory stimuli, where individuals require greater sensory input intensity to register or respond to sensations. In neurodivergent populations (autism, ADHD, dyslexia), hyposensitivity frequently manifests as sensory-seeking behaviors and spans all eight sensory modalities.

## Manifestations

Hyposensitive sensory-seeking behaviors in neurodivergent individuals include:

- **Visual** — Seeking bright lights, high-contrast visuals, or visual movement/flicker
- **Auditory** — Seeking loud sounds, music, or auditory stimulation; difficulty hearing quiet sounds
- **Olfactory** — Seeking intense or repeated smell experiences
- **Tactile** — Seeking deep pressure, strong touch sensations, or specific textures
- **Vestibular** — Seeking movement, spinning, swinging, or intense balance challenges
- **Proprioceptive** — Seeking heavy work, resistance activities, or physical pressure
- **Interoceptive** — Reduced awareness of internal bodily cues (hunger, pain, temperature, bathroom needs)

## Environmental Affordances

Outdoor built environments supporting hyposensitive individuals may include:

- Multi-sensory features (swings, slides, playground equipment)
- Movement opportunities ([[sensorimotor-movement]])
- Variable terrain and physical challenges
- Water features providing tactile and vestibular input
- [[sensory-zoning]] areas designated for sensory-seeking activities

## Contrast with Hypersensitivity

While [[hypersensitivity]] involves heightened responsiveness and sensory-avoidant behaviors, hyposensitivity involves reduced responsiveness and sensory-seeking behaviors. Finnigan (2024) found that roughly half of study participants reported lived experiences with hyposensitivities, while the larger majority expressed hypersensitivities, with many reporting both simultaneously ([[sensory-stacking]]).
