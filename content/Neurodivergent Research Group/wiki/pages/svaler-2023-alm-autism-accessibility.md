# Svaler 2023 — ALM Autism Accessibility

## Summary

Svaler (2023) explores how libraries and museums can become more accessible for autistic users through a survey (n=126), literature review, and case study. Finds that sensory overload, social interaction difficulties, and anxiety are the top barriers. Concludes with a range of sensory-friendly and social provisions. Published via Oslo Metropolitan University, Faculty of Social Sciences.

## Key claims

- Over half of autistic survey respondents struggled with sensory overload, social interactions, and anxiety in libraries/museums. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Research on autism and libraries has grown sharply since 2013; ~44% of Web of Science articles stem from 2020–2023. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Most prior research based on parent/faculty/administrator experience, not autistic people themselves (citing Shea & Derry 2019). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Autistic people prefer [[identity-first-language]]; [[functioning-labels]] are harmful and fluctuating ability invalidates them. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Top 5 barriers (survey): social interaction, anxiety, sensory overload, insufficient seating, nowhere to hide. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Even minimal changes (quiet hours, sensory activities) significantly improve museum experience (Freed-Brown 2010). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Libraries can function as a sensory escape for autistic students pursuing special interests (Anderson 2021). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Librarians perceived positively for giving space rather than initiating social interaction (survey participant quote). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]

## Methodology

- Anonymous questionnaire (n=126): 12 diagnosed autistic, 28 self-diagnosed/suspected autistic, 34 unsure, 55 non-autistic; autumn 2021; social-media recruited
- Analysis restricted to diagnosed + self-diagnosed autistic respondents (n=40)
- Case study: five institutions selected via Google search for autism accessibility documentation
- Literature review of ALM accessibility scholarship

## Survey findings

Top barriers (diagnosed + self-diagnosed autistic, n=40):
1. Social interaction
2. Anxiety
3. Sensory overload
4. Not enough seating places
5. Nowhere to hide

Secondary barriers reported by under half: no safe space for meltdowns/shutdowns; uncertainty/confusion; not knowing how/where to ask for help; difficulty navigating physical space; feeling of misplacement/not accepted.

Of 22 optional free-text responses: 8 said needs not met, 6 said needs met.

## Case study institutions

| Institution | Key provision |
|---|---|
| Smithsonian | "Morning at the Museum" (2011) — early entry, sensory activities, dim lights, lower volume; pre-visit videos |
| University of Warwick Library | Sensory study rooms: alternative lighting (bubble tubes, LED strips, galaxy projectors), rocking chairs, wobble cushions, accessible software |
| British Museum | Sensory map; quieter-times info; sensory-support backpacks (children 3–11, free with £10 deposit) |
| Multnomah County Library | Sensory kits; dedicated sensory-friendly hour with natural/limited lighting and limited patron access |
| Pickerington Public Library | Sensory bags (noise-cancelling headphones, fidget tools, verbal cue cards, weighted lap pads); staff trained via KultureCity |

## Recommended accommodations

See [[sensory-friendly-programming]] for a consolidated list. Briefly:
- Quiet/sensory-friendly hours; alternative lighting; sensory maps; sensory kits/bags; sensory study rooms
- Study space maps; guaranteed same study space; clear signage; decompression space
- Chat reference; personal librarians; autism support programme collaboration; campus outreach; special interest groups
- Sensory-inclusion staff training; updated accessibility web pages with pre-visit information; clear institutional rules
