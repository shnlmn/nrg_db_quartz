# Sensory Responsive Design

## Summary

Sensory responsive design is an approach to built environment planning and design that deliberately accommodates diverse sensory processing styles, particularly those of neurodivergent individuals experiencing [[hypersensitivity|hypersensitivities]] and [[hyposensitivity|hyposensitivities]]. The [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework]] (SREF) operationalizes sensory responsive design through [[design-from-sensorial-margins|design from sensorial margins]], [[sensory-zoning]], [[biophilic-design|nature-based approaches]], and [[co-design-in-research|co-design]].

## Design Principles

Sensory responsive design requires:

1. **Multi-sensory consideration** — Addressing all eight sensory systems (visual, auditory, olfactory, gustatory, tactile, proprioceptive, vestibular, interoceptive)
2. **Flexibility and adaptability** — Avoiding prescriptive one-size-fits-all solutions in favor of context-sensitive approaches
3. **User representation** — Centering neurodivergent voices and expertise in design processes
4. **Identification of barriers** — Understanding [[sensory-sensitivity-exacerbating-factors|sensory barriers]] specific to the intended site and users
5. **Solutions targeting extremes** — [[design-from-sensorial-margins|Designing from sensorial margins]] to support full spectrum participation

## Barriers in Current Practice

The study identified significant barriers in outdoor environments:

- **Anthropogenic sensory sources** — Noise pollution, artificial lighting, vehicle traffic
- **Lack of [[sensory-refuge|sensory refuges]]** — Limited spaces for recovery from overstimulation
- **Limited options** — Single-sensory or limited-sensory design approaches
- **Accessibility gaps** — ADA compliance insufficient for sensory needs

## Application to School Playgrounds

[[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. (2025)]] demonstrate sensory responsive design principles in inclusive playground design:

- **Vestibular and proprioceptive affordances** — Swinging, spinning, and climbing features support self-regulation and sustained engagement for neurodivergent children
- **Multi-sensory engagement** — Nature elements, varied textures, sound considerations create rich sensory environments supporting all children
- **Flexible participation modes** — [[prospect-refuge|Prospect-refuge]] design enables observation without forced social engagement, supporting socially anxious children
- **Design with neurodivergent children** — Co-design processes reveal that neurodivergent children are competent contributors identifying essential sensory features absent from standard playground designs

## Benefits of Implementation

Sensory responsive design approaches can:

- Enhance participation and belonging for neurodivergent users
- Support mental health and well-being through [[sensory-sensitivity-coping-strategies|coping support]]
- Enable longer engagement and positive social outcomes when combined with appropriate [[affordances|affordances]]
- Provide universal benefits for all users
- Align with environmental sustainability and biophilic principles
