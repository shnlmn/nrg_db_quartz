# Decolonization in Research

## Summary

A call to recognize and dismantle the destructive legacies of colonialism in research institutions, to re-power indigenous groups, and to construct alternative relationships between peoples and knowledges that liberate knowers and doers from colonial extraction and centralization of power. Decolonization is a foundational intellectual tradition informing [[participatory-action-research|PAR]].

## Key claims

- Decolonization in research requires recognizing and dismantling destructive colonial legacies in societal and research institutions [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- The primer draws on foundational decolonization texts: Linda Tuhiwai Smith's *Decolonizing Methodologies* (rooted in Indigenous thought in Aotearoa/New Zealand), Bagele Chilisa's *Indigenous Research Methodologies* (rooted in African knowledge systems), and Frantz Fanon's *The Wretched of the Earth* [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- PAR is recognized as useful in Indigenous health research with potential to reduce colonising effects of conventional research by avoiding exploitation, ensuring community control, specifying benefits, and preventing misrepresentation [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Decolonization involves constructing alternative relationships between peoples and knowledges, not merely adding marginalized voices to existing frameworks [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
