# Place Preference

## Summary

Place preference refers to an individual's overall positive or negative evaluation of, or liking for, a place. It is influenced by physical qualities, sensory experiences, emotional responses, and the opportunities a place affords for meaningful activity.

## Key claims

- The quantity and diversity of vegetation are essential characteristics related to higher environmental preference [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Places with higher visibility and openness are more restorative and preferred; higher openness in both natural and urban settings relates to lower perceived danger [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Green spaces improve human health through three functions: reducing harm (weather impact), restoring capacities (stress recovery), and building capacities (enabling activities) [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Biophilic design qualities (coherence, complexity, visual contact with nature, biomorphic patterns) support preference and wellbeing; aural, olfactive, and tactile experiences of nature also provide benefits [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- In one empirical study, liking for architecture, liking for appearance, and enjoying exploration and contemplation received the highest preference ratings (8.0/10); spending free time received the lowest (6.0/10) [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Material/texture experiences correlate with appearance preference; vegetation experiences correlate with desire to be in the place [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]

## Relationship to experience dimensions

Place preference appears to be an integrative outcome of emotional experience (pleasure, arousal, dominance), sensory quality assessments, and perceived affordances for desired activities.
