# Social Model of Disability

## Summary

The social model sees disability as caused by one's environment rather than by an individual's impairment. It separates psychological or physical impairment from disability (problems one faces in society), holding that a person is disabled to the extent that the world oppresses them due to a poor fit between their needs and the environment.

## Key claims

- The model has helped shift the onus of disability off individuals and onto society, leading to more rights for disabled people [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- A limitation is that it can ignore disabled people's physical embodiment of their impairments—individuals with chronic pain may feel disabled by both an unaccommodating society and their own bodies [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- The [[neurodiversity-paradigm]] retains aspects of the social model (emphasis on environmental accommodations) while going beyond it [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
