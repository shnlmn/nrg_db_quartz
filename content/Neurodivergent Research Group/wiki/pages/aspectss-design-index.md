# ASPECTSS Design Index

## Summary

The ASPECTSS® Design Index is an autism-specific architectural design framework developed by [[magda-mostafa|Magda Mostafa]] in 2013 at The American University in Cairo (in partnership with Progressive Architects). It provides a structured lens for conceiving autistic space, premised on the value of autistic insight and the view of architectural environments through an autistic perceptual model. Mostafa's authorship is confirmed across multiple applied projects listed in [[raw/PROJECTS]].

## Core Premises

- ~2% of the global population identifies somewhere on the autism spectrum across cultures, ages, socio-economic groups, and geographies [[raw/ASPECTSS_EXHIBITIONS]]
- Autistic individuals engage the world through a perceptual model different from the normative stance dictated by modernist standardisation
- The burden of adapting to sensory challenges currently falls almost entirely on autistic users; designers are largely absolved of responsibility
- ASPECTSS® thinking frames autistic insight as valuable rather than deficit-based

## Key Spatial Constructs

The framework identifies three spatial constructs for autistic sensory experience:

- **Sensory seeking** — spaces affording deliberate engagement with desired sensory input
- **Sensory refuge** — spaces offering escape and recovery from sensory overload (see [[sensory-refuge]])
- **Transition** — spatial sequences mediating between high- and low-stimulation zones (see [[sensory-zoning]])

## Exhibitions

### Venice Biennale 2023 — Palazzo Mora, European Cultural Center
**"A Case for Sensory Decolonisation: Autistic Escape"**

Two-part installation exploring the dichotomy of autistic spatial experience between stimulation and refuge:

- **Sensory Atlas** — wall-mounted multimedia collage of sensory vignettes documenting experiences of sensory overload from global city sensoryscapes, using audio, photography, digital collage, and film
- **EscapeScapes** — interactive spatial installation proposing "landscapes of escape"; visitors step away from a simulated cityscape into a sensory refuge, described as "senso-spatial acupuncture"

The exhibition framed [[sensory-decolonisation]] as the conceptual thesis: autistic escape as spatial protest against the sensory colonisation of cities by capitalistic proliferation.

### Venice Biennale 2021 — Palazzo Bembo
**"Autistic Imaginaries of Architectural Space: the World through the Lens of Autism"**

A collective exhibition curated from encounters between the ASPECTSS® Design Index and autistic individuals worldwide. Central message: "by viewing the world through the autistic lens, we are given a snapshot of the sensory world of our built environment from a position of alternate/innovative and novel perspectives, perspectives that can help inform the production of future worlds that are more broadly inclusive of the voices of neurodiversity."

## Applied Projects

Five documented real-world applications by [[magda-mostafa|Magda Mostafa]] (see full detail on her person page):

- **Dublin City University** (Dublin, Ireland, 2020–2021) — Autism Friendly University Design Guide; DCU designated world's first autism-friendly university (2018)
- **Sharjah City for Humanitarian Services Autism School** (Sharjah, UAE, 2016) — ASPECTSS-guided mixed-use neighborhood school; sensory design masterplan and peer review
- **Munroe-Meyer Institute Center of Excellence** (Omaha, Nebraska, USA, 2018–2019) — Therapy and medical center layering ASPECTSS with sensory-spatial diagnostics, narrative-centric design, and autism-inclusive universal design
- **El Balad Autism Center** (Ramallah, Palestine, 2018) — Education and live-learn facility for autistic Palestinian refugee orphans; vernacular stone construction, UNRWA funded
- **ELS Center for Excellence** (Jupiter, Florida, USA, 2015–2016) — Hybrid retro-fit/new build; ASPECTSS Post-Occupancy Evaluation informed acoustical, wayfinding, and de-escalation interventions; post-POE showed preliminary positive impact on student learning
