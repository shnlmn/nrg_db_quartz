# Photovoice

## Summary

A participatory action research method in which participants use photography to document and reflect on their experiences, needs, and community issues. Developed by Wang and Burris (1997) with rural women in China, photovoice has three aims: to record and reflect a community's concerns, promote critical dialogue through discussion of photographs, and reach policymakers. The method has a well-described nine-stage methodology including the [[showed-method|SHOWeD]] structured discussion technique.

## Key claims

- Photovoice was developed as a PAR method for participatory needs assessment, combining photography with group dialogue to surface community issues [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al., citing Wang & Burris 1997]]
- The method enables participants who may not have voice in traditional research to document their reality through visual representation and narrate their own experiences [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- Photovoice has been applied across diverse PAR contexts including youth participatory action research (YPAR) with undocumented migrant youth and urban education contexts [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al., citing Vecchio et al. 2017; Tuck & Habtom 2019]]
- Wang (1999) sets out nine key stages: selecting a target audience, recruiting participants, educating on the method, gaining informed consent, brainstorming the focus, distributing cameras, photo-taking time, meeting to discuss using [[showed-method|SHOWeD]], and planning dissemination to the target audience [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Photovoice positions community members rather than researchers as experts in understanding their experiences and needs, providing accessible self-representation beyond what can easily be put into words [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- The method is theoretically grounded in [[paulo-freire|Freire's]] concept of conscientização — generating critical consciousness about structural conditions through collective dialogue [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]

## Adaptations for intellectual disabilities

- A [[chinn-balota-2024-photovoice-intellectual-disabilities|systematic review of 31 studies]] found growing popularity with people with [[intellectual-disability|intellectual disabilities]], offering opportunities for self-representation for people who need support with verbal communication [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Common adaptations: individual interviews replacing or supplementing group discussions; digital cameras with colored sticker guides on buttons; mini printers for tangible copies; walking interviews with researcher accompaniment; carer-assisted photo-taking [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- For people with profound and multiple intellectual disabilities, support workers took photographs from the person's vantage point and at eye level [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota, citing Cluley 2017]]
- Photo-taking is frequently a process of co-creation between people with intellectual disabilities and supporters; this "intrusive presence" of significant others was often under-theorized [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Individual interviews were justified for discussing sensitive issues, exploring personal stories in depth, facilitating reflection, and minimizing peer influence [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]

## Critiques and limitations

- The "action" element is frequently under-delivered: most studies produce knowledge for academic audiences without clear trajectory to policy makers or tangible community change [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota; citing Sanon et al. 2014]]
- Participants with intellectual disabilities were generally excluded from analytic processes of coding and generating themes — seen as a "step too far" (Nind 2011); member checking was more common but critiqued for sidestepping power differentials [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Claims of participant "empowerment" risk framing participants as intrinsically powerless; true empowerment requires access to knowledge, decisions, networks, and resources — not merely giving cameras [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota, citing Wang & Burris 1994]]
- Researcher [[reflexivity]] received little consideration despite being key to qualitative research; few researchers used reflexive journals to examine ableist assumptions [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Ethical issues beyond informed consent (photo ownership, privacy, defamation, emotional aftercare) received limited attention [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
