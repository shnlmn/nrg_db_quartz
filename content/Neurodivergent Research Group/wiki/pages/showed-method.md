# SHOWeD Method

## Summary

A structured discussion technique used in [[photovoice]] to stimulate critical dialogue about photographs. The acronym represents five questions connecting individual images to wider structural inequalities: What do you **S**ee here? What is really **H**appening here? How does this relate to **O**ur lives? **W**hy does this concern, situation, or strength **e**xist? And what can we **D**o about it?

## Key claims

- Developed by Wang (1999) as a key stage of photovoice methodology to move beyond description toward critical consciousness about structural inequalities [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Used in reviewed studies both in group discussion settings (Heffron et al. 2018; Kim et al. 2021) and adapted for individual interviews [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- Some researchers used simplified question variants for people with [[intellectual-disability|intellectual disabilities]]: "What is in this photograph?", "Why did you take it?", "How does this photograph make you feel?" [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
- The SHOWeD questions are theoretically aligned with [[paulo-freire|Freire's]] concept of conscientização — generating critical consciousness that goes beyond individual experience to provide impetus for community social action [[raw/A systematic review of photovoice research methods with people with intellectual disabilities.md|Chinn & Balota]]
