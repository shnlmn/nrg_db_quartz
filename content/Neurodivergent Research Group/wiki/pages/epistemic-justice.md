# Epistemic Justice

**Summary** — Epistemic justice (Fricker, 2007) is the principle that all people deserve recognition as credible sources of knowledge about themselves, particularly concerning their own experiences, perspectives, and ways of knowing. It challenges exclusion and silencing that occurs when people's testimony and knowledge are unfairly discredited or dismissed based on prejudice or social marginalization.

## Key claims

- Epistemic injustice occurs when someone is unfairly excluded or discredited from sharing knowledge about themselves [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- In neurodivergent research contexts, epistemic justice requires recognizing neurodivergent people as credible experts on their own experiences, processing styles, and communication preferences [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Epistemic injustice in research manifests when medicalised views, diagnostic categories, or neurotypical communication norms silence or distort neurodivergent voices [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Achieving epistemic justice requires methodological commitment beyond conceptual agreement—researchers must embed inclusive design practices grounded in dignity, agency, and interpretive respect [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- [[participatory-action-research|PAR]] challenges the system of surveillance and knowledge control established through mainstream research; when communities seek control of research agendas they establish themselves as more powerful epistemic agents [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Foucault's analysis of power as resulting from interactions between people, institutional practices, and forms of knowledge underpins PAR's challenge to epistemic monopolies held by conventional researchers [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Epistemic injustice encompasses injustices in relation to knowledge, including whose knowledge counts and which knowledge is deemed valid or not [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- [[extractive-research]] is a form of epistemic injustice: it extracts information and exploits relationships, producing benefit for scholars elsewhere while depleting resources at research sites [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
