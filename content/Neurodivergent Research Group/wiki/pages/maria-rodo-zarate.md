# Maria Rodó-Zárate

## Summary

Maria Rodó-Zárate is a Serra Húnter Fellow at the University of Barcelona. She holds a PhD in Geography; her research focuses on social inequalities from an intersectional, spatial, and emotional perspective, applied to issues including the right to the city, gender-based violence, and LGBTI-phobia. She is the developer of the [[relief-maps]] tool.

## Key claims

- Developed Relief Maps during doctoral research on [[feminist-geographies]], studying youth access to public urban space in Manresa, a medium-sized Catalan town [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Found that young women felt afraid in public space due to gender; lesbian women additionally feared homophobic aggression, greatly affecting their access to public space [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Gay men shared lesbians' perception of vulnerability in public space; young men generally did not feel afraid [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Restrictions to access were not physical or direct but conditioned by feelings of fear, anxiety, or exclusion — emotional factors as central to urban inequality [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Has examined young lesbians' experiences of public space from an intersectional perspective in published research [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
