# Feminist Geographies

## Summary

Feminist geographies is an interdisciplinary academic field studying how gender and other social structures shape, and are shaped by, spatial arrangements and the lived experience of place. It foregrounds emotional, embodied, and intersectional dimensions of spatial experience as legitimate objects of study and uses them to expose and address urban inequality.

## Key claims

- Studying uneven experiences of the city reveals both vulnerable and privileged groups, and identifies places that function as shelters or safe spaces for different people [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Connects social structure, psychological (emotional) experience, and geographical (physical) space as co-constitutive [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Restrictions to urban access are often emotional rather than physical — fear, anxiety, exclusion — making emotional factors central to understanding urban inequality [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Mei Po Kwan's work defends feminist cartographies and highlights the importance of geoinformation tools (GIS) for social transformation [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
