# Conflict as Resource in Design

## Summary

A distinguishing principle of [[scandinavian-participatory-design|Scandinavian participatory design]] holding that conflicts, contradictions, different perspectives, argument, and heterogeneity are generative resources in design rather than problems to be eliminated. Contrasts with mainstream US design traditions that emphasise harmony and the elimination of "human error."

## Key claims

- Early Scandinavian PD projects asserted a "conflict perspective" in contradistinction to the prevailing "harmony perspective," taking up conflicts between management and labour as a matter of principle [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Morten Kyng: "The design process is a political one and includes conflicts at almost every step of the way... If the inevitable conflicts are pushed to one side or ignored in the rush toward an immediately workable solution, that system may be dramatically less useful and continue to create problems" [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Susanne Bødker frames conflict as a resource for creativity: "Design is fundamentally a collective activity, in which the various practices of the participants meet in a process of [[mutual-learning]]. This meeting creates conflicts that create new possibilities in design" [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Sjöberg (1996) frames the design process as a "culture of argument" in which doubt, openness, incompleteness, lack of closure, iterative processes, and critique are valued resources [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- The principle of "democratic dialogue" holds that "Power and dominance are... meant to be visualised, not neutralised" [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Cultural historical activity theory and Finnish developmental work research direct attention to systemic disturbances, discoordination, dilemmas, breakdowns, and contradictions as opportunities for innovation and expansive transitions [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- The HISP/DHIS project deliberately allowed users to make mistakes to promote continuous learning, contrasting with US design's "near obsession with 'human error' and its elimination" — including the longstanding principle to "eliminate human error" by automating people out of work processes [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Participatory design provides "a framework for individual designers to deal with issues raised during collaboration in design, including how to negotiate conflicting constraints and values, make visible diverse stakeholders' interests and knowledge, and assess design success along a variety of metrics" [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- When value conflict is located in the group of participants, PD's tools and techniques are used to explore value pluralism rather than to build consensus, while postponing decisions on formulating problems and solutions (Bratteteig & Wagner 2014) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Phronesis (practical wisdom) guides dealing with value conflicts in PD: "experience-based ethics oriented towards action" enabling imagination and emotions in the design process and guiding it to serve the common good (Ehn & Badham 2002; Kanstrup & Christiansen 2006) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- A dialogical process (Iversen et al. 2012) enables participants to take non-fixed positions and, through discussion, observation, and visualisation, re-negotiate values — potentially leading to new conceptualisations; e.g., teachers reconceptualised their role as "game masters" when confronted with student-centred values [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Value conflicts can also result from larger organisational conflicts that may be un-dissolvable (Bodker 1996); sensitivity to silences is needed as participants may express disagreement through withdrawal rather than speech [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
