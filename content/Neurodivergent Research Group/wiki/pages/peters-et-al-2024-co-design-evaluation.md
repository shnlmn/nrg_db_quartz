# Peters Et Al 2024 Co-Design Evaluation

## Summary

Systematic overview of reviews by Peters, Guccione, Francis, Best, Tavender, Curran, Davies, Rowe, Palmer, and Klaic (*Implementation Science*, 2024) developing the [[co-design-evaluation-framework|Co-Design Evaluation Framework]]. The study reviewed 51 literature reviews (2000–2022) on co-design evaluation in health research, identified 15 evaluation themes grouped into 7 clusters, and used stakeholder consensus (including consumers, healthcare professionals, and researchers) with a modified [[nominal-group-technique|Nominal Group Technique]] to produce a tree-metaphor framework for prospective, concurrent, and retrospective evaluation of research co-design.

## Key claims

- 51 reviews were included (17 systematic, 12 scoping, 3 narrative, 2 qualitative evidence synthesis, 14 unreported type); individual review sizes ranged from 7 to 260 primary studies [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- Fifteen evaluation themes were identified: structure/composition, contextual enablers/barriers, interrelationships, decision-making process, emotional factors, cognitive factors, value proposition, level/quality of engagement, research process, health outcomes for co-design group, sustainment, healthcare professional outcomes, system-level outcomes, organisational outcomes, and patient/community outcomes [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- Themes were grouped into 7 clusters: People (within co-design group), Group processes, Research processes, Co-design context, People (outside co-design group), System, and Sustainment [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- Research process was the most frequently reported evaluation theme (86% of reviews), followed by cognitive factors (69%) and emotional factors (67%) [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- 84% of reviews did not report measurement methods for evaluation themes; where methods were mentioned, they were mainly qualitative (interviews, focus groups, field notes, observations) or informal (participant feedback, researcher reflections) [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- The most frequently proposed association was between level/quality of engagement and emotional factors (10% of reviews), but these associations lacked empirical evidence [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- Co-design was predominantly used in early research phases: 36 reviews covered agenda/priority setting, 36 study design, 30 data collection, 25 data analysis, and 27 dissemination [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- Types of end-user involvement ranged from targeted consultation (n=14), embedded consultation (n=20), collaboration/co-production (n=14) to user-led research (n=6); 17 papers did not report involvement type [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- Co-design required extra time and funding and carried the risk of becoming tokenistic [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- The framework complements rather than replaces the [[gripp2|GRIPP2]] reporting checklist; it can be used prospectively to plan evaluation, concurrently for improvement, or retrospectively to complete GRIPP2 reporting [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
- PubMed publications involving co-design rose from 173/year in 2000 to 2,617 in 2022 [[raw/Evaluation of research co-design in health_ a systematic overview of reviews and development of a framework.md|Peters et al.]]
