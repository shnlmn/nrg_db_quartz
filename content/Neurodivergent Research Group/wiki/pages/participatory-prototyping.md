# Participatory Prototyping

## Summary

A core [[scandinavian-participatory-design|Participatory Design]] method (also called cooperative or collaborative prototyping) in which values are translated into requirements and materialised in a designed object through active involvement of future users (Lim, Stolterman & Tenenberg 2008). Creates a shared design space where tensions between "what is" and "what could be" are explored through enacting [[use-before-use]] scenarios.

## Key claims

- Participatory prototyping is the activity in the design process where values are translated into requirements and become materialised in a designed object [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Prototypes differ in material (paper, digital), resolution (mock-ups, low- and high-fidelity), and scope; focused prototypes enable more in-depth exploration of particular design dimensions (Lim et al. 2008) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Prototyping creates a shared design space for designers and co-designers to explore tensions through enacting use scenarios; it can provoke hindrances, new possibilities (Brodersen et al. 2008), opportunities, and dilemmas (Hillgren et al. 2011) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Prototyping is iterative, typically evolving from low-fidelity to high-fidelity with full specifications of the finished product [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Digital prototypes may look too "finished" and present a solution before the design problem is fully explored; on the other hand, concrete renderings of co-designers' values and wishes encourage engagement and continued design [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- In the KULU project with teenagers with chronic health challenges, participatory prototyping of a calendar function became a space for engaging with multiple understandings of privacy, materialised in a flexible design supporting three use-scenarios (medical-only, mixed, or both-but-separate) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
