# Health Disparities

## Summary

Health disparities are inequities in health outcomes, access, effectiveness, uptake, and reach of treatments and programming across populations, particularly affecting marginalized groups (e.g., ethnic and racial minorities). Addressing health disparities is the primary driving imperative for [[community-based-participatory-research|CBPR]] and a core motivation for participatory research approaches in public health and psychology.

## Key claims

- CBPR can enhance research efforts in addressing mental health disparities in access, effectiveness, uptake, and reach of treatments for marginalized groups (Belone et al. 2016) [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Government agencies including NIMHD, AHRQ, SAMHSA, and PCORI have created funding mechanisms to support research addressing health disparities through community involvement [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR-generated interventions have been effective in improving community health across populations and health outcomes (O'Mara-Eves et al. 2015) [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR is well-positioned to address health disparities because it engages hard-to-reach, marginalized populations through trust-building and equitable partnerships (Tapp et al. 2013) [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Health status is shaped by larger familial, community, societal, and geopolitical forces (Bronfenbrenner 1979), requiring multidisciplinary and identity-diverse teams [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
