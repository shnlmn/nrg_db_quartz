# Sensory Sensitivity Coping Strategies

## Summary

Coping strategies are mechanisms individuals use to manage, reduce, or tolerate sensory sensitivities. Qualitative research identifies five broad categories of adaptive responses: limiting sensory input, using compensatory sensory stimulation, self-regulation, social support, and environmental control/predictability.

## Limiting Sensory Input

Avoidance and attenuation strategies that reduce exposure to or intensity of unwanted sensory stimuli:

- **Avoidance** — withdrawing from or avoiding known problematic sensory environments (e.g., public spaces, specific venues)
- **Physical barriers** — earplugs, headphones, sunglasses, covering skin to reduce sensory contact
- **Environmental modification** — adjusting lighting, sound, or proximity to sources
- **Withdrawal or breaks** — taking breaks from challenging environments or withdrawing entirely to recover

## Sensory Stimulation (Compensatory/Masking)

Using enjoyable or neutral sensory inputs to distract, mask, or counterbalance unwanted sensations:

- **Fidget toys and stimming** — tactile self-stimulation (hand-wringing, finger-clicking, fidget toys) to manage overwhelming sensory environments [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Music or auditory masking** — using music or other sounds to distract or mask uncomfortable sensations (e.g., during exercise)
- **Counterbalancing** — applying opposing sensory input to balance an unwanted sensation (e.g., striking one side of body to offset being struck on the other); also includes reducing personal sensory contribution in loud environments
- **Visual or tactile immersion** — engaging with video games, mobile phones, or visually following something to redirect attention

## Self-Regulation

Cognitive and emotional strategies to manage internal responses to sensory stimuli:

- **Mindfulness and breathing techniques** — using awareness and controlled breathing to manage anxiety and emotional response
- **Acceptance** — acknowledging and accepting negative feelings rather than fighting them
- **Distraction and immersion** — engaging in conversation, games, or other absorbing activities to shift focus away from sensory input
- **Medication** — using prescribed medications (SSRIs, anxiety medications) to manage comorbid mental health symptoms that exacerbate sensitivities

## Control and Predictability

Establishing structure, routine, and advance planning to reduce uncertainty and maintain psychological control:

- **Routine and structure** — maintaining consistent daily patterns to create predictability
- **Anticipatory planning** — considering sensory outcomes before entering situations
- **Cognitive preparation** — mentally rehearsing or planning responses to known triggers

## The Role of Others

Leveraging social support and accommodation:

- **Communicating needs** — discussing sensory needs with friends, family, employers, housemates to increase awareness
- **Receiving accommodations** — obtaining practical support or environmental modifications from others
- **Suppression in social contexts** — managing or hiding reactions in front of others due to concerns about judgment or relational impact (note: this may be adaptive in some contexts but can increase emotional labor and fatigue)

## Negative Health Behaviors

Some participants reported maladaptive coping strategies:

- **Alcohol self-medication** — using alcohol to tolerate environments that would otherwise cause sensory overload [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Self-harm** — skin picking, digging fingernails into body as self-regulation responses [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]

## Professional Intervention

- **Exposure therapy** — some participants reported improved coping following professional therapy [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Mindfulness-based interventions** — building emotional resilience to challenging multi-sensory environments; some participants already use these techniques, and the study suggests broader applicability [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]

## Clinical Implications

- Effective coping often involves **multiple simultaneous strategies** tailored to context and sensory domain
- **Emotional and psychiatric comorbidity** affect coping capacity (depression, anxiety reduce effectiveness)
- **Social context and acceptance** significantly influence whether coping strategies are sustainable or create additional strain
- **Prevention of sensory overload** (through predictability and control) may be more sustainable long-term than crisis-response coping
- **Avoidance may have paradoxical effects** — commonly reported, but consequent exclusion from environments (especially social) also impacts wellbeing. For hyperacusis, chronic ear-plug use may worsen auditory sensitivity (Henry et al. 2022). [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Comparative efficacy of coping mechanisms is unknown** — whether engagement-based coping (stimming, mindfulness) produces better outcomes than avoidance remains an open question [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
