# Presume Competence

## Summary

Presuming competence means assuming an AAC user is capable of full, robust communication and designing systems and instruction accordingly, rather than pre-emptively limiting vocabulary or access on the assumption of low ability. In practice this means resisting the urge to mask words or shrink an AAC user's grid, and teaching search and descriptive-language strategies instead of building narrow, activity-specific pages.

## Key claims

- Before limiting a student's AAC system by removing words, educators should decide whether doing so will limit communication in other settings or with other partners; masking or shrinking the grid does not speed learning and instead holds the student back from communicating what they want across contexts. [[raw/AAC_and_Autism_Tip_Sheet_Edu.extracted.md]]
- "More words are often better than fewer words" — presuming competence means not limiting a student's potential through restricted vocabulary. [[raw/AAC_and_Autism_Tip_Sheet_Edu.extracted.md]]
- Rather than building a new page for each specific activity or game, educators should teach the AAC user to use the system's search feature and similar-word strategies, which generalize across the user's whole communication journey. [[raw/AAC_and_Autism_Tip_Sheet_Edu.extracted.md]]
