# Stuart Neilson

## Summary

Stuart Neilson is an autistic researcher, artist, and blogger based in Cork, Ireland, known for developing the [[motion-intensity-heatmap|motion intensity heatmap]] methodology for visualising pedestrian movement patterns in public spaces. He collaborated with [[magda-mostafa|Magda Mostafa]] on the "A Case for Sensory Decolonisation: Autistic Escape" installation at the 2023 Venice Architecture Biennale, contributing heatmaps, motion composites, and tactile CNC panels to the exhibition. He also contributed imagery to Mostafa's [[sensory-decolonisation|Sensory Atlas]].

## Background and Motivation

Neilson writes from an autistic first-person perspective: he experiences difficulty identifying where to place himself in relation to crowds and how to navigate to desired destinations in busy public spaces. His visual and mapping work is a direct attempt to capture, communicate, and address this experience — identifying **interstitial refuges** and navigable routes through sensory intensity.

He blogs at wordpress.stuartneilson.com and records video primarily in Cork City, supplemented by live-streamed feeds of global public spaces.

## Methodology

Neilson developed a [[motion-intensity-heatmap|motion intensity heatmap]] technique — accumulating pixel-level changes across video frames to produce visual maps of pedestrian flow intensity. He applies this to self-recorded and live-streamed video from cities worldwide (Cork, Dublin, Milan, Venice, Tokyo, London, Oxford, New York, Heraklion). The resulting maps reveal sensory refuges (low-intensity areas), busy paths, and the gradient structure between them.

He extends this into:
- **Tactile heatmaps** — CNC-cut physical panels (cardboard, aluminium, perspex, MDF, recycled plastics) enabling simultaneous visual and tactile engagement with the motion landscape
- **Sensory composites** — long-exposure / motion composite photographs evoking a multisensory sense of place; Neilson reports that viewing these images recalls weather, sound, and smell from the original recording
- **Semimaginary terrain maps** — treating motion intensity as topographic elevation to produce contour maps with imaginary rivers (low-intensity flight lines) and roads (minimum-gradient connecting routes)

## Venice Architecture Biennale 2023

Neilson's work was showcased in "A Case for Sensory Decolonisation: Autistic Escape", curated by Magda Mostafa as part of the Time Space Existence Collection at the 2023 Venice Architecture Biennale (Palazzo Mora, European Cultural Centre).

Three locations were selected for the wall projection installation:
- **Dublin City University** (Glasnevin Campus) — recorded by Neilson in person from the top floor of the campus car park
- **Rialto Bridge, Venice** — from streamed video
- **Eleftheriou Venizelou Square (Lions Square), Heraklion** — from live CCTV feed

Mostafa's team at the American University Cairo produced full-size MDF tactile panels based on Neilson's heatmap designs. Each panel set combined a long-exposure photo (above) and motion intensity heatmap (below), with a video loop cycling through photo, timelapse, developing heatmap, and final intensity map. The video fade metaphor: "as if the projected people have laser-cut the textured surface by walking on it."

The installation included a sensory refuge made from acoustic sound-absorbing foam with a stool for viewing. A walkthrough video of the installation was published 2023-11-29 on Vimeo (CC Attribution No Derivatives): https://vimeo.com/889418692

Neilson also published a detailed account of the work at: https://wordpress.stuartneilson.com/identifying-landscapes-of-sensory-escape

## Venice Architecture Biennale 2021

Neilson also contributed to the 2021 Biennale exhibition "Autistic Imaginaries of Architectural Space: The World from an Autistic Lens" curated by Mostafa at Palazzo Bembo.

## DCU Note

Neilson was **not** part of the process developing Dublin City University's autism friendly campus programme; he created the DCU motion composites as a naive first-time visitor to the campus.
