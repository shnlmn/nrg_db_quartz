# Cultural Humility

## Summary

Cultural humility is an orientation involving an accurate view of one's own identity, not assuming one's own identities, values, and perspectives are superior to others', and being open to and interested in the identities, values, and perspectives of others. It is a core principle of [[community-based-participatory-research|CBPR]], requiring researchers to recognize their intersecting social identities and critically examine their impact on research engagement and power dynamics.

## Key claims

- Cultural humility was defined by Hook, Davis, Owen, Worthington, & Utsey (2013) as having an accurate self-view, not assuming superiority, and being open to others' perspectives [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Originally conceptualized by Tervalon & Murray-Garcia (1998) as a lifelong commitment to self-evaluation and self-critique, addressing power imbalances in clinical and research relationships [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- CBPR practitioners cultivate cultural humility by recognizing their own intersecting social identities (race, ethnicity, gender, education, socioeconomic status) and critically examining their impact on community engagement [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- Researchers must recognize they do not have a monopoly on knowledge; communities have other "ways of knowing" that complement the scientific evidence base [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
