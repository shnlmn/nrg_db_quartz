# Patient And Public Involvement

## Summary

Patient and Public Involvement (PPI) is a research co-design approach, predominantly used in the United Kingdom, defined as doing research "with" or "by" the public rather than "to," "about," or "for" the public. PPI differentiates three levels of involvement: consultation (seeking public views on key aspects), collaboration (ongoing partnership throughout), and publicly-led research (public designs and undertakes the research, inviting researchers to participate).

## Key claims

- PPI was the UK-dominant term for end-user involvement in health research, while "research engagement" was more frequently used in the United States [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- PPI encompasses three levels of involvement: (1) consultation, where researchers seek public views on key aspects; (2) collaboration, an ongoing partnership throughout the research process; (3) publicly-led, where the public designs and undertakes the research [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- Brett et al. found PPI can build important community links, improve recruitment from seldom-heard groups, develop researcher empathy, improve informed consent, and help develop and assess research instruments [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- Some end-users experienced PPI as tokenistic, feeling "not listened to, frustrated and marginalised" — highlighting the gap between aspirational and actual involvement [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- PPI evaluation typically relied on narrative description and qualitative analysis of small samples; longer-term effects on health outcomes were not examined [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- The [[gripp2|GRIPP/GRIPP2]] reporting checklist provides a standardized framework for reporting PPI activities in research [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
