# Sensory Decolonisation

## Summary

Sensory decolonisation is the critical concept that the sensory environments of contemporary cities have been colonised by capitalistic proliferation — surfaces, spaces, and experiences commodified as sensory media competing for human attention and cognitive bandwidth. The autistic experience is positioned as a "litmus test" for this sensory taxation. Sensory decolonisation as a design goal calls for spatial infrastructure that restores access to sensory escape, retreat, and refuge.

## Key Claims

- Contemporary cities have become "canvases for sensory media, competing for our attention"; sensory perception has become commodified [[raw/ASPECTSS_EXHIBITIONS]]
- This sensory colonisation disproportionately burdens autistic citizens, for whom sensory processing already differs from the neurotypical norm
- The autistic experience "affords us a litmus test for sensory taxation and can expose the impact of this sensory commodification on our cognition, mental bandwidth and ability to access space equitably" [[raw/ASPECTSS_EXHIBITIONS]]
- Autistic sensory escape (retreat from overstimulation) is framed as **spatial protest** against sensory colonisation [[raw/ASPECTSS_EXHIBITIONS]]
- A decolonised sensory infrastructure requires a full spectrum of sensory experiences: escape, retreat, and refuge from over-stimulation alongside opportunities for sensory seeking

## Relation to ASPECTSS

The concept was introduced as the thesis of the [[aspectss-design-index|ASPECTSS®]] Venice Biennale 2023 installation "A Case for Sensory Decolonisation: Autistic Escape." It extends the ASPECTSS framework beyond individual building design toward critique of the sensory politics of entire urban environments.

## EscapeScapes

The installation proposed "EscapeScapes" — designed landscapes of sensory escape — as the spatial implementation of sensory decolonisation. These are spaces that:

- Provide relief from multi-sensory urban noise
- Function as [[sensory-refuge|sensory refuge]] at the urban/public scale
- Are framed as a form of "senso-spatial acupuncture"

## Neilson's Contribution and the Sensory Atlas

[[stuart-neilson|Stuart Neilson]] contributed [[motion-intensity-heatmap|motion intensity heatmaps]], long-exposure sensory composites, and timelapse sequences of global city spaces to Mostafa's **Sensory Atlas** — a curated collection of visual records of cities from an autistic sensory perspective. Three locations were selected from Neilson's work for the Venice Biennale 2023 wall projection: Dublin City University (Glasnevin Campus), Rialto Bridge (Venice), and Eleftheriou Venizelou Square (Heraklion). Physical tactile panels were produced by Mostafa's team at the American University Cairo: full-size MDF panels CNC-cut with the motion intensity contours, with a video loop cycling through photo, timelapse, developing heatmap, and final map. The metaphor: pedestrians laser-cutting the surface by walking on it.

## Open Questions

- How does sensory decolonisation relate to existing urban design policy (e.g., quiet zones, low-traffic neighbourhoods)?
- What empirical methods could measure the "sensory colonisation" of a public space over time?
- Does the framing risk centering autistic experience in ways that overshadow other marginalised sensory communities (e.g., people with chronic pain, PTSD)?
