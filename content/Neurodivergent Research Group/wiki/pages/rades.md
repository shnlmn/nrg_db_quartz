# RADES

## Summary

Reactions and Actions Description Survey (RADES) is a data collection technique that uses 120 visual images showing diverse human expressions, emotions, and activities to elicit verbal descriptions of people's subjective experiences in a place. Participants review images and indicate which experiences resonate with their visit, then quantify intensity on a 1-10 scale.

## Key claims

- RADES employs 120 images depicting people experiencing different sensory modalities, showing both positive and negative emotional expressions, and engaging in varied activities [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- The 120 images are balanced: 50% neutral expressions, 25% positive emotion expressions, 25% negative emotion expressions; approximately two-thirds show non-visual sensory experiences [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Participants select images that match their experience, annotate with free-text descriptions, then transform comments into quantified experience ratings (1-10 scale) [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- RADES was refined through pilot testing in a dozen different places over 2015-2022, improving images and instructions before formal deployment [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- The technique is flexible and transforms according to participants' reported experiences rather than imposing pre-set categories [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Participants rate temporal dimensions of awareness: what they noticed first (F), what they noticed while answering the survey (S), and what they consider most important (I) [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]

## Comparison to related methods

RADES differs from simpler sensory assessment techniques by:
- Capturing emotional, bodily, and interactive experiences alongside sensory ones (vs. visual analog scales for individual sensory descriptors)
- Using visual prompts to expand the range of experiences participants naturally describe (broader than open-ended narrative alone)
- Quantifying subjective experience intensity through participant-generated questions (vs. researcher-imposed scales)
