# Value Sensitive Design

## Summary

A design approach (VSD) that aims to advance a predefined set of moral values in technology design, particularly human welfare, ownership and property, privacy, freedom from bias, universal usability, trust, autonomy, informed consent, accountability, identity, calmness, and environmental sustainability (Friedman & Kahn 2003). Contrasted with [[scandinavian-participatory-design|Participatory Design]], which enables values to emerge dynamically through the design process rather than frontloading predetermined moral values.

## Key claims

- VSD advances specific moral values identified a priori: human welfare, ownership and property, privacy, freedom from bias, universal usability, trust, autonomy, informed consent, accountability, identity, calmness, and environmental sustainability (Friedman & Kahn 2003) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Manders-Huits & Zimmer (2009) differentiate VSD from PD: VSD seeks to broaden criteria for judging technological systems to include the advancement of moral and human values, while PD promotes functional or instrumental values [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Manders-Huits & Zimmer acknowledge PD as a value-centred approach but argue it "falls short of directly addressing values of moral import, such as privacy or autonomy" [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- This distinction raises the question of whose values are advanced: VSD's predetermined list reflects researcher/designer judgements, while PD enables co-designers to define which values matter in their context [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
