# Kilfoy Et Al 2024 Digital Codesign Umbrella Review

## Summary

Umbrella review by Kilfoy, Hsu, Stockton-Powdrell, Whelan, Chu, and Jibb published in *npj Digital Medicine* (2024) synthesizing 21 reviews (2015–2023) on how co-design of [[digital-health-interventions]] is conducted and reported. The review found pervasive inconsistency in co-design terminology, methods reporting, participant demographics, and evaluation—all 21 included reviews were rated critically low quality on [[amstar-2]].

## Key claims

- Searched five databases from inception; identified 3903 titles, screened 2861 after deduplication, included 21 reviews published between 2015 and 2023 [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Co-design terminology varied widely across reviews: co-design (n=3), participatory design (n=3), human-centered development (n=2), human factor approaches (n=1), community-based participatory research (n=1), patient-centered methods (n=1), and 9 reviews used multiple terms interchangeably [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Co-design participants typically included patients, caregivers, and healthcare professionals; 19 of 21 reviews included a range of stakeholder types, while only 2 involved patients exclusively [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Demographic reporting was poor: only 1 review reported participant race/ethnicity, and only 3 reported gender/sex [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Duration and frequency of co-design sessions reported by fewer than half of reviews (8/21 for duration, 7/21 for frequency), with inadequate detail even where reported [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Most frequently used co-design activities: focus groups (17/21 reviews), interviews (14/21), surveys (11/21), observation (10/21), think-aloud (6/21), storyboarding (6/21), persona/scenario building (6/21) [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Only 2 of 21 reviews qualified the degree of participant involvement using a participatory framework; Cole et al. used an inform–consult–involve–collaborate–empower scale finding most studies involved only the first three levels; Orlowski et al. found 70% of projects reported predominantly consultative consumer involvement [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Only one included meta-analysis evaluated co-design effectiveness, and it did not support the notion that participatory-designed digital games improve health outcomes more than non-co-designed ones [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Reported barriers to co-design: power imbalances between researchers and participants, time and financial constraints, difficulty recruiting minority/vulnerable participants, participant "groupthink," and privileging of medical professionals' views over patients' [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Mitigating strategies for co-design barriers: building relationships and trust, empowering end-users, building end-user knowledge, establishing value and interest, and conducting co-design in environments familiar to participants [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Design work historically engages "super-users" (frequent research contributors) who are often not representative of the target population, potentially exacerbating digital divides for older adults, young children, and those with low health or digital literacy [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Accessibility and equity discussed in only 8/21 reviews; strategies included cultural advisors, proactive outreach, and formal community protocols (e.g., Maori/Pacific Islander opening/closing sessions) [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Only half of reviews utilized a framework to guide their review or reported frameworks from their identified studies; 32 co-design frameworks/models/theories were described across reviews, most commonly participatory design, user-centered design, and human-centered design [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- All 21 reviews were classified as critically low quality on [[amstar-2]], with particularly low scores on description of included study designs [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Existing guidelines ([[gripp2|GRIPP and GRIPP2]]) are generic and not specific to co-design in digital health; standardized digital-health-specific co-design reporting guidelines are needed [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Reviews were only conducted in high-income countries; reporting from low- and middle-income countries is required given digital health's potential to improve healthcare access globally [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
