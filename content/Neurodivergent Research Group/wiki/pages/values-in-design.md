# Values in Design

## Summary

A distinguishing principle of [[scandinavian-participatory-design|Scandinavian participatory design]] holding that design is an inherently value-laden activity and that values — including design intentions, imagined futures, and commitments to democracy — should be made explicit and discussed collaboratively among all participants rather than treated as external to the technical process.

## Key claims

- Lucy Suchman describes the Scandinavian PD orientation: "designing computer artefacts is an inherently value-based activity, deeply implicated in longstanding political struggles of the wider society... this alternative position embraces the place of systems development as a critical arena for the expression and enhancement of values of industrial democracy" [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- This stands in sharp contrast to the still-dominant US mainstream view of systems design as "strictly technical and commercial arenas" in which technical expertise is privileged above other forms of knowledge [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Value-oriented design encompasses care in building working relationships of trust, reciprocity, and [[mutual-learning]], with the understanding that these are relationships with communities that need to last over time [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- The orientation towards imagined futures includes participatory design methods for critiquing the present and envisioning change, keeping design decisions open, and conceptualising "diverse forms of ongoing design-in-use" [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Design is conceptualised in relation to social practices, continuous learning, and imagined futures rather than narrowly in relation to a product or system [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- Discussions of design intentions, explication of values embedded in design strategies and choices, and shared discussions of values implicit and explicit in imagined futures all characterise collaborative work in Scandinavian PD [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- PD projects do not frontload a fixed set of values but enable values to emerge and be co-created during the design process; moral values may also be frontloaded when appropriate to the particular context (van den Hoven 2007) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- The relationship of values to design is dynamic: values emerge during co-design whether or not designers look for them, can change and even conflict as the process unfolds; this bottom-up approach provides leverage for solving practical co-design problems (Halloran et al. 2009) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- Values can stay implicit or latent, only emerging when participants are challenged by others or by developments in the design process; a dialogical process (Iversen et al. 2012) enables participants to question and renegotiate their values through discussions, observations, and visualisations [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- The design process functions as a "contact zone" (Haraway 2003; Pratt 1998) — a space for communication across irreducible differences and situated partial connections, where different meanings of values meet and grapple; consensus is agonistic and non-coercive (Mouffe 1993) [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- [[value-sensitive-design|Value Sensitive Design]] contrasts with PD by frontloading a predetermined set of moral values; Manders-Huits & Zimmer (2009) argue PD promotes functional/instrumental values while VSD promotes moral values, but this raises the question of whose values are advanced [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
- In multicultural settings, the same values (e.g., autonomy, privacy) can have different and conflicting meanings; the design process as contact zone helps navigate this pluralism without requiring full mutual understanding [[raw/Participatory_Design_and_Design_for_Valu.extracted.md|van der Velden & Mortberg]]
