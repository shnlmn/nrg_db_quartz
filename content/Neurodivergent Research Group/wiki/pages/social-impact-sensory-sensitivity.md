# Social and Functional Impact of Sensory Sensitivities

## Summary

Subjective sensory sensitivities significantly constrain both functional participation and social relationships. The impact extends beyond direct sensory discomfort to encompass emotional volatility, exhaustion, social isolation, and relational strain—particularly when others lack understanding of the underlying sensory basis for behavior.

## Functional Limitations

- **Daily tasks disrupted** — participants reported difficulty with sleep, driving, shopping, public transport use, cooking, and other essential activities [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Impairment pathways:** Direct (sensory challenge prevents task) or indirect (emotional/physical consequences of hypersensitivity reduce capacity to perform)
- **Work and study impact** — lighting, sounds, and smells contribute to difficulties with concentration, uncompleted work, and reduced academic attainment. Some participants report being unable to hold down a job due to sensory overstimulation. [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Exhaustion as a consequence** — managing sensory input is cognitively and emotionally taxing; tiredness both results from and exacerbates sensory sensitivities
- **Challenging locations** — of 213 spontaneously mentioned location instances: supermarkets/retail, education/work, and events/concerts accounted for over half; public transport, bars/restaurants, outdoor/streets, and nightlife also featured. [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]

## Social and Relational Impact

- **Emotional dysregulation triggered by sensory stimuli** — participants reported that sensory input (particularly repetitive noises) could cause emotional lability, anger, or lashing out at others unrelated to the sensory trigger [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Other people as sensory challenge** — specific individuals (often household members) can be the direct source of difficult sensory experiences (e.g., eating sounds, voice quality), creating particular tension because withdrawal causes guilt [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Guilt and shame** — participants described guilt over needing space from family members (including children), shame about sensory reactions, and embarrassment about coping behaviors [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]
- **Difficulty communicating sensory needs** — without explicit dialogue, sensory distress may be misattributed to being "dramatic," oppositional, or having personality problems
- **Social withdrawal** — avoidance of social settings (crowds, public spaces, group events) to manage sensory challenges, leading to reduced participation and potential isolation. Participants often expressed a desire to attend events but inability to do so. [[price-et-al-2025-sensory-sensitivities]]

## Suppression of Reactions (Social Masking)

- **Hiding sensory distress in social contexts** — many individuals suppress, minimize, or hide their sensory reactions when others are present due to concerns about:
  - How they will appear to others
  - Impact on others' comfort or judgment
  - Social acceptability of their response
- **Emotional labor** — suppression requires cognitive and emotional effort, compounding exhaustion
- **Unmet support** — suppression may prevent others (family, employers, peers) from understanding needs and offering accommodation

## Implications for Understanding and Support

- **Misattribution of behavior** — without sensory literacy, sensory avoidance may be labeled as shyness, rudeness, anxiety disorder, or oppositional defiance
- **Importance of transparent communication** — participants who discussed their sensory needs with those close to them received practical accommodations and emotional support
- **[[double-empathy-problem|Bidirectional miscommunication]]** — neurotypical others may not recognize sensory distress without explicit communication; neurodivergent individuals may assume others should understand without explanation
- **Validation and acceptance reduce isolation** — research underscores the importance of creating environments where sensory needs are recognized as legitimate rather than treated as behavioral problems
