# Autism Friendly University Design Guide

## Summary

The Autism Friendly University Design Guide is an open-access design guide developed by [[magda-mostafa|Magda Mostafa]] (Progressive Architects / American University in Cairo) for Dublin City University (DCU), the world's first designated autism-friendly university. Completed 09/04/2023, it extends [[aspectss-design-index|ASPECTSS® 2.0]] to the higher education built environment using evidence from a 2016 DCU student study and sensory audits co-conducted with autistic stakeholders. It received the UIA Friendly and Inclusive Spaces Award 2023 ([[uia-world-congress-2023]]).

## Context

DCU was designated the world's first autism-friendly university in 2018. The guide was initiated in response to the "peak cohort" — autistic individuals diagnosed during the early-2000s awareness surge — who are now entering higher education that is unprepared for them. See [[autism-lifespan-design]] [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]].

> "A mindful organization of the built environment of higher education spaces may play an important role in achieving these goals by removing barriers and creating more authentic and integrated opportunity for spatial inclusion of autistic and fellow learner students."

## Methodology

Two primary data sources [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]]:

1. **"Living with Autism" (2016)** — DCU student study developing "University Friendly" autism support recommendations
2. **Sensory Audits** — conducted by Autism Friendly with DCU stakeholders (students, advocates, staff)

The research process included:
- Literature review on HE support for autism; review of European and American autism research
- Online survey of all DCU students
- Semi-structured interviews and focus groups with autistic students
- Sensory audit of two DCU campuses (noise, smells, lighting, signage)
- Additional sensory audits for guide development purposes

## Workshop Stakeholders

The design process used participatory workshops with representatives from [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]]:

- Autism Friendly University Initiative
- AsIAm (autism charity and self-advocacy group)
- The Neurodiverse Student Society
- The autistic student body
- DCU Student Services
- DCU Occupational Therapists
- DCU Estates Office
- DCU Health and Safety
- DCU Smart / Facilities
- Ireland's National Autism Society

Moderated by the core design team and the Coordinator of DCU's Autism Friendly Initiative.

## Design Thinking Adaptations for Autistic Participants

The guide was developed through design thinking workshops adapted for autistic participants using seven named principles [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]]:

| Principle | Core Adaptation |
|---|---|
| **Visual over Verbal** | Visual aids, clear grids, fresh boards for each section; reduce visual chaos |
| **Order over Stimulation** | Separate boards and "fresh starts" per workshop portion; avoid layered accumulation |
| **Personal over Social Broadly** | Groups of 5 with one moderator; more personal connection, manageable social environment |
| **Autistic Social Space over Neurotypical Social Space** | Autistic voice positioned as "expert voice"; balanced groupings across stakeholders |
| **Planning Ahead and Social Familiarization** | Detailed agenda, participant bios, and video tutorial shared in advance; designated technical support contact |
| **Timing and Scheduling is Key** | Granular agenda (5-minute intervals); visual timer visible to all; clear start/stop and break times |
| **Predictability and Preparedness** | Objectives always stated clearly; agenda always visible and recapped; expectations explicit throughout |

Key tension: open-ended design thinking encourages lack of conformity, which can be difficult for autistic participants — expectations are shared while still inviting new ideas. See also [[inclusive-research-methods]].

## Design Topics (21 Criteria)

The guide addresses 21 design domains, corresponding to [[aspectss-design-index|ASPECTSS 2.0]] plus social and operational dimensions [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]]:

1. Community Integration, Blurring Boundaries, and Space as Stigma Reduction
2. Reverse Inclusion
3. Design as a Tool for Social and Environmental Management and Mediation
4. Integration of Natural Elements
5. Universal Design: The Autistic Perspective
6. Agility, Flexibility, and Adaptability
7. Acoustics
8. Spatial Sequencing
9. Escape Spaces
10. Compartmentalization
11. Transition Zones
12. Sensory Zoning
13. Safety
14. Colour
15. Lighting
16. Material Selection
17. Furnishing
18. Wayfinding and Navigation
19. Technology
20. Sensory Economics
21. Programming and Operation

Items 7–21 map to the 15 ASPECTSS 2.0 criteria (7 original + 8 added). Items 1–6 address broader social, ecological, and universal design dimensions specific to the HE context.

## Sensory Spatial Organization

The guide proposes a three-tier sensory hierarchy for HE spaces [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]]:

| Stimulus Level | Space Types |
|---|---|
| **High** | Transitional space, outdoor entry/pre-space, group dining/socializing |
| **Moderate** | Group socializing/dining, study area |
| **Low** | Isolation space, sensory escape space |

Three spatial typologies:

1. **Open/Dynamic/Group** — large open plan, small group outdoor pavilion, dynamic group platform
2. **Alcove/Pod/Individualised** — enclosed social alcoves/pods, private 2-person spaces, small group quiet pods with partitions
3. **Individual/Enclosed/Static** — contoured body seating, hammocks, community swings and sensory hammocks

This tripartite schema operationalizes [[sensory-zoning]] and [[sensory-refuge]] at campus scale.

## Audit Application

The guide includes an Autism Friendly Design Audit tool for [[post-occupancy-evaluation|post-occupancy evaluation]] [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]]:

- Assess existing environments
- Evaluate designs and pilot projects
- Provide gap analysis to prioritize design and facility decisions
- Facilitate community member participation across university life

## Replication and Dissemination

Explicit intent [[raw/UIA A1 POSTER_ Final to submit.extracted.md]]:

- Open-access document
- Catalyse design processes at different scales and across HE typologies
- Intended for replication across HE globally and scale-up to cities, neighbourhoods, and nations
- Template for "an autism friendly world where diversity can flourish everywhere"
