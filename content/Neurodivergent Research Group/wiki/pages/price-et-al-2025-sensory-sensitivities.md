# Price Et Al 2025 Sensory Sensitivities

## Summary

Price, A., Oates, R., Sumner, P., & Powell, G. (2025). "They Impact My Life Daily and Greatly": A Qualitative Exploration of How Subjective Sensory Sensitivities Are Experienced, Exacerbated, and Coped With. Published in *Neurodiversity* (SAGE), DOI: 10.1177/27546330251383024. A diagnosis-independent qualitative study of 713 individuals exploring how [[subjective-sensory-sensitivities]] affect daily life, what exacerbates them, and how people cope.

## Key Claims

- **Sensory sensitivity is trans-diagnostic and pervasive.** The study took a diagnosis-independent approach, finding that themes were not primarily driven by autistic participants (max 30% contribution to any theme). Of 713 participants, 297 reported no diagnosis or neurodivergence, yet described comparable sensory challenges. [[raw/'They Impact My Life Daily and Greatly'_ A Qualitative Exploration of How Subjective Sensory Sensitivities Are Experienced, Exacerbated, and Coped With - Alice Price, Rebe.md]]

- **Impact on daily life is multi-faceted.** Sensitivities limit social and functional capabilities, create challenges in personal relationships, and are described as effortful and exhausting. Participants reported effects on work, study, daily tasks (shopping, cooking, driving, sleeping), and interpersonal relationships. [[social-impact-sensory-sensitivity]]

- **Exacerbating factors are both situational and internal.** Situational: lack of prediction/control, focused tasks, crowds. Internal: tiredness, stress, symptom change (anxiety, depression, OCD, migraine). Several factors show bidirectional causal relationships (stress increases sensitivity which increases stress — a "sensory avalanche" cycle). [[sensory-sensitivity-exacerbating-factors]]

- **Coping mechanisms are diverse.** Four main categories: limiting sensory input (avoidance, earplugs, sunglasses); sensory stimulation (fidget toys, stimming, music, counterbalancing); self-regulation (mindfulness, breathing, distraction, acceptance); and the role of others (communicating needs, receiving accommodations, suppressing reactions). Negative health behaviors (alcohol, skin picking) were also reported. [[sensory-sensitivity-coping-strategies]]

- **Specific physical locations are spontaneously identified as problematic.** Of 213 coded location instances: supermarkets/retail, education/work, and events/concerts accounted for over half. Other categories included outdoor/streets, public transport, bars/pubs/restaurants, nightlife, indoor public spaces, homes, cinemas, and recreation.

- **Environmental accommodations are recommended.** Adjustable blinds, sound reduction, recovery spaces, and prior information about crowded events. Individual voice and [[co-design-in-research|co-design]] remain important because common themes do not establish that accommodations suit every individual.

- **Mindfulness-based interventions show promise.** Some participants already use mindfulness and breathing techniques; the study suggests these could build emotional resilience to sensory environments that cannot be physically modified, though efficacy requires further investigation.

## Methodology

- **Design:** Qualitative online survey with open-ended questions; template thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke 2021; Brooks et al. 2015) combining deductive and inductive coding
- **Epistemology:** Critical realism — acknowledges researcher influence while maintaining that phenomena exist independently
- **Sample:** n=713 (563 undergraduate students at Cardiff University + 150 recruited via social media forums for clinical conditions/neurodivergence)
- **Demographics:** 82.6% female, mean age 22.7 (SD 9.4), range 17–75
- **Diagnoses:** 297 (42%) reported no diagnosis or neurodivergence; remainder self-reported various conditions
- **Analysis:** Template TA with both a priori and a posteriori themes; count analysis of spontaneously mentioned problematic locations (n=213 instances)

## Conceptual Framework

The paper presents a conceptual framework (Figure 1) showing:
- Heightened subjective sensitivity exists in clinical/neurodivergent populations and the general population
- Exacerbating factors have feedforward and bidirectional relationships with sensitivity
- Coping mechanisms mediate between sensitivity and quality-of-life impacts
- Physical locations provide targets for environmental accommodation
