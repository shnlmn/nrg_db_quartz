# Sensory Refuge

## Summary

A sensory refuge is a space within an overstimulating environment (typically a park, plaza, or public outdoor space) that is deliberately designed to be calm, quiet, and free from anthropogenic sensory stressors. Sensory refuges provide opportunities for self-regulation and recovery from [[sensory-sensitivity-exacerbating-factors|sensory overload]], particularly important for neurodivergent individuals with [[hypersensitivity|hypersensitivities]].

## Design Elements

Effective sensory refuges include:

- **Noise reduction** — Distance from traffic, machinery, or crowds; sound-absorbing materials or water features to mask ambient noise
- **Visual calm** — Reduced artificial lighting, minimal visual complexity, natural color palettes, soft fascination (nature elements)
- **Reduced crowding** — Spacing that prevents overwhelming social presence
- **Natural elements** — [[biophilic-design|Biophilic design]] with greenery, water, natural light, and living materials
- **Therapeutic design** — Incorporating Attention Restoration Theory and stress reduction approaches
- **Clear boundaries** — Recognizable spaces that signal quietness and invite restoration

## Strategic Placement

Study participants emphasized the importance of sensory refuges adjacent to or within overstimulating public spaces, allowing for:

- Easy access during sensory overload
- Brief recovery periods within longer outings
- Balance between participation and protection

## Interstitial Refuge in Public Space

[[stuart-neilson|Stuart Neilson]] identifies sensory refuges in city spaces as **interstitial** — small low-intensity pockets within high-traffic zones, often overlooked by urban designers. His [[motion-intensity-heatmap|motion intensity heatmaps]] make these pockets visible: e.g., a small triangular refuge in Campo Santa Maria Formosa (Venice) surrounded by busy pedestrian flow, where children had organically "decolonised" low-intensity real estate. A critical constraint: a refuge must be reachable via a sufficiently low-intensity route to be usable — a refuge surrounded by sensory ridges becomes isolating. See [[sensory-wayfinding]].
