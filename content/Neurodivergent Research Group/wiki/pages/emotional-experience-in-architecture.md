# Emotional Experience in Architecture

## Summary

Emotional experiences of built environments are affective responses shaped by appraisals of environmental changes relevant to wellbeing. Emotions manifest through multiple channels: experienced feelings, appraisals, expressive behavior, actions, language, and physiological responses.

## Key claims

- Emotional states can be characterized through three dimensions: pleasure-displeasure (valence), arousal (activation level), and dominance-submissiveness (sense of control) — the PAD model [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- The pleasure-displeasure dimension is the most characteristic aspect of emotions, distinguishing positive affective states (tranquility, excitement) from negative ones (anger, boredom) [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Appraisal theory explains emotional response: an event's perceived outcome relative to one's wellbeing determines the emotion; when appraisals change, emotional states change [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Emotions begin quickly, have brief duration, are unbidden (we don't choose them), and associate with specific facial expressions, physiology, and precipitating events [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Davitz's emotional checklist provides 556 descriptors covering physical sensations, perception, cognitive events, social relation, self-relation, behavioral impulses, and temporal qualities of emotions [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]

## Architectural measurement

Environmental experience studies using the Affect Grid (pleasure-displeasure + arousal dimensions) have empirically measured emotional responses to architecture. One empirical study identified relaxation/tranquility as the most intense emotion reported (9.3 of 10), particularly associated with views from elevated vantage points.
