# Psychological Safety in Research

## Summary

Psychological safety in research contexts refers to the shared belief that Community Advisory Board members can take interpersonal risks—such as expressing controversial opinions, sharing lived experiences, or admitting gaps in understanding—without fear of negative consequences. In participatory neurodiversity research, it is essential for fostering open dialogue and honest feedback.

## Key claims

- In institutional settings, participatory research requires explicit creation of psychological safety mechanisms to facilitate open disclosure of personal opinions and experiences, even when views contradict those of others [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- Anonymity between [[community-advisory-board]] members (allowing them to communicate directly with the research team, which then shares back anonymized contributions) can be used to create psychological safety, though research suggests that anonymity alone may impede active contribution and shared ownership [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- Psychological safety can be sustained through cohesive bonds based on strong interpersonal relationships alongside clear communication of expectations, and can support trust and collective action in participatory research [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- Mixed formal and informal communication channels—where board members can reach out informally at any time in addition to scheduled meetings—can fill gaps left by formal participation structures and improve psychological safety [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
