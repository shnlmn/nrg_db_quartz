# Sensory Processing Dysfunction

## Summary

Sensory processing dysfunction refers to atypical patterns in how the nervous system receives, filters, integrates, and responds to sensory input. It can manifest across modalities (auditory, tactile, visual, proprioceptive, vestibular) and patterns (hyper-responsivity/sensitivity, hypo-responsivity/seeking, difficulty filtering or integrating). It is not itself a diagnosis, but rather a behavioral phenotype that frequently co-occurs with autism spectrum disorder, ADHD, anxiety, and developmental coordination disorder.

## Key Claims

- **Sensory differences are not primarily a behavior problem.** A child described as "difficult," refusing to participate, having meltdowns, or avoiding activities may be responding to sensory experiences rather than being oppositional. [[sensory-profile-2|Standardized assessment]] helps distinguish sensory underpinnings from behavioral diagnosis.

- **Multisensory integration is required for adequate functioning.** [[multi-sensory-environment-evaluation|Assessing sensory experience holistically]] (auditory + tactile + visual + proprioceptive) rather than in isolation provides richer understanding of a person's actual sensory experience in environments.

- **Sensory patterns are measurable and guide intervention.** The [[sensory-profile-2|Sensory Profile 2]] and other standardized tools document specific [[sensory-quadrants|patterns of sensory response]] that vary across individuals with the same diagnostic label, enabling personalized intervention planning.
