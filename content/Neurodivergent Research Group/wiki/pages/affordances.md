# Affordances

## Summary

Concept from ecological psychology (Gibson, 2015) describing the **relationship between an environment and an individual**—the possibilities for action that environmental features offer. Affordances are perceived and engaged with differently by each individual based on their abilities, intentions, context, and neurobiology. Central to understanding how environmental design either supports or constrains participation and wellbeing.

## Core Principle

**Affordances = relational concept**, not inherent property of an object or environment:

> An affordance is not a property of an individual or a property of an object. It is a relationship between the organism and the object. — Gibson (1977)

- The same feature affords different possibilities to different individuals
- A swing affords vestibular input and self-regulation for one child; anxiety or fear for another
- A high structure affords climbing and proprioceptive feedback for one child; inaccessible or overwhelming for another

## Implications for Inclusive Design

From [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. (2025)]]:

**Designers must understand affordances through user perspectives**:
- A design feature's affordances are not determined by designer intent but by how users actually perceive and engage with it
- Removing "risky" features (swings, height, sand pits) eliminates essential vestibular and proprioceptive affordances for many neurodivergent children, regardless of safety intentions
- [[design-from-sensorial-margins|Designing from sensorial margins]] (addressing extremes of sensory seeking/avoiding) creates affordances supporting the full spectrum of users

**Central to [[sensory-affordances|sensory affordances]]**:
- [[sensory-affordances|Sensory affordances]] are specific applications of Gibson's concept to environmental sensory possibilities
- [[inclusive-playground-design|Inclusive playground design]] requires understanding how diverse users perceive and engage with design features

## Scale and Context Dependency

Affordances change based on:
- **Individual capacity** — Motor ability, sensory processing, cognitive style
- **Individual needs** — Regulation needs, sensory preferences, social comfort
- **Context** — Environmental conditions, social presence, prior experiences
- **Scale of features** — A structure's affordances depend on its position, height, proximity to other features, and overall layout

Example: A high climbing structure affords different possibilities when positioned adjacent to a quiet observation area (supporting integration of children with different play styles) versus isolated in open space.

## Neurodivergent Perspective

For neurodivergent children with [[sensory-processing-dysfunction|sensory processing differences]]:

- **Essential for regulation** — Vestibular and proprioceptive affordances (swinging, spinning, climbing) support self-regulated arousal and attention
- **Barriers vs. affordances** — Design that ignores neurodivergent sensory needs creates barriers where others perceive affordances
- **Diverse perception** — Neurodivergent children's engagement with features reflects their regulatory needs and sensory processing profiles, not deficits in playground design perception
