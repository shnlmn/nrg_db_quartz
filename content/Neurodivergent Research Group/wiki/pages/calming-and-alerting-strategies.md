# Calming and Alerting Strategies

## Summary

Self-regulation techniques targeting specific [[sensory-preferences]] to modulate arousal level—either reducing overstimulation through calming strategies or increasing engagement through alerting strategies. Strategies are individualized based on sensory profile and environmental context.

## Key claims

- Calming and alerting strategies are matched to individual [[sensory-preferences]] to support regulation, attention focus, and concentration [[raw/NAIT-Guide-to-Assessment-of-Sensory-Preferences-in-Adults-2022.extracted.md]]
- Strategies should be documented during [[sensory-assessment-neurodevelopmental]] as "activities I use to reduce stress/relax" [[raw/NAIT-Guide-to-Assessment-of-Sensory-Preferences-in-Adults-2022.extracted.md]]
- Both approaches address sensory environment mismatch that is associated with poorer outcomes and distress [[raw/NAIT-Guide-to-Assessment-of-Sensory-Preferences-in-Adults-2022.extracted.md]]

## Calming strategies

Techniques to reduce stimulation and promote [[sensory-refuge]]:

- Pressure: weighted blanket or deep pressure application
- Oral: sucking a sweet, mints, herbal tea
- Movement: walking, gentle stretching, rocking
- Touch: massage, stroking a pet
- Scent: lavender, essential oils
- Environment: breaks from stimulation, subdued lighting, headphones
- Proprioceptive: using stairs instead of escalators, sunglasses, closing eyes

[[raw/NAIT-Guide-to-Assessment-of-Sensory-Preferences-in-Adults-2022.extracted.md]]

## Alerting strategies

Techniques to increase stimulation and promote engagement/wakefulness:

- Movement: jogging, running, jumping jacks, push-ups
- Proprioceptive: physical exertion, use of muscles
- Oral: crunchy foods, cold drinks
- Auditory: music, upbeat sounds
- Environment: movement/activity, busy settings, bright lighting
- Social: group activities
- Scent: perfume or strong scents

## Individualization

Effective strategies are:
- Tailored to the person's specific [[sensory-preferences]]
- Matched to the [[sensory-processing-autism|neurotype]] and context
- Documented and practiced as part of intervention planning
- Adjusted over time as preferences and environments change
