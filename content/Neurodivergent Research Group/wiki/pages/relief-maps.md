# Relief Maps

## Summary

Relief Maps is a free-access digital tool for collecting and visualising data on the emotional dimension of inequalities, showing how places are differently experienced depending on one's social positions. Developed by [[maria-rodo-zarate]] during doctoral research in feminist geographies. The name invokes two meanings: 'relief' as accentuation (hills = places of oppression/discomfort) and 'relief' as alleviation (valleys = places of comfort or safety).

## Key claims

- Connects three dimensions: social (social structures), psychological (emotions), and geographical (physical spaces) [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Participants self-identify social categories (e.g., gender, age, religious identity, ethnicity, class) and rate comfort/discomfort across named places [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Collects quantitative data (comfort grade per place/axis) and qualitative data (comments and emotions per data point) for further analysis [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Visual output: a graph with comfort–discomfort on the vertical axis and spaces on the horizontal axis, disaggregated by social category [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Applied in studies of young lesbians in public space, women and mobility, cyclo-activist narratives, LGBTI discrimination in cities, and university campus experiences [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Distancing from essentialist categorisation: power structures and identity categories conceived as non-rigid and non-stable, focus on material repercussions of inequality [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- When place is central to analysis, experience is situated in specific context, avoiding universalisation [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]
- Can function as a consciousness-raising intervention when used collectively, making normalised discriminations visible [[raw/Relief Maps_ a tool for understanding intersectional experiences of place.md]]

## Method

- Researcher creates a project on the platform and shares it with participants
- Participants choose their social categories and rate their subjective comfort/discomfort for each combination of place and social axis
- Qualitative comments and emotions are linked to each data point
- Flexible: any place typology (individual street to large district), any social categories, any parameters of lived experience
- Quantitative output allows cross-group comparison (e.g., who feels afraid in what spaces and for what reason)

## Related applications

- **Situa App** (in development as of 2021): follows work of Mei Po Kwan on feminist cartographies; uses GIS for spatial analysis of discriminations in Barcelona
- **Your Ground** (Monash University XYX Lab and CrowdSpot): comparable tool for marking safe and unsafe spots across Australia; demonstrates an alternative technique for capturing lived experience of place
