# Participatory Action Research

## Summary

Participatory action research (PAR) is a research methodology in which researchers and participants undertake collective, self-reflective inquiry to understand and improve the practices and situations in which they find themselves. The reflective process is directly linked to action, influenced by understanding of history, culture, and local context, and embedded in social relationships.

## Key claims

- PAR differs from conventional research in three ways: (1) it focuses on research whose purpose is to enable action through an iterative reflective cycle of data collection, reflection, and action; (2) it deliberately shares power between researcher and researched, blurring the line until the researched become the researchers; (3) it avoids removing data from their contexts, advocating active involvement of those being researched [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- PAR has antecedents in questioning the nature of knowledge and the extent to which knowledge can reinforce the positions of the powerful in society [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- [[paulo-freire]] used PAR to encourage poor and deprived communities to examine and analyse the structural reasons for their oppression; his concept of praxis holds that action and reflection are indissolubly united [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- PAR posits that the observer has an impact on the phenomena being observed and brings a set of values that exert influence on the study, contrasting with positivist science's assumption of objective, independent observation [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- PAR draws on the paradigms of critical theory and constructivism and may use a range of qualitative and quantitative methods; methodological pluralism is now accepted as essential for effective public health research [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- In the 21st century PAR is increasingly used in health research; initially mainly in low-income countries for needs assessment and health service planning, more recently in rich countries including mental health research and empowerment evaluation [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- PAR is increasingly recognised as useful in Indigenous health research, with the potential to reduce the colonising effects of conventional research by avoiding exploitation, ensuring community control, specifying benefits, and preventing misrepresentation [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- [[empowerment-in-research|Empowerment]] is a crucial underpinning concept: Labonte conceptualises it as a shifting quality of power relations tending towards equity by reducing inequalities in access to resources [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Foucault's view of power as resulting from interactions between people, institutional practices, and forms of knowledge is particularly relevant; PAR challenges the system of surveillance and knowledge control established through mainstream research [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- [[critical-reflection]] is central to PAR: Freire's praxis holds that reflection without action is "sheer verbalism" and action without reflection is "pure activism"; through praxis, critical consciousness develops leading to further transformative action [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- PAR draws on [[lived-experience]] and phenomenological traditions that argue humans cannot describe an object in isolation from the conscious being experiencing it, uniting subject and object [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- Academic dilemmas with PAR include that it is time-consuming, unpredictable, unlikely to produce high journal output, and less likely to attract competitive research funding; acceptance requires institutional change in how research performance is judged [[raw/Participatory action research.md|Baum et al.]]
- A comprehensive methods primer identifies six building blocks for PAR project design: (1) building relationships; (2) establishing working practices; (3) establishing a common understanding of the issue; (4) observing, gathering and generating materials; (5) [[collaborative-analysis]]; (6) planning and taking action [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- Key challenges: mismatches with institutional research infrastructure; risks of [[co-option-in-research|co-option]]; power inequalities; and the decentralizing of control [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- Counter-strategies: build PAR-friendly networks; cultivate a critical community for accountability; use critical [[reflexivity]]; redistribute powers; learn to trust the process [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- PAR's societal contribution can best be advanced by engaging with contemporary social movements demanding redress of inequities and recognition of situated expertise [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- PAR draws on intellectual traditions including Freire, decolonization studies (Smith, Chilisa, Fanon), intersectionality (Crenshaw), feminist scholarship (Mohanty, Mama), and [[action-research]] (Lewin) [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- PAR and action research provide significant inspirations and broad methodological grounding for [[scandinavian-participatory-design|Scandinavian participatory design]] practices, particularly in emphases on [[mutual-learning]] and reciprocity in relationships [[raw/IJEE1353.extracted.md|Gregory 2003]]
- PAR's "northern tradition" traces to Kurt Lewin's [[action-research]] (1940s), while the "southern tradition" traces to Orlando Fals Borda's participatory research conference (late 1970s) and [[paulo-freire]]'s liberation pedagogy; CBPR emerged from the convergence of both traditions [[raw/Community-based Participatory Research (CBPR)_ Towards Equitable Involvement of Community in Psychology Research.md|Collins et al.]]
- [[relational-ethics]] in PAR situates ethical conduct as ongoingly negotiated within respectful relationships, beyond procedural rules set out by ethics committees [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- [[extractive-research]] — where research exploits relationships and depletes resources at research sites — is what PAR explicitly seeks to counter [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
