# Functioning Labels

## Summary

Functioning labels ("high-functioning" / "low-functioning") are contested descriptors applied to autistic people that reduce fluctuating, multidimensional abilities to a single binary. Autistic self-advocates and researchers argue they are harmful because they either dismiss support needs or deny strengths, and because autistic functioning varies substantially across contexts, mood, time, and situational demands.

## Key claims

- "High-functioning" can be used to dismiss struggles: "it can be used in a way to diminish their struggles, or suggest we don't have it as hard as typically considered 'low-functioning' autistics. But that's not true." (Flynn 2018, cited in Svaler 2023) [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- "Low-functioning" removes humanity: "The low-functioning label dismisses what those individuals are good at. It takes away some of their humanity, and it can make people less willing to help them achieve more." (Flynn 2018) [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Functioning is not fixed: "Our function at any given day, on any given week, or month, or year, fluctuates depending on the many variables and contextual elements of our lives." (Heyworth 2021, cited in Svaler 2023) [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Labels are based on external observer perception, not internal experience; they place a fluctuating condition into fixed boxes. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Functioning level correlates with sociocultural experience, fatigue, stress, and environmental context — not intrinsic capacity. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Asperger's syndrome is being phased out and removed from DSM-5 diagnostic criteria; similarly, functioning labels tied to this concept are losing formal standing. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]

## Why labels persist

- Clinicians and institutions often need shorthand for service allocation
- Media portrayal of autism uses functioning language heavily
- Labels reflect neurotypical observer perspective on visible behavior, not autistic experience

## Preferred alternatives

- Describe specific support needs in specific domains rather than assigning global labels
- Acknowledge intra-individual variability; avoid assuming stable trait profiles
- Use [[identity-first-language]] and focus on individual needs rather than categorical position on a "severity" spectrum
