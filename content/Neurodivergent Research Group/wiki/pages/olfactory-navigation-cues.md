# Olfactory Navigation Cues

## Summary

Smell landmarks that help people with visual impairment navigate and confirm their location in public spaces. Distinct odors create recognizable, memorable markers for specific areas or transitions, supporting orientation and wayfinding.

## Key cues

### Place-Based Smells
- Distinctive odors associated with specific locations (bakery, coffee shop, restroom deodorizers)
- Create distinct landmarks for particular areas
- Help verify location or validate travel path [[jenkins-et-al-2015-multisensory-visual-impairment]]

### Environmental Odors
- Bathroom deodorizers, industrial smells, natural smells
- Serve as consistent wayfinding references when distinct and recognizable [[jenkins-et-al-2015-multisensory-visual-impairment]]

### Thermal/Airflow-Associated Odors
- Steam coming from sidewalk grates
- Hot air from building vents
- Often combined with temperature cues for additional orientation information [[thermal-navigation-cues]], [[jenkins-et-al-2015-multisensory-visual-impairment]]

## Integration with other senses

Olfactory cues are most effective when:
- Distinct and recognizable across visits
- Consistent in location and timing
- Combined with other sensory modalities (auditory, tactile, thermal) to validate direction [[context-dependent-sensory-effectiveness]]

## Design considerations

Research suggests that odor landmarks are underutilized in built environment design despite being reliable and memorable navigation aids for people with visual impairment. Buildings typically control or eliminate odors rather than strategically employing them as wayfinding features.
