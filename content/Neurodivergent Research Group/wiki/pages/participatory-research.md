# Participatory Research

## Summary

Participatory research in the neurodiversity context means including neurodivergent people as co-researchers, collaborators, and decision-makers in research about neurodivergence—not merely as subjects. This approach aligns with the disability rights principle "Nothing about us, without us." It draws on the broader tradition of [[participatory-action-research]] (PAR), which originated in development and public health contexts as a methodology enabling researchers to work in partnership with communities in a manner that leads to action for change.

## Key claims

- Participatory models like PAR redistribute power and capture neurodivergent voices in authentic ways; [[neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-model|NCTIM]] complements participatory approaches by serving as a preparatory tool to adapt research settings before participatory models are implemented, ensuring the space is both accessible and affirming from the outset [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Bernard et al. argue that neurodiversity research conducted without neurodivergent collaborators risks reproducing harmful assumptions and terminology; neurodivergent researchers are available and should be sought out for collaboration [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al.]]
- The authors prefer "neurodivergent" over "neuroatypical" because "neuroatypical" differs from "neurotypical" by only one letter, which is a barrier for people with dyslexia — one neurodivergent author did not initially realize the focal article was about neuroatypicality rather than neurotypicality [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al.]]
- Neurodivergent team members commonly mask identity-stereotypical behaviors to increase acceptance, taking a toll on personal resources; neurotypical team members should actively reduce the need to assimilate; team members should practice [[reflexivity]] on how their standpoints influence both the research and the collaboration [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al.]]
- Applying the [[canary-code]] to research collaborations means: collectively determining a detailed project completion plan (focusing on task completion over execution style); flexible time, place, and work-style arrangements; discussing task assignments, methods, and authorship decisions in advance; inclusive information sharing; and transparent compensation (grants, authorship order) based on performance outcomes rather than implicit interpersonal politics [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al.]]
- Per APA guidelines, reviewing a manuscript for final approval constitutes a substantial contribution; community members who review manuscripts may qualify for authorship; where authorship is not desired, other compensation is owed for their expertise and time [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al.]]
- Inclusion strategies for neurodiverse research teams and supplemental methods of neurodivergent community control over research are essential ethical considerations [[raw/Not your 'typical' research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al.]]
- The NDAQ development team included autistic researchers and parents of autistic children involved in all aspects of the study, demonstrating participatory principles in instrument development [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- Tim Smyth, a neurodivergent researcher at Salesforce, used his own lived experience with ASD and ADHD to design more inclusive research methods [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Neurodivergent people designing research bring unique strengths: "We've been thinking about this our whole lives, wondering what other people are thinking about" [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Article 9 of the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) requires ensuring access to information and communications technologies on an equal basis [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]

- Vaughn & Jacquez define PR as an umbrella term for research designs, methods, and frameworks that use systematic inquiry in direct collaboration with those affected by an issue for the purpose of action or change; they catalogue 25+ PR frameworks across disciplines sharing the value of doing research *with* rather than *on* people [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- The [[vaughn-jacquez-2020-choice-points|choice points model]] identifies decision junctures at each step of the research cycle where partnerships must decide which tools and methods will produce the desired level of participation; there is no prescription for the "right" way — partners must collaborate to choose methods maximizing stakeholder interests and real-world impact [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- The [[participation-continuum]] ranges from *inform* (providing information) through *consult*, *involve*, *collaborate*, to *empower* (community-led decision-making); the same method can operate at different levels depending on implementation [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- Conventional methods like surveys and focus groups are not off the table in PR — they can be adapted to be participatory if chosen and utilized collaboratively; the distinguishing feature is stakeholder power in decision-making and implementation [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]
- Explicit description of participatory methods, tools, and processes along with documentation of challenges and facilitators is often minimal to non-existent in the literature, weakening PR's broader impact [[raw/Participatory Research Methods – Choice Points in the Research Process _ Published in Journal of Participatory Research Methods.md|Vaughn & Jacquez]]

- A 7-member [[community-advisory-board]] composed of neurodivergent students (representing ADHD, ASD, dyslexia, and dyspraxia) that used a [[co-design-in-research]] approach to review and improve all aspects of a [[focus-groups]] study led to: more accessible materials (improved formatting, online-adjustable documents, clearer definitions), faster ethical approval, and likely improved recruitment rates [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- The three phases of co-design can include: partnership formation (recruiting and defining expectations), iterative development with multiple rounds of feedback, and outcome reporting with a "transparency report" detailing which feedback was acted upon and why [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- In the Le Cunff et al. study, neurodivergent CAB members identified blind spots the research team had missed; for example, dyslexic members identified formatting issues that would impair accessibility [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- While CAB members reported significant benefits (sense of representation and inclusion, understanding of the research process, better self-understanding of neurodiversity), they also faced challenges: difficulty articulating their own neurodiversity, maintaining communication, and finding time to engage [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
