# Sensory Zoning

## Summary

Sensory zoning is a spatial design strategy within the [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework]] that creates diversified landscape areas with varied [[sensory-affordances|sensory affordances]], allowing users with different sensory needs to choose appropriate experiences. Rather than a one-size-fits-all design, sensory zoning provides implicit adaptability and responds to the multi-sensory environment through designated zones serving different sensory profiles.

## Design Rationale

Outdoor built environments are less flexible and personalizable than interior spaces, making a uniform solution infeasible. Sensory zoning addresses this constraint by offering multiple options within a single site, accommodating both [[hypersensitivity|hypersensitive]] and [[hyposensitivity|hyposensitive]] users simultaneously.

## Zone Types

A sensory-responsive park or landscape might include:

1. **Sensory-Seeking Zones** — Areas with multi-generational playgrounds, swings, varied terrain, and high sensory stimulation for [[hyposensitivity|hyposensitive]] users
2. **Sensory Refuge Zones** — Calm areas away from noise pollution, artificial stressors, and overcrowding, supporting [[sensory-refuge|sensory refuge]] needs
3. **Transitional Zones** — Areas with moderate sensory conditions and variety, accommodating users at mid-range sensory thresholds
4. **Nature-Based Zones** — Areas emphasizing [[biophilic-design|biophilic elements]], natural materials, water features, and greenery

## Implementation

Effective sensory zoning involves:

- Diversified landscape types and [[sensory-affordances|sensory affordances]]
- Clear wayfinding to help users identify appropriate zones
- [[design-from-sensorial-margins|Design from sensorial margins]] to ensure all zones serve their intended users
- Flexibility to accommodate evolving sensory needs
