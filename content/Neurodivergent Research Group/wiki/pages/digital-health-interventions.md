# Digital Health Interventions

## Summary

Health interventions delivered through digital tools or communication technologies that collect, store, share, and analyze health information to improve patient health and healthcare delivery. The umbrella term encompasses wearable devices, mobile apps, texting, telehealth, virtual gaming, virtual reality, chatbots, remote monitoring, and other digital modalities.

## Key claims

- Digital health interventions have been associated with positive changes in disease self-management, clinical outcomes, and quality of life across populations including young adults, pediatrics, and older adults [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- The number and popularity of digital health interventions has rapidly grown over the past decade due to increased interest in healthcare digitalization and its potential to improve access to personalized care at lower costs [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Despite successes, challenges include rarity of rigorous scientific evaluation, lack of adoption by end-users, and poor integration into routine clinical care [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Lack of end-user involvement in design is a key contributor to limited intervention use, adversely affecting health outcomes and sustained practice integration [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Reviews used varied terminology: digital health (n=6), mobile health/mHealth (n=6), electronic health/eHealth (n=3), serious digital gaming, information communication technologies, assistive technology, technology-based interventions, and health-related technology [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Most commonly targeted individuals with chronic conditions (n=8) or a combination of chronic conditions and health promotion (n=8) [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
- Not embedding equity and accessibility principles in co-design of digital health interventions risks worsening the digital divide, particularly for older adults, young children, and those with low health or digital literacy [[raw/An umbrella review on how digital health intervention co-design is conducted and described - npj Digital Medicine.md|Kilfoy et al.]]
