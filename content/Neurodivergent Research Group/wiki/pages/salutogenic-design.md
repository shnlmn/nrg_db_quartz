# Salutogenic Design

## Summary

Design approach that emphasizes health-promoting features, assets, strengths, and resources rather than deficit-focused risk management. Salutogenic design intentionally creates [[affordances|affordances]] supporting positive wellbeing, emotional regulation, and engagement. Derived from Antonovsky's salutogenesis concept—a stress-resource model orienting toward health generation rather than disease prevention.

## Theoretical Foundation

**Salutogenesis** (Antonovsky, 1987) conceptualizes health as a dynamic resource in response to stress, rather than an absence of disease. The salutogenic approach asks: "What generates health?" rather than "What causes disease?"

In environmental design, salutogenic principles translate to:
- Creating sensory affordances supporting self-regulation and positive emotional states
- Designing for meaning, coherence, and manageability (sense of comprehensibility)
- Integrating nature, social connection, and opportunities for mastery and control

## Salutogenic Design in Neurodivergent Contexts

From [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. 2025]], salutogenic design principles support neurodivergent children through:

- **Self-regulation affordances** — Features like swinging, spinning, and climbing providing vestibular and proprioceptive feedback enabling sustained attention and emotional balance through neurotransmitter release

- **Extended engagement** — Once neurodivergent children feel welcomed through sensory-responsive [[affordances|affordances]], they stay longer, make positive social connections, and experience improved wellbeing

- **Choice and autonomy** — Environments offering multiple participation modes (active play, quiet observation, nature engagement) support children's sense of control and reduce anxiety

- **Nature integration** — [[biophilic-design|Biophilic elements]] restore attention, reduce anxiety, and provide restorative benefits contributing to overall health trajectories

## Contrasts with Risk-Averse Design

Traditional institutional approaches to playground safety prioritize risk elimination (removing swings, height, challenge). Salutogenic design reframes risk elements as health-generating when appropriately managed:

- **Risky play supports development** — Controlled challenge, fall risk, and physical risk stimulate resilience, confidence, behavioral development, and skill-building
- **Risk assessment capacity** — Children demonstrate ability to identify safety concerns and suggest improvements, affirming their role as competent contributors
- **Behavioral benefits** — Schools implementing "loose parts and nature play" with controlled risk (tree climbing, firepits) report reduced negative behaviors and improved social development

## Integration with Other Frameworks

Salutogenic design aligns with and reinforces:

- **[[affordances|Affordance Theory]]** — How environmental features generate specific action and regulatory possibilities
- **[[biophilic-design|Biophilic Design]]** — Nature-based approaches supporting health and wellbeing
- **[[prospect-refuge|Prospect-Refuge Theory]]** — Psychological safety through observation and protection
- **[[social-model-of-disability|Social Model of Disability]]** — Environmental barriers (not individual difference) as sources of exclusion; responsive design addresses barriers to wellbeing
