# Hypersensitivity

## Summary

Hypersensitivity (also termed over-responsiveness) is heightened or excessive responsiveness to sensory stimuli, where individuals experience sensations as more intense, aversive, or overwhelming than neurotypical individuals. In neurodivergent populations (autism, ADHD, dyslexia), hypersensitivity spans all eight sensory modalities and frequently manifests as sensory-avoidant behaviors.

## Manifestations

Hypersensitivity patterns in neurodivergent individuals include:

- **Visual** — Difficulty with artificial lighting, fluorescent lights, bright colors, visual patterns, or flicker
- **Auditory** — Aversion to loud or sudden sounds, difficulty filtering background noise (acoustic environments noted as most impactful in [[sensory-processing-dysfunction]])
- **Olfactory** — Strong negative reactions to smells, scents, or air quality
- **Tactile** — Aversion to certain textures, clothing sensations, or physical touch
- **Vestibular/Proprioceptive** — Discomfort with movement, balance challenges, or spatial awareness difficulties
- **Interoceptive** — Heightened awareness of internal bodily sensations (hunger, pain, temperature)

## Environmental Barriers

In outdoor built environments, hypersensitive neurodivergent individuals commonly encounter:

- Anthropogenic sensory stressors (vehicle noise, machinery, air pollution)
- Artificial lighting and visual complexity
- Lack of [[sensory-refuge|sensory refuge]] spaces for self-regulation
- Limited options for sensory choice
- Overstimulating public spaces

## Contrast with Hyposensitivity

While [[hyposensitivity]] involves reduced sensory responsiveness and sensory-seeking behaviors, hypersensitivity involves heightened responsiveness and sensory-avoidant behaviors. Individuals often experience both hyper- and hypo-sensitivities across different sensory modalities simultaneously or at different times ([[sensory-stacking]]).
