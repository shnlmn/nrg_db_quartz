# Strength-Based Approach Autism Design

## Summary

A strength-based approach to autism and the built environment reframes autistic individuals as bringing strengths, abilities, insights, and alternate perspectives — not deficits requiring remediation. In design practice this means treating autistic users as expert informants and design partners rather than as passive beneficiaries of charitable accommodation.

## Key Claims

- Moving away from inclusive design as "a charitable act for individuals" toward a "more respectful strength-based approach" is among the biggest challenges in autism design [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- A strength-based stance is "more attuned with the social model of autism" and acknowledges that autistic individuals "come with strengths, abilities, insights, and alternate perspectives" they contribute to home, learning, workplace, or public space environments [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- The designer-as-expert model should be flipped: "I always try to approach my work not from an expertise position, because I don't think anyone can claim expertise until they've had the lived experience" — Mostafa (2021) [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- Autistic individuals are the true experts on autistic experience; design professionals contribute architectural tools to codify autistic insight into built environment form [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- The autism spectrum implies deep heterogeneity: "if you've met one person with autism, you've met one person with autism" — no single autistic profile should guide design [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]

## Contrast with Deficit-Based Framing

Deficit-based or medical-model framing positions autism as a disorder to be mitigated by design; the environment is modified to compensate for the "problem" of the autistic person. Strength-based framing inverts this: the "problem" lies in disabling built environments, not the person. This aligns with the [[social-model-of-disability]] and the [[neurodiversity-paradigm]].

## Design Process Implications

Strength-based design practice requires:
- Autistic self-advocacy groups as primary design clients and stakeholders
- Co-design processes where autistic participants lead problem identification
- Research methods accessible to diverse autistic communication styles (see [[inclusive-research-methods]])
- Humility framing in workshops: "I am here to hear from, learn from, and understand the autistic perspective" [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
