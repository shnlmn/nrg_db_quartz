# Visual Supports

## Summary

Visual supports are pictures, photographs, objects, or words used to support a student, such as visual schedules, timers, task breakdowns, feelings charts, or first/then boards. They are often not considered AAC on their own unless accompanied by a communicative behavior (e.g., pointing to or handing over the visual), but they are frequently used alongside AAC systems and can support internal language — such as talking oneself through a daily schedule — making them a strong support for people with executive-functioning difficulties.

## Key claims

- Visual supports include visual schedules, timers, task breakdowns, feelings charts, and first/then boards. [[raw/AAC_and_Autism_Tip_Sheet_Edu.extracted.md]]
- Visual supports alone are often not considered AAC unless accompanied by other communicative behavior (e.g., pointing to the visual, handing it to another person). [[raw/AAC_and_Autism_Tip_Sheet_Edu.extracted.md]]
- Visual supports can support internal language (e.g., talking to oneself through a daily schedule), making them a strong support for those with [[executive-functioning]] difficulties. [[raw/AAC_and_Autism_Tip_Sheet_Edu.extracted.md]]
