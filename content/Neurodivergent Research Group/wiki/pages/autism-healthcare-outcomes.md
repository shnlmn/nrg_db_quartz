# Autism Healthcare Outcomes

## Summary

Autistic individuals experience measurably worse physical health outcomes than the general population — shorter lifespans, higher mortality rates, and longer illness durations. Emerging evidence implicates healthcare space design as a contributing factor: anxiety triggered by clinical environments may deter autistic people from seeking or accessing care.

## Key Claims

- Autistic individuals have shorter lifespans and higher mortality rates than comparable non-autistic populations [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- Autistic individuals present with more complicated medical problems and get sicker for longer than peers in other average groups [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- Emerging evidence links these poor outcomes to anxiety caused by healthcare spaces: "access to healthcare and anxiety for autistic individuals of being in healthcare spaces" is part of the problem behind poor health outcomes [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- Mostafa (2021) identifies the healthcare sector as the most under-served by autism-friendly design, despite high urgency [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- Autism-friendly healthcare design is proposed as a partial solution: if clinical spaces cause less anxiety, some of the access and outcome gap can be "resolved through design" [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]

## Design Implication

Healthcare facilities are a priority application domain for [[autism-friendly-design]] and [[aspectss-design-index|ASPECTSS]] principles. Reducing sensory overwhelm, increasing predictability, and providing escape/refuge areas in clinical settings could lower pre-appointment anxiety and improve healthcare engagement.
