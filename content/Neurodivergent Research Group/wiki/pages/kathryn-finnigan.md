# Kathryn Angela Finnigan

## Summary

Kathryn Angela Finnigan is a researcher and landscape architect at the University of Colorado, Denver, College of Architecture and Planning. She conducted a landmark qualitative study exploring the perceived relationships between outdoor built environments and sensory sensitivities in neurodivergent individuals, resulting in the development of the [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework]] (SREF™).

## Research Focus

**Dissertation (2023):** *Neuro-Inclusive Spaces: Sensory-Responsive Environments*

**Published Article (2024):** "Sensory Responsive Environments: A Qualitative Study on Perceived Relationships between Outdoor Built Environments and Sensory Sensitivities" — *Land* Journal, Vol. 13, Article 636

## Methodology

Finnigan's research employed:

- **Approach** — Phenomenological, participatory action research (PAR), inductive emic approach
- **Sample** — 31 semi-structured interviews with participants having lived experience with autism, ADHD, dyslexia (firsthand, secondhand, professional perspectives)
- **Geography** — Colorado-based with participants from 7 US states
- **Analysis** — Thematic narrative analysis with member checks and interdisciplinary peer review

## Key Contributions

1. **Sensory Responsive Environments Framework (SREF™)** — Theoretical framework centered on [[design-from-sensorial-margins|DFSM]], [[sensory-zoning]], [[biophilic-design|nature-based approaches]], and [[co-design-in-research|co-design]]
2. **The Sensorial Prism™** — Visualization of non-dichotomous sensory spectrum across eight sensory systems
3. **Outdoor environment focus** — First comprehensive exploration of sensory sensitivities in designed outdoor (not indoor) built environments
4. **Multi-neurotype approach** — Integration of autism, ADHD, and dyslexia in single framework
5. **Adult representation** — Centering neurodivergent adults (not solely children)

## Dedication

The research honors Matthew Paul Finnigan (Starblade), a queer E2 autistic individual who aimed to uncover patterns linking biological and environmental factors to improving experiences for autistic individuals.
