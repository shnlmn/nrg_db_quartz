# Motion Intensity Heatmap

## Summary

A motion intensity heatmap is a visual map of movement patterns across a public space, generated by accumulating pixel-level changes across video frames. Developed and applied by [[stuart-neilson|Stuart Neilson]] as a method for identifying sensory refuges, busy pedestrian paths, and navigation routes in city spaces — motivated by his autistic experience of difficulty locating himself relative to crowds.

## Method

The map is produced by summing the absolute change at each pixel position across a video sequence (typically 20 minutes). The result is a spatial distribution of motion intensity across the visual field.

**Color coding:**
- **Red** — high intensity (busy paths, dense pedestrian flow)
- **Orange / green** — intermediate intensity (transition zones)
- **Blue** — low intensity (sky, building facades, potential sensory refuge)

Most pixel changes are caused by pedestrians or vehicles; non-movement changes (flashing lights, flags) introduce minor noise but the overall map remains a reasonable approximation of footfall intensity.

## Locations Applied

Neilson has produced heatmaps from both self-recorded video and live-streamed feeds:

- **Cork City** — Grand Parade, St Patrick's Quay, Paul Street (self-recorded)
- **Dublin** — Aston Quay; DCU Glasnevin Campus (self-recorded from car-park roof)
- **Milan** — Il Duomo (streamed)
- **Venice** — Campo Santa Maria Formosa, Rialto Bridge (streamed and self-recorded)
- **Tokyo** — Dotonbori (streamed)
- **London** — King's Cross Station, Trafalgar Square (streamed)
- **Oxford** — Broad Street (streamed)
- **New York** — Times Square (streamed)
- **Heraklion** — Eleftheriou Venizelou Square / Lions Square (streamed)

Three locations (DCU, Rialto Bridge, Lions Square Heraklion) were selected by [[magda-mostafa|Magda Mostafa]] for the wall projection in "A Case for Sensory Decolonisation: Autistic Escape" at the 2023 Venice Architecture Biennale.

## Tactile Extension (CNC)

Neilson translates heatmaps into physical tactile objects using a small CNC cutter, cutting the intensity contours into cardboard, aluminium, perspex, MDF, and recycled plastics (roughly A4 scale). A clear Perspex overlay cut with the intensity map placed over a photograph makes it possible to simultaneously see and feel the landscape of movement. MDF was chosen for the Venice Biennale installation panels as it accepts CNC cutting easily and provides a pale surface suitable for video projection.

## Semimaginary Terrain

Neilson extends the heatmap metaphor into a semimaginary topographic map, treating motion intensity as elevation:

- Contour lines connect areas of equal motion intensity (analogous to elevation contours)
- Low-intensity areas become water (lakes, rivers, sea) — representing flight-lines away from crowds
- High-intensity areas become mountain ridges — barriers disconnecting sensory refuge from desired destinations
- Imaginary roads follow minimum-gradient paths, parallel to high-intensity routes, merging gently to avoid conflict

This semimaginary terrain makes visible a key problem: **rivers do not flow over mountains** — a flight path into sensory refuge may be isolating if no safe route connects back to desired destinations. The imaginary roads represent potential solutions minimising discomfort while retaining full urban access.
