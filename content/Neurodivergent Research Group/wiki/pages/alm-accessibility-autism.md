# ALM Accessibility for Autism

## Summary

The challenge of making Archives, Libraries, and Museums (ALM institutions) accessible to autistic users encompasses physical sensory environments, social interaction demands, information access, and institutional attitudes. Research in this area grew substantially after 2013 but remains underdeveloped relative to physical disability access.

## Key claims

- Research on autism + libraries is sparse but growing: Web of Science search returns 32 articles 2013–2023, zero before 2013; 44% published 2020–2023. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Prior research is skewed toward parent/faculty perspectives rather than autistic people's own experiences (Shea & Derry 2019). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Museums have made "terrific strides" for physical disabilities but developmental disabilities are frequently overlooked (Freed-Brown 2010). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Libraries serve autistic users in a distinctive role: sensory escape, pursuit of special interests, and academic support (Anderson 2021). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Access is a legal obligation under the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (Article 21) and Norway's Equality and Anti-Discrimination Act (Section 18). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- "Invisible" disabilities like autism receive less institutional support than "visible" ones (Roberson et al. 2022). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]

## Main barrier categories

### Sensory
- Fluorescent/flickering lighting, unexpected sounds, crowd noise, temperature variation
- Sensory overload leading to anxiety, stress, fatigue

### Social/interactional
- Mandatory face-to-face reference desk interactions
- Unpredictable social encounters; crowding; fear of being misunderstood

### Navigational/executive
- Difficulty navigating physical space without clear signage
- Executive functioning demands for planning library/museum visit
- Unfamiliarity triggering anxiety; no pre-visit information available

### Institutional/attitudinal
- Lack of staff training in autism
- Insufficient or absent accessibility documentation
- Needs perceived as "dramatic and unfair" (Roberson et al. 2022)
