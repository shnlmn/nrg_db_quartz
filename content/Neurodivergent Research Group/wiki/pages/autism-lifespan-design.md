# Autism Lifespan Design

## Summary

Autism lifespan design is the recognition that autistic individuals require built environment support across all life stages — not only in childhood and K-12 schooling — and that design and advocacy infrastructure must expand accordingly. The concept emerged as autistic adults, who were largely invisible to the design profession, became a more prominent self-advocacy voice.

## Design Wave Sequence

[[magda-mostafa|Magda Mostafa]] describes a historical sequence of design demand she observed across her practice [[raw/When We Design for Autism, We Design for Everyone]]:

| Wave | Setting | Status (as of 2023) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | K-12 schools | Well-established — earliest client requests, most published frameworks |
| 2 | Higher education | Emerging — [[aspectss-design-index|ASPECTSS]]-informed Autism Friendly University Design Guide for Dublin City University |
| 3 | Workplace | Active — workplace projects beginning at time of interview |
| 4 | Assisted living / senior housing for autistic aging | Anticipated — not yet built out |

## Context

The peak autism diagnosis cohort of ~2002 reached adulthood with more awareness and support than previous generations. These individuals are now entering higher education and the workforce without the design infrastructure existing for K-12 settings. Mostafa quotes a parent: "Everyone's talking to me as if my son's going to evaporate at the age of 18." [[raw/When We Design for Autism, We Design for Everyone]]

## Advocacy Gap

A critical observation: two autistic women working in autism advocacy in the UK — in their twenties — told Mostafa after a panel presentation that it had never occurred to them they had the right to ask for built environment accommodations, in the same way colleagues using wheelchairs or hearing aids do. This reflects how the design profession has historically communicated autistic design rights only in relation to children's school environments [[raw/When We Design for Autism, We Design for Everyone]].

## Design Implications

- Autistic adults are a distinct user group with distinct needs from autistic children — e.g., workplace sensory environment, public transport access, housing design
- Existing frameworks (ASPECTSS, SREF) were primarily developed for children's educational settings
- Self-advocacy groups are the appropriate design clients for adult settings — DCU guide originated from an autistic student self-advocacy group
- Burnout is relevant to adult autistic experience; sensory environment design interacts with autistic burnout risk
