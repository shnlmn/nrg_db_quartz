# Soundwalk

## Summary

A soundwalk is a qualitative research procedure in which researchers walk alongside study participants through a location to listen to, observe, and describe environmental sounds and acoustic experiences. Soundwalks are used to profile and characterise the soundscape — the acoustic environment as perceived and experienced by people in a place.

## Key claims

- A soundwalk is a procedure where the researcher walks with participants in a target place to listen and describe environmental sounds for research purposes [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Soundscape is defined as "the acoustic environment perceived or experienced by a person or people in a place (influenced by its context)" per ISO 12913-1:2014 [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Soundwalks have been used to profile the soundscape of various cities and urban environments [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Auditory cues in places (music, noise, natural sounds, background sounds) influence purchase intentions, behaviour (e.g., eating rate), and product choice and experience [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Natural and human-related sounds (birds, human voices, wind, water) are typically evaluated as positive in urban environments, while traffic and ventilation are experienced as negative [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- The appropriateness and liking of the sonic atmosphere positively correlates with the overall pleasantness of experiences (e.g., eating) in a place [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
