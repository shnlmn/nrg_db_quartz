# Biophilic Design

## Summary

Biophilic design is an approach that incorporates nature-based elements, natural materials, living systems, and natural processes into built environments to support human health, well-being, and sensory engagement. Within [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework|SREF]], biophilic design is identified as a key principle supporting neurodivergent individuals through multi-sensory nature experiences.

## Theoretical Foundations

Finnigan (2024) identifies biophilic design as informed by:

- **Wilson's Biophilia Hypothesis** — Innate human affinity for natural elements
- **Ulrich's Stress Reduction Theory** — Nature exposure reducing psychological stress and physiological arousal
- **Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan & Kaplan)** — Natural environments restoring directed attention and mental fatigue
- **Prospect-Refuge Theory** — Evolutionary advantage of landscapes offering both vantage points and protection

## Sensory Benefits for Neurodivergent Individuals

Study participants reported therapeutic and calming impacts of:

- **Natural lighting** — Preferred over artificial, fluorescent lighting
- **Greenery** — Visual biophilic engagement and [[sensory-affordances|sensory affordances]]
- **Water features** — Acoustic masking, visual engagement, vestibular affordances, temperature modulation
- **Wildlife** — Visual interest and engagement
- **Natural materials** — Textural variety and tactile engagement

## Application to School Playgrounds

[[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. (2025)]] found that both neurodivergent and neurotypical children prioritized nature and natural elements in ideal playground design:

- **Nature spaces supporting both active and reflective play** — Treehouses, nature mountains, and natural areas enabling active engagement and quiet restoration
- **Biophilic benefits for neurodivergent children** — Green spaces and natural materials reduce anxiety and depression, supporting sustained engagement and positive social outcomes
- **Essential for post-pandemic recovery** — Nature-based play spaces address heightened mental health needs in young people following pandemic isolation

## Contrasts with Artificial Intervention

The studies found detrimental effects of:

- **Hardscapes and artificial materials** — Reduced sensory richness and therapeutic benefit
- **Anthropophonic noise** — Vehicle, machinery, and human-generated sounds; playground design should consider acoustic management
- **Excessive human intervention** — Loss of natural variation and complexity

## Integration with Environmental Goals

Finnigan (2024) notes no conflicts between [[sensory-responsive-design|sensory-responsive design]] and broader environmental sustainability goals, suggesting potential synergies:

- Climate mitigation
- Water conservation
- Ecological principles
- Regenerative design
