# Magda Mostafa

## Summary

Magda Mostafa is an Egyptian autism design consultant and architect, developer of the [[aspectss-design-index|ASPECTSS® Design Index]] (2013, American University in Cairo, in partnership with Progressive Architects). She works internationally as a design consultant applying ASPECTSS principles across school, therapeutic, and community settings.

## Origin

Mostafa has worked in autism design for over 20 years. Her entry point was "necessity is the mother of invention": in 2002, while in the first year of her PhD at Cairo University, she was given the project of designing Egypt's first educational centre for autism — the **Advance School for Developing Skills of Special Needs Children**, a retrofit of an existing residential building in Egypt [[raw/An Interview with Magda Mostafa_ Pioneer in Autism Design.md]].

She searched mainstream design guidelines and accessibility codes expecting to find a section on designing for autism: "I had a rude awakening; there was virtually nothing." She discarded 18 months of work on a prior dissertation topic and pivoted — supported by her thesis advisor [[zakia-shafie|Zakia Shafie]] — to make autism architecture her doctoral focus. That dissertation became the research foundation for ASPECTSS [[raw/When We Design for Autism, We Design for Everyone]].

A 2008 study arising from this work was "among the first autism design studies to be prospective not retrospective, have a control group, and measure quantifiable factors in a systematic way" [[raw/An Interview with Magda Mostafa_ Pioneer in Autism Design.md]].

Mostafa documented the original Advance School retrofit in an article published in the National Autistic Society magazine *Communication* (2006) [[raw/An Interview with Magda Mostafa_ Pioneer in Autism Design.md]].

## Design Equity Principle

Mostafa's stated architectural ethic (2022): "no one has the right to exist more comfortably, safely or effectively in space than anyone else" — it is the architect's responsibility to create built environments affording comfort, safety, and efficacy to the entire spectrum of the human condition. Expressed as "architecture must listen" in response to growing autistic self-advocacy. See [[mostafa-2022-architecture-for-all]].

## Key Contributions by Project

**Dublin City University (2020–2021):** Led development of the Autism Friendly University Design Guide for DCU, designated the world's first autism-friendly university (2018). The guide was a response to a global call for proposals from Dublin City University (DCU) and Ireland's National Autism Society (AsIAM). Mostafa: "I was honored to be selected for this pioneering work, primarily because it was informed and led by autistic students, advocates and leaders at DCU and AsIAM" [[raw/Magda Mostafa Honored With Friendly and Inclusive Spaces Award.md]]. The guide was initiated by an autism self-advocacy group of autistic students and individuals. Co-design process: Mostafa was paired with an autistic student for one-on-one sensory audits through the autistic lens. The guide was peer-reviewed by autistic students, autistic architects, autistic parents, and people who received diagnosis in adulthood. Published open access, intentionally written at two levels of specificity so facility managers, workplace designers, housing developers, and transportation planners could all apply it [[raw/When We Design for Autism, We Design for Everyone]].

**Sharjah City for Humanitarian Services (2016):** ASPECTSS-guided consultation on a multi-functional mixed-use neighborhood including an autism school. Scope: program development, sensory design masterplan, architectural guidelines, peer review of concept and design development, interior design detailing and material selection.

**Munroe-Meyer Institute (2018–2019):** Comprehensive sensory therapy and medical services center. Layered ASPECTSS with additional sensory design strategies: users as a spectrum, autism-inclusive universal design, independence as goal, narrative-centric design, sensory-spatial diagnostics, sensory zoning and sequencing.

**El Balad Autism Center, Ramallah (2018):** Education center for autistic Palestinian refugee orphans built with vernacular load-bearing stone and courtyard hierarchy in an olive grove. Review conducted via Cairo workshop applying ASPECTSS principles; live-learn facility serving a triple-vulnerable population.

**ELS Center for Excellence / Els for Autism Foundation, Jupiter, Florida (2015–2016, follow-up 2023):** Hybrid retro-fit and new build at The Learning Academy (K-12 charter school for autistic students). Performed an ASPECTSS-informed [[post-occupancy-evaluation|Post-Occupancy Evaluation]] (POE) in 2018 informing five design interventions: colour-coded navigation, acoustical treatments, transition alcoves (purple pods), classroom compartmentalization template, and de-escalation spaces. Outcomes assessed via staff survey, structured observation, and interviews published in [[mostafa-et-al-2023-aspectss-case-study]] (Mostafa, Sotelo, Honsberger, Honsberger, Brooker Lozott & Shanok 2023); results generally positive across all four intervention categories.

## Autism Across the Lifespan

Mostafa anticipated a design wave shifting from K-12 to higher education, then to workplace, and predicts the next will be assisted living / senior housing for autistic aging. She attributes the shift to the cohort diagnosed ~2002, who grew up with more aware support systems and are now entering higher education and the workforce. See [[autism-lifespan-design]] [[raw/When We Design for Autism, We Design for Everyone]].

A formative observation: two autistic women working in autism advocacy in the UK, both in their twenties, told Mostafa after a panel presentation that it had never occurred to them they had the right to ask for built environment accommodations in the same way colleagues using wheelchairs or hearing aids do. Mostafa describes this as a "light bulb moment" that exposed a gap in how her profession communicates design rights.

## Sensory Atlas

Mostafa curated a **Sensory Atlas** — a collection of visual records of cities worldwide from an autistic sensory perspective, combining audio, photography, digital collage, and film. [[stuart-neilson|Stuart Neilson]] contributed motion intensity heatmaps and sensory composites of global public spaces. The Atlas was exhibited as the "Sensory Atlas" wall component at the 2023 Venice Biennale installation. Visitors could add their own contributions via a display panel in the installation space.

## Survival Stage and Greenhouse Effect

Mostafa describes a **"survival stage"** of autism in which minimizing sensory input from the built environment — through muted colors, natural materials, good acoustics, simple shapes, intimate spaces, natural lighting — creates "a precious window of opportunity" for the user to be freed from overwhelming sensory noise and instead communicate, respond, learn, and interact [[raw/An Interview with Magda Mostafa_ Pioneer in Autism Design.md]].

The purpose: "to take those fleeting moments of calm and connection with our children and make them last longer."

However, Mostafa explicitly cautions against a **"greenhouse effect"**: designing a perfectly calm, stimulus-free environment in which a child thrives only to fall apart in the real world. ASPECTSS addresses this by proposing a **gradual weaning-off** from the strictest application of the criteria, allowing users to generalize newly acquired skills in progressively less-controlled environments [[raw/An Interview with Magda Mostafa_ Pioneer in Autism Design.md]].

## Evidence-Based Design Position

Mostafa does not view evidence-based design as "the future" of architecture — she notes it has existed for a long time — but maintains it has an essential place. Her argument: architects control the built environments humans live, work, play, learn, and socialize in; that is a "tremendous responsibility" that requires being "armed with data and information of which environments work best." Research also functions as a vehicle for including previously excluded user groups (neurodivergent people, emerging modes of life) in the design process without determinism [[raw/An Interview with Magda Mostafa_ Pioneer in Autism Design.md]].

On the challenge of controlling variables in built-environment research: unlike blind testing, users and their teachers or parents know they are in an altered environment, which may colour their reporting — making it "very difficult to eliminate bias completely."
