# SAP Autism at Work

## Summary

SAP's Autism at Work program (launched 2013) is described by Mostafa (2021) as "one of the first and most influential workplace initiatives" addressing how workplace design can support autism-inclusive spaces. It emerged from recognition that autistic employees with strengths in numeracy, pattern identification, and innovative thinking represent a significant and underserved workforce segment in the tech sector.

## Key Claims

- Launched at SAP in 2013 as "Autism at Work"; influenced subsequent workplace inclusion programs across the tech sector [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- The tech industry's interest in autism-inclusive workplace design was partly driven by recognition of specific autistic cognitive strengths: numeracy, pattern identification, coding, and innovative thinking [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]
- This initiative contributed to growing awareness of neurodiversity in the workplace, alongside the post-COVID mental health crisis and rethinking of workplaces during lockdown [[raw/Autism friendly design – an interview with Magda Mostafa.md]]

## Context

The tech sector's early adoption of autism-inclusive workplace design reflects the third wave of [[autism-lifespan-design]]: as the cohort diagnosed ~2002 entered the workforce, employers with high concentrations of autistic workers (particularly tech firms) began responding to design and HR demands from autistic employees and self-advocacy groups.
