# Environmental Experience

## Summary

Environmental experience refers to the totality of subjective psychological states arising during human-environment encounter, encompassing emotional, sensory, bodily, interactive, and cognitive dimensions. Experience conveys "what it is like" to inhabit a place and can only be accessed through first-person reports.

## Key claims

- Experience is distinct from consciousness: consciousness enables experience, but is not itself the content of experience [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Environmental experience encompasses perceptual experiences (through distinct senses), emotional and bodily experiences, desires, thoughts, memories, and mental images [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Experience is more than observation or knowledge; it describes the "complete, encompassing influence of a space, object, or person on the user through mind, perception, and senses" [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Environmental experience operates through three systems: sensorimotor (sensory stimulation + bodily response), emotion-valuation (affective response), and knowledge-meaning (cognitive/interpretive understanding) [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Places are experienced through four interconnected aspects of the lifeworld: lived space (how place makes people feel), lived body (corporeal experience), lived time (temporal experience), and lived human relation (social bonds) [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Contemporary built environments often lack sensory richness; sight is privileged while other senses remain understimulated [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]
- Empirical research on environmental experience is crucial for evidence-based design: people's experiences are the primary measure of architectural design efficacy [[raw/fpsyg-15-1303397.extracted.md]]

## Dimensions of environmental experience

One study discovered 10 abstract dimensions through multidimensional scaling analysis:
1. Pleasure/displeasure
2. High/low arousal
3. Dominance/submissiveness
4. Knowing/inhabiting
5. Environment/self
6. Higher/lower cognition
7. Spatiality/materiality
8. States/processes
9. Natural/built
10. Visual/sensory

## Methodological note

First-person reports are the *only* way to study human experiences, since experience conveys subjective "what it is like" quality inaccessible to external observation. Phenomenological introspection (descriptive, not interpretive) is the empirically sound approach.
