# Identity-First Language

## Summary

Identity-first language ("autistic person") is preferred by most autistic people over person-first language ("person with autism") because autism is understood as an intrinsic, lifelong aspect of identity rather than a separate condition a person carries. Using the community's preferred language signals genuine consideration of autistic perspectives and supports inclusive practice.

## Key claims

- Most autistic people prefer identity-first language; autism is understood as fundamental to the individual — present from birth, not separable from identity (Autistic Self Advocacy Network). [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- The contrast with person-first framing: conditions like anxiety may come and go, so person-first framing ("person with anxiety") can be appropriate; autism is lifelong and constitutive, so "autistic person" is preferred. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]
- Using correct language with marginalized groups is vital for creating inclusive, safe spaces and demonstrating that their self-views have been considered. [[raw/svaler-2023-on-making-libraries-and-museums-more-accessible-for-autistic-people.extracted.md]]

## Person-first vs. identity-first

| | Person-first | Identity-first |
|---|---|---|
| Form | "person with autism" | "autistic person" |
| Assumption | autism is separate from the person; carried externally | autism is constitutive of identity |
| Community preference | medical/professional default | preferred by most autistic self-advocates |

## Notes on contested usage

- Some individuals and families prefer person-first; preference is not universal across all autistic people
- Institutional and medical contexts (especially older literature) default to person-first
- The safest approach: ask individuals their preference; default to identity-first when writing for the autism community generally

## Adoption in educator resources

- The TRIAD (Vanderbilt Kennedy Center) AAC educator tip sheet uses identity-first language throughout, citing the TRIAD identity-language web page as its rationale. [[raw/AAC_and_Autism_Tip_Sheet_Edu.extracted.md]]
