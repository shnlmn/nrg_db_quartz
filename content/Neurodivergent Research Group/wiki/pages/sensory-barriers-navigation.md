# Sensory Barriers to Navigation

## Summary

Environmental characteristics that hinder independent navigation and participation for people with visual impairment. Jenkins et al. (2015) identified four main categories of barriers: population-specific design, extreme sensory backgrounds, uneven ground surfaces and objects, and inconsistent lighting.

## Key barriers

### Population-Specific Design
Built environments constructed from perspective of sighted people prioritizing visual appeal and convenience without considering non-visual users. Examples:
- Large open spaces with vaulted ceilings that confuse sound information or magnify it to excessive levels
- Low-hanging signs posing safety hazards
- Curb cuts pointing to center of intersection rather than crossing each street separately
- Bus stations with multiple buses running under roof, creating cacophony that masks directional cues [[jenkins-et-al-2015-multisensory-visual-impairment]]

> [!quote] One respondent: "The setup is a nightmare. With ten buses together, engines running under a roof, it's like being inside a drum with someone pounding on it. I rely almost solely on sound. I cannot distinguish one bus from another, and the cacophony of noise seems to jumble my mobility skills."

### Extreme Sensory Backgrounds
Environments with excessive or absent sensory cues preventing effective orientation:
- Loud persistent noise masking target auditory signals needed for directional travel
- Significant echo and reverberation creating sound distortion (difficult to isolate cues and determine direction)
- Large open spaces void of sensory cues (open fields, parking lots)
- Quiet vehicles (hybrid cars) providing insufficient auditory information for street crossing decisions [[jenkins-et-al-2015-multisensory-visual-impairment]]

### Uneven Ground Surfaces and Objects
Physical hazards creating fall risks:
- Cracks, bumps, and drop-offs on sidewalks
- Tree roots pushing up sidewalk surfaces
- Low-lying obstacles like signs [[jenkins-et-al-2015-multisensory-visual-impairment]]

### Inconsistent Lighting
Visual inconsistencies problematic even for those with residual vision:
- Shadows cast by trees or buildings
- Low contrast signage
- Creates confusion and difficulty orienting [[jenkins-et-al-2015-multisensory-visual-impairment]]
