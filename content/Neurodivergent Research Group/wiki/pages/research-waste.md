# Research Waste

## Summary

Research waste refers to the avoidable loss of health research investment through non-publication, incomplete reporting, poor design, and misalignment between researcher priorities and the needs of patients, clinicians, and other end-users. Glasziou and Chalmers (2016) estimate that 85% of medical research funding — approximately $170 billion annually — is wasted in this way.

## Key claims

- Only 9 of 334 studies reviewed by Oliver and Gray (2006) compared researchers' priorities with those of patients or practitioners, indicating a fundamental disconnect between research agendas and end-user needs [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- Ioannidis (2016) argues that a lack of pragmatism and a lack of patient-centeredness are two major reasons for research waste; "practicing doctors and other health care professionals [are] familiar with how little of what they find in medical journals is useful" [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- Research waste is highest at the study planning phase where research topics, questions, and designs are set — involvement of end-users at this stage is critical for reducing waste [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- Organisations such as INVOLVE (UK), the James Lind Alliance (UK, est. 2004), and PCORI (US, est. 2010) were established specifically to promote end-user involvement in health research to combat research waste [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
- [[co-design-in-research|Research co-design]] is the primary proposed remedy for research waste: meaningful end-user engagement during the planning phase ensures research addresses questions and outcomes relevant to those who will use the findings [[raw/Research co-design in health_ a rapid overview of reviews - Health Research Policy and Systems.md|Slattery et al.]]
