# Co-option in Research

## Summary

A process through which a person or group's activities are altered or appropriated to serve another group's interests. In [[participatory-action-research|PAR]], co-option is a key risk where participatory processes are adopted in name but directed to serve institutional or researcher agendas rather than community interests.

## Key claims

- Co-option is identified as one of the key challenges faced by PAR projects, alongside mismatches with institutional infrastructure, power inequalities, and decentralizing of control [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- Counter-strategies include cultivating a critical community to hold researchers to account and using critical [[reflexivity]] to examine whose interests are being served [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
- Co-option risk is closely related to [[extractive-research|extractive research]] practices, where participatory language is used but community benefit and ownership are not achieved [[raw/Participatory action research - Nature Reviews Methods Primers.md|Cornish et al.]]
