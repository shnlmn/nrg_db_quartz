# Sensory Transect

## Summary

Walking methodology where researcher or participant physically traverses a predetermined or spontaneous route through a space while systematically documenting presence, intensity, and change in sensory experiences. Captures spatial transformation of sensory perception across an environment rather than static point measurements. Can focus on single sensory modality or integrate multiple senses simultaneously.

## Key Claims

- **Transect routes can vary in configuration depending on research goals:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Linear routes (including reversed paths with specified stopping points)
  - U-shaped or circular routes
  - Combination approaches adapted to site topography
  - Each configuration has distinct benefits and limitations for different research questions

- **Sensory transects capture spatial variance that point measurements miss:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Reveal how sensory experience changes across a space
  - Enable identification of multiple sound sources (e.g., across a market) or visual features (e.g., landscape diversity)
  - Useful at multiple scales: small-medium spaces through large outdoor sites
  - More rapid turnover than separate single-sense walks, enabling greater site coverage

- **Aural transects:**
  - Audio recordings synchronized with video enable post-visit sound source identification [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Capture transformation of aural experience while minimizing noise distortion from movement [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Can include narrative reports of researcher's perspective; videography assists with source/structural feature identification [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]

- **Olfactory transects (smellwalks):** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Pre-determined routes maximize exposure to diverse olfactory experiences
  - Continuous routes explore spatial variance; stopping points focus on site-specific odors [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Duration should be limited to 45 minutes; flexible for spontaneous route variation when engaged [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Video/audio recordings enable researcher focus on olfactory elements; narrative reports capture preference, intensity, emotional experience [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]

- **Tactile transects:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Active engagement touching site-specific iconic objects and ground textures
  - Assess "feel" of ambient spaces, crowds, temperatures, humidity, temperature flux
  - Routes identical to sensory paths ensure data consistency
  - Hands-free videography enables haptic exploration; handheld recorders facilitate creative engagement and retrospective mapping

- **Multisensory transects balance integration with concentration:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Benefit: capture rapid multisensory integration and achieve greater site coverage
  - Limitation: constant sensory switching reduces concentration and may miss unique single-sense components (e.g., detecting timber smell through touching)
  - More suitable for research exploring multisensory integration or involving multiple participants [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]

- **Dual-researcher transects reduce individual bias:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Can be conducted in silence (immersive familiarization) or with live discussion (temporal/spatial contextualization)
  - Combining local and nonlocal researchers provides cultural awareness plus habituation avoidance [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Locational familiarity may be important for capturing full sensory experience

- **Transcription and reflection best captured immediately post-transect:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Narrative reports document sensory impressions, mental associations, preferences, intensities
  - Impression mapping can occur retrospectively using video as reference
  - Time lag between observations and documentation can affect recall accuracy
