# Sensory Processing in ADHD

## Summary

Sensory processing patterns in ADHD can both co-occur with and be confused with attention deficits. The [[sensory-profile-2|Sensory Profile 2]]'s **Registration quadrant** — describing children who appear not to notice sensory input, seem inattentive, and miss environmental cues — is frequently endorsed for children initially presenting with attention concerns. Elevated SP-2 Registration scores, when occurring in the absence of elevated attentional behavioral indicators, support a sensory-based hypothesis (under-responsivity requiring sensory intervention) over a primary attention hypothesis (requiring behavioral or cognitive interventions).

## Key Claims

- **Registration quadrant as differential marker:** Children with elevated SP-2 Registration scores who are inattentive and miss environmental cues may have sensory under-responsivity rather than ADHD. This distinction is clinically important because sensory-based occupational therapy (environmental design, multisensory strategies to increase salience) differs fundamentally from ADHD-directed interventions (behavioral supports, executive function training, medication).

- **Cross-setting assessment value:** The [[sensory-profile-2|School Companion SP-2 (SCSP-2)]] administered by teachers alongside parent-completed Child SP-2 can help clarify the mechanism: if Registration scores are elevated in school but not at home, or vice versa, this points to environmental factors rather than purely child-intrinsic attention deficits.
