# Sensory Walk Questionnaire

## Summary

The Sensory Walk Questionnaire is a quantitative tool for measuring the multi-sensory human experience of a place. Developed by Pohjanheimo, Ojansivu, and Hopia (2023), it uses a structured lexicon of descriptors across four sensory modalities — sound, smell, vision, and somatosensory (touch/temperature) — combined with hedonic liking, intensity, and appropriateness ratings. It is the first instrument to apply established multi-sensory evaluation practices to the assessment of surrounding environments.

## Key claims

- The questionnaire characterises a place using a Check-All-That-Apply (CATA) question built from a multi-sensory lexicon constructed via literature review and expert interviews with architects and landscape architects [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- In addition to the CATA lexicon, the tool measures hedonic liking, perceived intensity, and perceived appropriateness of sensations in each modality [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- A case study in three areas of a restaurant (lobby, dining area, waste point) detected significantly different sensory profiles across locations — e.g., odours were more intense in the lobby and dining area, while sound intensity was highest at the waste point [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- The type and intensity of sensations affect the perceived pleasantness of the overall experience [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- The tool enables quantitative comparison across places, addressing a gap left by qualitative-only approaches (interviews, focus groups, recordings) that yield rich data but make cross-site comparison difficult [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Mapping sensory experiences can serve as a first step to localise problems in an environment and identify characteristics to improve [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
