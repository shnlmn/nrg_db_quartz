# Taste Documentation Methods

## Summary

Methodologies for systematically identifying and recording gustatory (taste) sensory experiences in on-site research contexts. Recognizes that taste is inherently multisensory—involving olfactory (food odors), visual, and tactile (texture) components alongside pure taste perception. Focuses on documentation of iconic foods, flavor experiences, and cultural/heritage gustatory significance. Addresses major gap in sensory research where gustatory elements often underexplored compared to aural/visual modalities.

## Key Claims

- **Gustatory documentation is understudied compared to other senses:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Little prior discussion of identification and documentation of in-situ taste and flavor experiences
  - Often research sites contain iconic foods with strong cultural/heritage associations
  - Conversely, sites without iconic foods require documentation of broader available gustatory elements

- **Taste descriptions must capture basic taste dimensions and multisensory context:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Basic taste perception: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami
  - Chemical analysis employed as needed for detailed analysis
  - Context documentation: food color, shape, smell, texture, temperature, presentation techniques
  - Supplemented with photographic imagery (external and internal features of food)

- **Flavor is fundamentally multisensory and must be documented holistically:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Olfactory component: food aromas critical to flavor perception
  - Visual component: appeal and presentation of food
  - Tactile component: texture and mouthfeel sensations
  - All dimensions intertwine; separation into components risks losing integrated experience

- **Taste description templates enable comparable datasets:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Standardized formats allow consistent documentation across multiple foods or sites
  - Structure: taste dimensions, aroma, texture, visual features, temperature, cultural/social significance

- **Social contextualization of iconic foods enriches documentation:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Compare same foods across multiple sites/places to document variations in (multi)sensory experience
  - Document food preparation methods and practices
  - Include local familiarity effects (local eaters may perceive differently than outsiders)
  - Mapping of stalls/shops providing foodstuffs documents foodways and contextualizes multisensory relationships

- **Researchers should employ multiple tasters to reduce bias:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Dual researchers recommended for subjective gustatory documentation
  - Pre-study basic taste testing accounts for physiological differences in taste perception capabilities
  - Individual taste sensitivity affects degree of detection and identification

- **Non-iconic products warrant documentation despite lesser cultural significance:** [[raw/parker-et-al-2024-the-identification-and-documentation-of-on-site-sensory-and-multisensory-experience-a-methodological.extracted.md]]
  - Non-regionally distinct yet iconic products can be documented intra- and inter-place
  - Enables comparison of (multi)sensory experience across contexts

## Methods Summary

| Method | Benefits | Limitations | Best Suited For |
|--------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Taste descriptions of iconic foods | Captures multisensory experience; creates representative record; identifies hedonic value; creates comparable dataset | Subjective measurement; unknown iconic foods may be missed | Iconic and common foods at specific sites |
| Visual documentation of foods/preparation | Creates representative record; allows heritage practices documentation; captures flux over time/space | Does not capture multisensory components | Hawker markets, cultural food events, food preparation observation |
| Mapping of stalls/shops | Allows mapping; contextualizes multisensory experience | Cannot allow for contextualization of multisensory experience | Food markets where spatial distribution affects experience |
| Inter- and intra-place food comparison | Documents cultural practice variations; enables multisensory experience variation analysis | Time-intensive; requires multiple visits | Heritage food products with regional or seasonal variation |

## Open Questions

- How do researchers document iconic foods in cultural contexts where tasting/consumption carries social/religious significance or restrictions?
- What methods best capture changing seasonal gustatory experiences without requiring multiple year-long data collection periods?
- How can taste documentation account for individual dietary restrictions, allergies, or neurodivergent taste sensitivities?
- How do inter-place food comparisons control for variations in ingredient sourcing, preparation, freshness, and storage affecting taste?
