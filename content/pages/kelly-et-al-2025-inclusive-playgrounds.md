# Kelly et al. 2025: Inclusive School Playgrounds

**Let's Play: Co-designing inclusive school playgrounds with neurodivergent children**

*Shelly Kelly, Jeremy Kerr, Janice Rieger, Debra Flanders Cushing*

International Journal of Educational Research Open, vol. 9 (2025), article 100494.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijedro.2025.100494

## Summary

Qualitative research using co-design methodology to develop inclusive school playground designs with neurodivergent (ND) children. Seven predominantly neurodivergent children (ages 10–12) at one Brisbane school and six predominantly neurotypical (NT) children at another school participated in four iterative co-design workshops over twelve weeks. Through play-based activities, sensory provocations (tactile bunny sculpture), avatar-based design, and reflective journals, participants co-created six versions of an ideal playground prototype. Analysis revealed four design principles—sensory experiences, challenging opportunities, social interaction, and natural elements—grounded in [[affordances|affordance theory]], [[salutogenic-design|salutogenic design]], [[biophilic-design|biophilic design]], and prospect-refuge concepts.

## Key Claims

- **Play feature preferences span sensory modalities**: ND and NT children both prioritized spinning/swinging (vestibular), climbing (proprioceptive), nature, music, and imaginative elements—demonstrating that [[affordances|affordances]] for self-regulation support inclusion for all children [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]

- **Four inclusive design principles emerge from co-design analysis**: sensory experiences, challenging opportunities, social interaction, and natural elements; these are essential for both engagement and wellbeing in school playgrounds [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]

- **Prospect-refuge design supports anxious and socially uncertain children**: High vantage points (ridges, treehouses) allowing parallel play and observation without direct interaction reduce social pressure while enabling inclusion for autistic and sensory-sensitive children [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]

- **Safety-averse policies inadvertently exclude neurodivergent children**: Removal of challenging elements (flying foxes, swings, sand pits) due to liability concerns eliminates vestibular and proprioceptive affordances essential for ND children's self-regulation, despite children's capacity to identify and suggest safety improvements [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]

- **Co-design methodology positions neurodivergent children as competent contributors**: Iterative feedback cycles, visual supports (avatars, responsive toolkit), and multi-modal data collection (video, audio, drawings, journals) enabled equitable participation and revealed design insights absent from adult-only processes [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]

- **Post-pandemic social isolation heightens need for socially flexible play spaces**: Increased mental health implications in 10–14-year-olds require playground features supporting both parallel and direct social engagement, with particular attention to ND children's anxiety and reduced peer interactions [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]

## Methodology

**Research design**: Qualitative, four co-design workshops across two participant groups over twelve weeks.

**Co-design methods**:
- [[IDEAS]] (Interface Design Experience for the Autistic Spectrum) — visual supports and templates tailored to neurodivergent children
- [[cooperative-inquiry]] — flexible, child-led design approach adapted to individual needs
- TEACCH method — visual cues, schedules, structured routines
- [[zones-of-regulation]] — traffic-light emotion regulation system
- Responsive Toolkit — sensory and reflective prompts (balloons, drums, Velcro, fabric)
- Tactile Bunny — 1.6m sensory provocation with reversible sequin fabric
- Clay avatars — individual representations to guide play area mapping
- Reflective journals — home-based ideation and feedback
- Journey mapping — individual play preference documentation

**Data analysis**: Inductive thematic analysis with multimodal methods (video, audio, photographs, transcripts, drawings, prototypes). Triangulation and member-checking with participants. NVivo software for coding verification.

**Ethical framework**: Social model of disability, strengths-based neurodivergent approach, epistemic justice principles. QUT and Queensland Department of Education approvals obtained.

## Findings

**Six themes emerged from workshop analysis**:
1. Playgrounds Are Too Safe and Boring
2. Sensations and Sounds to Make You Feel Good
3. Challenging Play Features: "Why do I feel better when I climb up high?"
4. Nature
5. Quiet Seating and Social Play
6. Unusual, Fantastical, and Unexpected Features: "Under the slide is a good place to hide"

**Playground prototype evolution (V.1–V.6)**:
- V.1: 18 initial features, felt overcrowded; punch bag removed in favor of high-energy sensory alternatives
- V.2: 13 refined features, validated by ND group
- V.3: 17 features including sandpit, firepit, dual swings, open grassy area, elevated treehouse, high ridge for observation
- V.4: Feedback template for NT group review
- V.5: Three NT-suggested amendments (pathways, grassy area, firepit relocation)
- V.6: 20 final features; ND group accepted all NT amendments

**Key play features identified across both groups**:
- Spinning and swinging (vestibular affordance)
- High climbing structures and elevated seating (proprioceptive, prospect-refuge)
- Nature and natural materials (biophilic)
- Music and imaginative elements
- Quiet withdrawal spaces
- Open grassy running areas
- Pathways for wheelchair access

## Theoretical Integration

**Affordance Theory** — Environmental features provide differential action possibilities based on individual capacities, needs, and perception. Swings may provide essential vestibular feedback for ND self-regulation while producing sensory aversion (static shock) for others.

**Salutogenic Design** — Health-promoting features emphasizing emotional and physical benefits. Presence of sensory affordances enables longer engagement, positive social outcomes, and wellbeing.

**Biophilic Design** — Natural elements (greenery, water, natural materials) reduce anxiety, restore attention, and provide restorative benefits.

**Prospect-Refuge Theory** — Evolutionary preference for vantage points with protection; supports parallel play, observation, and inclusion without direct social pressure.

**Social Model of Disability** — Recognizes environmental barriers (not individual neurotype) as sources of exclusion; co-design empowers neurodivergent children to identify and address these barriers.

## Implications

**For playground design**:
- Move beyond safety compliance toward responsive, multi-sensory environments
- Integrate [[prospect-refuge]] design elements for socially anxious users
- Reframe risky play as developmental necessity, not liability
- Include quiet zones and nature spaces alongside active features
- Design for diverse sensory profiles using [[design-from-sensorial-margins]] principles

**For inclusive research**:
- Co-design with neurodivergent participants reveals design insights absent from adult-only processes
- Equitable participation requires visual supports, flexible communication, and adapted timelines
- Neurodivergent children are competent contributors and experts of their own experience

**For education and policy**:
- Systemic change needed in how play environments are conceived, funded, implemented
- Post-pandemic mental health priorities require socially flexible, emotionally supportive spaces
- Integration of sensory responsive and biophilic design supports all children
