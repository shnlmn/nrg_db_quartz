# Self-Regulation in Play

## Summary

Capacity to manage emotional, behavioral, and physiological responses through play engagement. Self-directed play with appropriate [[sensory-affordances|sensory affordances]]—particularly vestibular and proprioceptive stimulation—enables children to modulate arousal and emotional state, supporting sustained attention, behavioral control, and emotional wellbeing.

## Neurobiological Mechanisms

**Self-directed vestibular and proprioceptive engagement** supports neurotransmitter release enabling:
- Sustained attention and focused behavior
- Emotional balance and stress management
- Behavioral control without external enforcement
- Anxiety reduction and arousal modulation

From [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. 2025]]: Self-directed engagement through swings, spinners, climbing enables neurodivergent children to regulate themselves when environments support their specific regulatory needs.

## Vestibular and Proprioceptive Affordances

**Vestibular system** (balance, spinning, acceleration):
- Swinging and spinning features supporting vestibular input
- High-speed or height changes providing activation
- Essential for children seeking vestibular input for arousal management

**Proprioceptive system** (bodily awareness, force/pressure):
- Climbing requiring muscle engagement and spatial awareness
- Structures enabling jumping, pushing, pressing
- Heavy work and resistance providing grounding and calming feedback
- Essential for children seeking proprioceptive input for regulation

## Stimming and Self-Soothing

Self-stimulating behaviors (stimming)—rocking, spinning, twisting fingers—are **essential coping mechanisms and self-regulation strategies**, not behaviors to suppress. Play environments supporting stimming behaviors through appropriate [[affordances|affordances]] enable:
- Voluntary self-regulation without shame
- Anxiety management through familiar, controllable engagement
- Integration of stimming into socially valued play (swinging, spinning) rather than isolation or suppression

## Environmental Support for Self-Regulation

From [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]:

**Features enabling self-regulation**:
- Spinning, swinging, climbing structures providing sought sensory input
- Quiet withdrawal spaces enabling recovery from overstimulation
- [[prospect-refuge|Prospect-refuge]] design enabling observation without social pressure
- Flexible participation modes (solo, parallel, direct interaction) supporting social regulation
- Nature and natural materials supporting attention restoration and calming

**Features inhibiting self-regulation**:
- Removal of vestibular/proprioceptive affordances despite safety policies
- Overwhelming sensory environments (excessive noise, unregulated lighting)
- Forced direct social engagement without observation/parallel options
- Insufficient quiet or withdrawal space for recovery

## Behavioral Outcomes

When environments support self-regulation through appropriate sensory affordances:
- Children stay engaged longer
- Positive social interactions increase
- Behavioral dysregulation decreases
- Anxiety and emotional distress reduce
- Sense of wellbeing and school belonging improve

## Differential Impact of Design

Play features and their regulatory affordances are **perceived differently by each child** depending on:
- Individual sensory processing patterns (hypersensitivity, hyposensitivity)
- Arousal state and regulatory needs in the moment
- Prior experiences and associations
- Presence of co-regulatory relationships

Example: A swing may provide essential calming vestibular input for one child while producing anxiety (fear of falling) for another. [[affordances|Affordance theory]] and [[design-from-sensorial-margins|design from sensorial margins]] emphasize designing for diverse profiles.
