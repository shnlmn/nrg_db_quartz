# Monotropic Attention

**Summary** — Monotropic attention is a pattern of attentional focus in which cognitive resources concentrate intensely on one or a few interconnected interests at a time, rather than distributing attention fluidly across multiple simultaneous activities. This attentional style is particularly common in autistic people and represents a valid, distinct neurocognitive trait.

## Key claims

- Monotropic attention describes how some neurodivergent people (particularly autistic individuals) direct cognitive and emotional focus; attentional resources concentrate on one or few highly connected topics/activities [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Monotropic attention is a neurocognitive trait worthy of understanding as part of research design—not a deficit or limitation to be overcome [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
- Research environments that recognize monotropic attention can design around sudden topic shifts, extended focused engagement periods, or difficulty context-switching [[raw/dark-2025-inclusion-by-design-a-neuro-cognitive-trait-interaction-approach-to-neurodivergent-research.extracted.md|Dark 2025]]
