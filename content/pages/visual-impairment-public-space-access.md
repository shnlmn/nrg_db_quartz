# Visual Impairment Public Space Access

## Summary

The challenges and strategies people with visual impairment use to access and participate in public spaces. Access is primarily an information problem (lack of sensory cues), not a physical problem, and is mediated by the built environment's sensory characteristics rather than visual presentation alone.

## Key claims

- Inaccessibility for people with visual impairment is characterized not by physical barriers per se, but by lack of information sources (signage, spatial knowledge) that cannot be accessed without vision [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

- The relationship between people with visual impairment and "visually biased" public spaces is one of the most challenging human-environment interactions [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

- Participation in public spaces depends on ability to decode and choreograph multiple sensory interactions simultaneously to produce meaningful understanding of the environment [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

- Sensory characteristics in built environments—auditory, olfactory, haptic, thermal—can either support or inhibit independent navigation and activity participation [[sensory-barriers-navigation]], [[auditory-navigation-cues]], [[olfactory-navigation-cues]], [[tactile-navigation-cues]]

- Level of participation varies based on characteristics of the environment, not just individual capability [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]
