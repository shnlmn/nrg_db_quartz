# Cooperative Inquiry

## Summary

Flexible, child-led participatory design methodology emphasizing responsiveness to participants' individual needs, behaviors, and communication styles. Cooperative Inquiry is a creative research approach enabling co-design and co-creation adapted in real-time to support equitable participation across neurodivergent participants with diverse support needs.

## Core Principles

**Child-centered flexibility**:
- Adapts workshop structure, duration, and activities to individual needs and preferences
- Responsive to behavioral cues, energy levels, and communication differences
- Values diverse participation modes (verbal, gestural, non-verbal expression)
- Maintains dignity and autonomy while providing necessary scaffolding

**Creative collaboration**:
- Uses open-ended, playful materials and provocation
- Enables ideation through multiple modalities (drawing, construction, discussion, movement)
- Welcomes unexpected ideas and emergent directions
- Celebrates diverse thinking and expression styles

## Adaptations for Neurodivergent Participants

From [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. 2025]]:

- **Communication flexibility** — Accepting alternative expression forms: emotional cues, gestural communication, artwork, construction, quiet engagement
- **Duration and pacing** — Flexible workshop timing responding to fatigue, overstimulation, or interest fluctuation
- **Environmental responsiveness** — Availability of fidgets, sensory items, breaks, and emotional regulation supports
- **Individual vs. group options** — Parallel individual activities alongside group discussion, honoring social preferences

## Integration with Other Co-Design Methods

[[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]] combined Cooperative Inquiry with [[IDEAS]] for structured yet flexible workshops:
- IDEAS provided visual templates and clear expectations
- Cooperative Inquiry added responsiveness and adaptation in real-time
- Combined approach enabled both structure (reducing anxiety) and flexibility (honoring individual differences)

## Emphasis on Non-Verbal Expression

Recognition that neurodivergent individuals—particularly non-verbal children—contribute essential design insights through:
- Emotional and gestural cues indicating preference or aversion
- Sound and movement patterns showing engagement
- Artwork and construction demonstrating design thinking
- Absence of verbal contribution does not indicate absence of valuable input
