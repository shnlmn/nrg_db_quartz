# Landscape Architecture for Neurodiversity

## Summary

Landscape architecture for neurodiversity is the field practice of designing outdoor built environments (parks, plazas, streetscapes, transportation systems, educational and work spaces) that support the sensory, cognitive, and social needs of neurodivergent individuals. Finnigan's (2024) research highlights significant voids in this field, particularly regarding outdoor environments, neurodivergent adults, and conditions beyond autism.

## Current State of Practice

As of 2024:

- Some design guidelines address autistic children and indoor environments (Mostafa's ASPECTSS™ Design Index, recommendations from Sachs and Gaines)
- Limited resources for other neurotypes (ADHD, dyslexia, synesthesia)
- Scarcity of resources for neurodivergent adults (assumption that needs fade in adulthood)
- Minimal integration of sensory-processing perspectives in mainstream landscape design training
- Disconnect from disability rights and neurodiversity movements in design education

## Key Findings from Landscape Practice

Finnigan's 31 participants (including landscape professionals) identified:

- **Sensory barriers** — Anthropogenic noise, artificial lighting, vehicle-centric design, lack of refuge
- **Design gaps** — Limited seating variety, inadequate wayfinding, missing [[sensorimotor-movement|sensorimotor opportunities]]
- **Social barriers** — Overcrowding, unsafe conditions, social isolation, lack of accessibility for diverse body types
- **Positive interventions** — [[biophilic-design|Biophilic design]], [[sensory-zoning|sensory zoning]], [[sensory-refuge|sensory refuges]], pedestrian-centric transportation

## Intersectionality

Landscape design for neurodiversity must account for:

- Multiple neurodivergent conditions simultaneously (autism + ADHD + dyslexia)
- [[sensory-stacking]] (multiple simultaneous sensory sensitivities)
- Intersectional identities (race, gender, disability, socioeconomic status)
- Multi-generational needs (children through older adults)
- Varied body types and physical abilities

## Future Directions

The field needs:

- Research spanning generations and neurotypes
- Integration of [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework|SREF]] principles in design practice
- [[co-design-in-research|Co-design]] with neurodivergent communities
- Occupational therapy and neuroscience collaboration
- Training in neurodiversity-affirming design
