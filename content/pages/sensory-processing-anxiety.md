# Sensory Processing in Anxiety

## Summary

Sensory over-responsivity is significantly associated with anxiety disorders in children across diagnostic groups (ASD, ADHD, and typically developing populations). The [[sensory-profile-2|Sensory Profile 2]] quadrants most relevant to anxiety are **Sensitivity** and **Avoiding** — patterns characterized by low neurological threshold (noticing sensory input easily) and avoidant or distress-based responses. Documenting sensory over-responsivity via [[sensory-profile-2|SP-2 assessment]] in anxious children supports sensory-based occupational therapy as a complementary intervention alongside psychotherapy, strengthening medical necessity arguments and multi-disciplinary collaboration.

## Key Claims

- **Research evidence:** Lane et al. (2012) documented significant relationships between sensory over-responsivity (Sensitivity and Avoiding quadrants) and anxiety disorder diagnoses in children, establishing the co-occurrence as empirically grounded rather than merely associational.

- **Clinical rationale for sensory intervention:** Children with anxiety often show elevated Sensitivity (noticing input easily, responding with distress) and Avoiding (withdrawing from sensory experiences) patterns. Occupational therapy interventions addressing sensory modulation — such as reducing unpredictable or overwhelming sensory input, increasing environmental predictability, and providing control over sensory exposure — complement psychotherapy by addressing the sensory dimension of anxiety.

- **Strengthens multidisciplinary approach:** Documenting sensory over-responsivity via SP-2 as a component of anxiety presentation provides evidence-based rationale for coordinated OT and mental health treatment, improving payer support and team communication.
