# Universal Design for Public Space

## Summary

Application of universal design principles to public spaces to ensure accessibility and usability for all people, including those with visual impairment, without visible stigmatization or special accommodation. Jenkins et al. (2015) argue that universal design offers solution to balance accessibility needs with shared, welcoming public space.

## Definition

Universal design is "the design of products and environments to be usable by all people, to the greatest extent possible, without the need for adaptation or specialized design." [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

## Core philosophy

The underlying philosophy holds that:
- Public spaces can achieve balance between accommodating all populations and avoiding stigma/discrimination
- Strategic embedding of sensory cues can create engaging, welcoming spaces for broad spectrum of users
- Subtle changes to building layout and configuration can benefit people with disabilities while requiring minimal adaptation for other users [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

## Design principles for public spaces

### Outdoor Spaces
- **Sound and surface texture:** Prioritize for safe street crossings
- **Accessible pedestrian signals:** Install at traffic light intersections with directional information
- **Vehicle detection devices:** Especially important for quiet-running hybrid vehicles
- **Tactile ground surface indicators:** Such as truncated domes for hazard warning and traffic direction indication
- **Landmark cues:** Strategically placed for inclines, declines, turns, curves
- **Street furniture placement:** Appropriate spacing and positioning [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

### Indoor Spaces
- **Information sources:** Available in auditory and/or tactile format (tactile maps with auditory feedback, braille labels)
- **Directional signage:** Large with bright lettering as effective orientation cues
- **Lighting:** Consistent, well-lit, non-fluorescent, without glare, with high luminance for contrast
- **Acoustic control:** Reduction of non-directional background noise and echo reverberation via acoustic tiles and soft wall items
- **Landmark sounds:** Strategic placement of sound-emitting objects (fountains) providing wayfinding and spatial information
- **Texture and tactile contrasts:** Defining different areas and landmarks
- **Floor surfaces:** Materials providing distinct tactile and auditory feedback for cane navigation
- **Airflow and air quality:** Strategic control supporting orientation and travel direction [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

## Implementation challenges

- May not be feasible to have different floor surfaces at every entry or location
- Requires balance between universal design and avoiding forced accommodations that stigmatize
- Requires conscious involvement of people with disabilities and rehabilitation professionals in design stages [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

## Key principle: Salience of Sensory Cues

"The key is to generate a balance of salient sensory cues in the built environment." This requires:
- Avoiding overwhelming sensory backgrounds
- Ensuring functional cues are distinct and recognizable
- Considering [[context-dependent-sensory-effectiveness|context-dependent effectiveness]] of sensory features
- Integration with broader design goals [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

## Benefits Beyond Visual Impairment

When designed consciously and creatively, universal design supporting sensory cues benefits:
- All users with sensory sensitivities
- Older adults with age-related sensory changes
- Temporary disability users
- Neurodivergent individuals with atypical sensory processing
- All users' wayfinding and comfort

Without identifying or stigmatizing particular groups [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]
