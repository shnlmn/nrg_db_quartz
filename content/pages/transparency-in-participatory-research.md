# Transparency in Participatory Research

## Summary

Transparency in participatory research is the practice of documenting, sharing, and explaining decisions made in response to [[community-advisory-board]] feedback—specifically, what actions were taken, what wasn't, and why. Transparency reports contribute to research that is democratic, reflexive, and respectful by making the process of co-design visible to community partners.

## Key claims

- A "transparency report" is a methodological artifact that collates and anonymizes all feedback received from Community Advisory Board members during [[co-design-in-research]], documents what action(s) were or were not taken in response, and provides justifications for decisions [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- Sharing the transparency report with CAB members after ethics committee approval—with an opportunity for final feedback—reinforces the democratic nature of participatory research and demonstrates responsiveness to community input [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- Transparency reports serve a reflexive function, prompting researchers and community partners to evaluate the participatory process itself and identify opportunities for improvement in future collaborations [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
