# Design from the Sensorial Margins (DFSM)

## Summary

Design from the Sensorial Margins (DFSM) is a design principle adapted from Rigot's (2022) "Design from the Margins" approach, applied to sensory needs in the [[sensory-responsive-environments-framework]]. DFSM involves centering neurodivergent user groups experiencing sensory extremes — both [[hypersensitivity|hypersensitive]] and [[hyposensitivity|hyposensitive]] individuals — rather than designing exclusively for the neurotypical "average" or majority.

## Core Concept

DFSM recognizes that designing for sensory extremes (the "margins") creates inclusive spaces that benefit not only those at the extremes but also individuals at various points along the sensory spectrum. By focusing design attention on the sensory edges where extreme discomfort meets manageable difficulty, designers can develop solutions that accommodate a wider range of users.

## Application in Practice

Instead of a prescriptive one-size-fits-all approach, DFSM involves:

1. **Understanding the sensory extremes** — Identifying users with the most intense hyper- or hypo-sensitivities in the intended use context
2. **Designing from that edge** — Creating interventions specifically addressing those extreme sensory needs
3. **Benefiting the broader spectrum** — Solutions designed for sensory extremes accommodate users at various points along [[the-sensorial-prism]]
4. **Iterative refinement** — Testing and refining with the communities experiencing sensory extremes

## Intersectionality

DFSM operates hand-in-hand with [[sensory-zoning]] to provide multiple sensory experiences, ensuring that marginalized sensory needs are not an afterthought but a design priority.
