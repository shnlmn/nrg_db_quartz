# Sensorimotor Movement

## Summary

Sensorimotor movement refers to the integration of sensory input with motor (movement) output, particularly engaging vestibular and proprioceptive sensory systems. For neurodivergent individuals, opportunities for diverse sensorimotor engagement in outdoor environments support regulation, body awareness, and meaningful participation in public spaces.

## Sensory Systems Involved

- **Vestibular** — Balance, movement, spatial orientation
- **Proprioceptive** — Body position awareness, muscle and joint feedback
- **Motor integration** — Coordinated movement, strength, control

## Manifestations in Outdoor Design

Sensorimotor affordances in outdoor built environments include:

- **Playground equipment** — Swings (vestibular), climbing structures (proprioceptive), slides (integrated movement)
- **Varied terrain** — Hills, uneven surfaces, and challenges requiring active balance and coordination
- **Movement spaces** — Open areas for running, walking, dancing, or free movement
- **Water features** — Wading, splashing, or crossing (vestibular and tactile)
- **Multi-generational equipment** — Accessible movement opportunities for various ages and abilities
- **Seating with movement options** — Benches allowing rocking, swaying, or fidgeting

## Neurodivergent Relevance

Study participants emphasized the importance of diversified sensorimotor opportunities, particularly for individuals with:

- [[hyposensitivity]] (sensory-seeking movement)
- Proprioceptive challenges (body awareness difficulties)
- Vestibular processing differences
- Need for self-regulation through movement
