# IDEAS (Interface Design Experience for the Autistic Spectrum)

## Summary

Co-design methodology developed for participatory design with autistic and neurodivergent children. IDEAS uses **visual supports and templates** tailored to neurodivergent communication and information-processing styles, enabling equitable participation in design workshops and technology development.

## Core Features

**Visual scaffolding**:
- Pre-designed templates guiding ideation and design contribution
- Visual communication supports reducing cognitive load
- Structured, predictable workshop format
- Clear, concrete design prompts rather than open-ended discussion

**Participatory approach**:
- Children as co-designers, not merely subjects
- Iterative feedback and refinement cycles
- Respect for diverse communication styles
- Design outputs reflecting children's direct input

## Applications

Originally developed for technology/interface design with autistic spectrum children (Benton et al., 2012). [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. 2025]] applied IDEAS methodology in playground co-design workshops, combining with [[cooperative-inquiry|Cooperative Inquiry]] for flexibility while maintaining visual structure.

## Integration with Co-Design Practice

In [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]]'s workshops:
- IDEAS templates supported initial ideation and play feature brainstorming
- Combined with sensory provocations (tactile bunny) and creativity tools
- Enabled both individual and group design contribution
- Reduced anxiety through clear expectations and structured participation
