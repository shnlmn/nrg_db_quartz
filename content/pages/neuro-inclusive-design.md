# Neuro-Inclusive Design

## Summary

Neuro-inclusive design is an approach to architecture, landscape, and urban design that intentionally accommodates diverse neurological profiles, including autism, ADHD, dyslexia, and other forms of neurodivergence. Neuro-inclusive design moves beyond accessibility compliance to actively create spaces that recognize and support the inherent diversity of human neurology and sensory processing.

## Core Principles

Neuro-inclusive design:

1. **Recognizes [[neurodiversity-paradigm|neurodiversity]]** — Views neurological differences as natural human variation, not pathology
2. **Addresses sensory needs** — Accommodates [[hypersensitivity|hypersensitivity]] and [[hyposensitivity|hyposensitivity]] across all sensory systems
3. **Centers marginalized voices** — Includes neurodivergent people in design processes as experts in their own experiences
4. **Provides choice and control** — Allows users to select sensory experiences matching their needs
5. **Supports accessibility** — Goes beyond ADA compliance to address invisible disabilities and cognitive/sensory needs

## Challenges and Gaps

Current landscape architecture and urban design practices:

- Often overlook neurodivergent sensory needs
- Focus primarily on autistic children rather than adults or other neurotypes
- Concentrate on indoor rather than outdoor environments
- Lack comprehensive multi-neurotype frameworks
- Neglect sensory processing differences in design guidelines
