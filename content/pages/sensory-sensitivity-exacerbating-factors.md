# Sensory Sensitivity Exacerbating Factors

## Summary

Exacerbating factors are conditions or states that amplify or intensify subjective sensory sensitivities. Research identifies both situational (environmental, external) and internal (physiological, psychological, emotional) factors that increase sensitivity severity and reduce coping capacity.

## Situational (Environmental) Factors

- **Unpredictability and lack of control** — uncertainty about when or what sensory stimuli will occur increases distress and reduces adaptive coping capacity
- **Cumulative sensory load** — multiple simultaneous sensory inputs are more overwhelming than single modalities in isolation
- **Intensity and duration** — stronger or longer-lasting stimuli exceed tolerance thresholds more readily
- **Social setting and attention to sensation** — awareness that others are present or observing amplifies discomfort and emotional response

## Internal Factors

- **Emotional state** — anxiety and distress amplify sensory sensitivity; mood dysregulation magnifies perceived intensity of stimuli
- **Tiredness and fatigue** — fatigue both exacerbates sensory sensitivities and is a consequence of managing sensory overload, creating bidirectional feedback
- **Physical health** — illness and pain increase sensory sensitivity
- **Psychiatric symptoms** — depression, anxiety, and symptom severity interact with sensory threshold and response

> **Note:** Several factors showed bidirectional causal relationships, meaning they both exacerbate sensitivities AND result from sensory overload—e.g., exhaustion triggers heightened sensitivities, which cause further exhaustion.
