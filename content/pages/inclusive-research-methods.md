# Inclusive Research Methods

## Summary

Inclusive research methods are approaches to design research, user testing, and academic study that account for the cognitive, sensory, and communicative diversity of neurodivergent participants. Traditional methods (surveys, focus groups, structured interviews) often inadvertently exclude neurodivergent people by relying on strong recall, unambiguous social cues, and narrow response formats.

## Key claims

- Surveys rely on strong recall skills, which may be challenging for those with ADHD; focus groups can misinterpret behavioral cues like eye contact aversion associated with autism [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- About 10–20% of the global population is estimated to be neurodivergent (Deloitte), meaning non-inclusive methods exclude a significant portion of respondents [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Real-time, conversational feedback tools (e.g. Slack) can mitigate [[recall-bias]] by capturing pain points as they happen [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Open-ended formats allow neurodivergent participants to communicate in ways that suit them—screenshots, long-form text, bullet points—rather than being constrained by pre-generated scale items [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Private channels reduce social-desirability bias and sentiment analysis bias by creating safe spaces for honest feedback [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Emoji reactions can serve as a lightweight pulse check akin to a net promoter score, reducing reliance on verbal sentiment expression [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Likert scales should be labelled with clear descriptors for each number, not just endpoints, and should include an open-box option for further comments [[raw/3 tips for designing user research for neurodivergent testers|AbilityNet]]
- The [[double-empathy-problem]] highlights miscommunications between neurodivergent and non-neurodivergent individuals; questions must use clear, specific language to avoid ambiguity [[raw/3 tips for designing user research for neurodivergent testers|AbilityNet]]
- [[alexithymia]], prevalent among neurodivergent individuals, makes it challenging to articulate emotions through language; alternative mediums (art, descriptions of physical sensations) should be offered [[raw/3 tips for designing user research for neurodivergent testers|AbilityNet]]
- Qualitative methods benefit neurodiversity research by enabling open responses less restricted than pre-generated quantitative scales, which can reflect researchers' preconceived notions [[raw/Not your "typical" research_ Inclusion ethics in neurodiversity scholarship _ Industrial and Organizational Psychology|Bernard et al.]] (cited via [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]])
- However, qualitative-only approaches (interviews, focus groups, recordings) make cross-site comparison difficult; standardised quantitative multi-sensory tools like the [[sensory-walk-questionnaire]] can complement qualitative methods by enabling structured comparison across environments [[raw/Sensory characteristics of a place_ The development of the sensory walk questionnaire|Pohjanheimo et al.]]
- Start every research engagement by asking: "How do you prefer to be communicated with?" [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Include neurodivergent researchers in the planning process — "Nothing about us, without us" [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Inaccurate or ill-considered design research can create "invisible users" — you cannot solve problems that go unidentified [[raw/How to Make Design Research Neurodivergent Friendly|Salesforce]]
- Using [[community-advisory-board]]s in a [[co-design-in-research]] approach improves accessibility by leveraging diverse neurodivergent perspectives to identify blind spots; for example, dyslexic CAB members identified formatting issues that the research team had not noticed [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- [[reasonable-adjustments]] for [[focus-groups]] with neurodivergent participants include: multiple modes of participation (in-person and online); ground rules in written and verbal formats; permitted breaks; questions presented in both verbal and written forms (e.g., chat for online); and permission to use assistive software [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
