# Thermal Navigation Cues

## Summary

Temperature, airflow, and sunlight placement that provide orientation cues for people with visual impairment. Thermal cues help travelers determine location, direction of travel, and changes in environment (e.g., moving from indoors to outdoors).

## Key cues

### Temperature Transitions
- Cold air in warm building plus traffic sounds indicating open door to outside
- Temperature changes marking boundaries between spaces or indicating proximity to specific locations [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

### Airflow Direction
- Direction and placement of air movement providing information about building layout and openings
- Consistent airflow patterns helping determine location within space [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

### Solar Placement
- Position of sun relative to individual providing directional information for outdoor navigation
- Warmth from sun providing consistent directional reference
- Useful for longer-duration wayfinding and spatial orientation [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

### Steam and Thermal Emissions
- Steam from sidewalk grates (often combined with odor cues)
- Hot air from building vents
- Creates recognizable location markers [[Experience of Multisensory Environments in Public Space among People with Visual Impairment]]

## Multi-sensory integration

Thermal cues are most effective when:
- Combined with other sensory information (olfactory cues often accompany thermal ones)
- Providing consistent, recognizable sensations
- Supporting broader wayfinding strategy alongside auditory and tactile information [[context-dependent-sensory-effectiveness]]
- Used indoors to indicate transitions or boundaries

## Design considerations for built environments

Indoor spaces:
- Conscious control of air quality and temperature can assist with orientation and travel direction
- Strategic placement of air vents and HVAC returns at identifiable locations
- Consideration of thermal comfort alongside functional navigation value

Outdoor spaces:
- Preservation of natural thermal features (sun exposure, wind patterns)
- Minimal interference with natural thermal cues
- Integration with other landmark cues
