# Community Advisory Board

## Summary

A Community Advisory Board (CAB) is a group composed of members who share a common experience, interest, or identity (in this context, neurodivergence) who provide feedback and guidance throughout the research process. CABs offer a structured mechanism for community members to represent their perspectives in research design, data interpretation, and dissemination without necessarily conducting the research themselves.

## Key claims

- CABs are a form of participatory research involvement that shares similar benefits and limitations with other participatory models such as community-based participatory research (CBPR) [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- Community members in a CAB can help ensure data is interpreted correctly and that results are representative of the experience under study, making research more translational and relevant to their lives [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- CABs improve the effectiveness of research and opportunities to translate findings into action, but are labor-intensive, time-consuming, and may not be fully representative of the wider community [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- In a focus group study with a 7-member neurodivergent CAB (representing ADHD, ASD, dyslexia, and dyspraxia), members identified blind spots in research design not noticed by the research team; for example, dyslexic members caught formatting issues that interfered with accessibility [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- The diversity of perspectives within a CAB is critical—members with different neurodivergent profiles catch issues that affect their specific profiles; however, the limitation of a lack of ethnic diversity and representation of some neurodivergent conditions (e.g., dyscalculia, Tourette's syndrome) should be addressed in ongoing recruitment [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- CAB members reported benefits including a personal feeling of better representation and inclusion (mentioned by 4 of 7 members), greater understanding of the research process (3 members), improved understanding of their own neurodiversity (1 member), and increased awareness of neurodiversity impacts on others (2 members) [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- CAB members faced challenges including difficulty articulating certain aspects of their own neurodiversity, distinguishing their neurodivergent traits from others', lack of clarity about roles and timelines, difficulty keeping up with email communication, and time constraints in reading and understanding materials [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- While anonymity between CAB members was initially maintained to foster psychological safety, members later suggested that allowing voluntary group sessions for those comfortable breaking anonymity could increase belonging and shared ownership [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
