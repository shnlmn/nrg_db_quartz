# TEACCH Method

## Summary

**Treatment and Education of Autistic and Related Communication-Handicapped Children** (TEACCH) is a structured teaching approach originating from autism education practice. Emphasizes **visual cues, predictable schedules, and structured routines** to support clear expectations and reduce anxiety in learning and participatory environments.

## Core Components

**Visual supports**:
- Clear visual schedules showing sequence of activities
- Pictorial or written cues indicating expectations
- Organized physical environment with distinct zones and clear transitions
- Visual timers and activity completion indicators

**Structure and predictability**:
- Consistent routines reducing uncertainty
- Clear beginning, middle, and end points for activities
- Transition warnings before activity changes
- Explicit communication of expectations

**Scaffolding for understanding**:
- Reducing cognitive ambiguity through visual representation
- Supporting executive functioning and task initiation
- Enabling independent work and participation when expectations are clear
- Decreasing reliance on constant adult directions

## Application in Co-Design Research

From [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. 2025]]:

- **Workshop structure clarity** — Visual schedules and cues made workshop expectations transparent to neurodivergent children
- **Reduced anxiety through predictability** — Knowing what would happen, in what order, reduced social and procedural anxiety enabling focus on design tasks
- **Independence support** — Clear expectations enabled children to participate with minimal adult prompting
- **Anxiety-safe participation** — Combined with [[cooperative-inquiry|Cooperative Inquiry]] flexibility, TEACCH structure balanced security with responsiveness

## Complementary to Responsive Approaches

TEACCH structure is not rigid or controlling but rather **supportive scaffolding** enabling participation by reducing uncertainty. When combined with [[cooperative-inquiry|Cooperative Inquiry]] flexibility, provides both safety and responsiveness essential for equitable co-design.
