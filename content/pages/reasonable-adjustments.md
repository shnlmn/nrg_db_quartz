# Reasonable Adjustments

## Summary

Reasonable adjustments are practical accommodations and modifications made to research methods, materials, and settings to ensure neurodivergent participants can engage equitably. They aim to remove or reduce disadvantages related to a participant's neurodivergence, provide opportunities for full and equitable participation regardless of personal circumstances or health status, and account for logistical challenges.

## Key claims

- Reasonable adjustments are grounded in guidelines for conducting inclusive and accessible focus groups and should be compiled in a central, accessible location (such as a project website) to support potential participants' informed decision-making about participation [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- For [[focus-groups]] with neurodivergent participants, reasonable adjustments include: 1) offering multiple modes of participation (in-person and online videoconference); 2) providing ground rules both verbally and in writing; 3) allowing breaks from conversation as needed; 4) stating questions verbally and in written format (e.g., pasting in chat for online groups); 5) permitting use of assistive software during online participation [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
- When reasonable adjustments are already implicitly or explicitly included in a protocol or information sheet, collating them in one central place for participants' convenience does not require additional ethical approval [[raw/Co-Design for Participatory Neurodiversity Research_ Collaborating With a Community Advisory Board to Design a Research Study _ Published in Journal of Participatory Resea.md|Le Cunff et al.]]
