# Zones of Regulation

## Summary

Self-regulation framework developed by Kuypers (2013) based on **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)** principles. Uses **color-coded "zones" (traffic light system)** to help children identify their emotional state, understand triggers, and develop strategies for emotional regulation and adaptive response.

## The Four Zones

**Blue Zone** — Low arousal, disengagement
- Feelings: sad, tired, bored, sick, calm
- Behaviors: withdrawn, slow moving, lack of focus
- Goal: Move toward green zone through alerting strategies

**Green Zone** — Optimal arousal, regulated state
- Feelings: happy, calm, focused, ready to learn
- Behaviors: appropriate social engagement, task-focused
- Goal: Maintain green zone functioning

**Yellow Zone** — Elevated arousal, heightened alert
- Feelings: anxious, excited, frustrated, worried, embarrassed
- Behaviors: wiggly, talkative, rushing, difficulty focusing
- Goal: Use calming strategies to transition to green

**Red Zone** — High arousal, dysregulation
- Feelings: angry, terrified, explosive, out of control
- Behaviors: aggressive, dangerous, extreme emotional expression
- Goal: Safety and de-escalation toward yellow/green zones

## Application in Co-Design Research

From [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al. 2025]]:

- **Emotion identification support** — Traffic light color system enabled children to communicate emotional state clearly
- **Workshop emotional management** — Children could self-identify when moving through zones, supporting self-awareness
- **Transitions and coping** — Understanding zones helped children develop and use regulation strategies during demanding group activities
- **Safe participation** — Explicit emotion-state framework reduced shame/judgment, supporting psychological safety in research

## Integration with Other Supports

In [[kelly-et-al-2025-inclusive-playgrounds|Kelly et al.]] workshops, Zones of Regulation combined with:
- [[TEACCH]] visual structure (clarity about transitions)
- [[cooperative-inquiry|Cooperative Inquiry]] flexibility (responsive support)
- [[IDEAS]] templates (reducing cognitive load)
- Sensory supports (fidgets, emotional support items like bubble wrap)

## Relevance to Neurodivergent Self-Regulation

Zones framework particularly valuable for:
- **Alexithymia support** — Visual system helping children identify emotions when word-finding is difficult
- **Interoception development** — Explicit attention to internal states supporting body awareness
- **Executive functioning** — Recognizing need to use strategies when dysregulated
- **Self-advocacy** — Children learning to communicate regulation needs to adults
