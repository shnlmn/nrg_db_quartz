# Medical Model of Disability

## Summary

The medical model views neurological and cognitive differences as abnormalities that need to be prevented, fixed, or cured. While it enables treatment of genuine medical issues (e.g. epilepsy, gut issues co-occurring with neurodevelopmental conditions), widespread use can lead to viewing disability as shameful and to intervening on targets deemed unacceptable by society but not actually harmful.

## Key claims

- The model can lead to intervening on non-harmful behaviors (e.g. non-injurious self-stimulation like hand-flapping) simply because society deems them unacceptable [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- Historical parallel: homosexuality was classified as a mental disorder until 1973 under a medical-model framework [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
- The [[neurodiversity-attitudes-questionnaire]] includes a "Medical Model" factor measuring alignment with this perspective [[raw/The Neurodiversity Attitudes Questionnaire_ Development and Initial Validation|NDAQ paper]]
